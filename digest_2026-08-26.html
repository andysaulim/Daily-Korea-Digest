Trilateral

PACOM confirms 2026 Freedom Edge trilateral exercise with ROK and Japan is in preparation

US Pacific Command spokesperson Lt. Cmdr. Chloe Morgan told Yonhap that PACOM is preparing for the 2026 iteration of Freedom Edge alongside the Japan Self-Defense Forces and the Republic of Korea Armed Forces, describing it as a 'premier multi-domain exercise' to reinforce shared values and interoperability. The confirmation comes after Trump's directive scaled back Ulchi Freedom Shield, raising questions about whether trilateral exercises would similarly be curtailed. Freedom Edge is a separate exercise from the bilateral US-ROK combined exercise program and involves Japan as a co-equal partner.

So what — The exercise timeline intersects with the September 9 DPRK founding day window — a historically active period for North Korean provocations — and with ongoing US-DPRK diplomatic signaling.

Pattern: The CSIS NK provocations database records a pattern of DPRK missile tests clustered around US-ROK-Japan exercise periods; the database shows 15 provocations in 2026 to date.

Korea Times / PACOM spokesperson Lt. Cmdr. Chloe Morgan via Yonhap
US-Korea

Russian military aircraft enter KADIZ near Dokdo and Ulleung Island; ROK scrambles fighters

Several Russian military aircraft entered Korea's Air Defense Identification Zone near Dokdo and Ulleung Island on Tuesday afternoon before retreating, prompting the ROK Joint Chiefs of Staff to dispatch Air Force fighter jets. The JCS stated the aircraft did not violate Korea's territorial airspace. The incursion comes as Russia-Korea bilateral diplomatic relations remain strained over Seoul's sanctions posture on Russia.

So what — The incident will be on the agenda of ROK-Russia diplomatic channels and may affect Seoul's deliberations on attending the international economic forum in Russia, which the foreign ministry said Wednesday it has not yet decided on.

Korea Times / ROK Joint Chiefs of Staff statement
Business

Seven transport ministry officials referred to prosecution for alleged cover-up of faulty localizer in 2024 Jeju Air crash

A special police investigation team referred seven Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport officials to prosecutors Tuesday for allegedly drafting press releases claiming a concrete-mounted localizer navigation aid at Muan International Airport met safety regulations, despite knowing it did not. The localizer was struck by a Jeju Air aircraft making an emergency belly landing on December 29, 2024, killing 179 people — the deadliest aviation disaster in South Korean history. Police allege the officials drafted false statements about the localizer's placement after the crash.

So what — The referral advances the criminal accountability track of the Jeju Air investigation and keeps pressure on the transport ministry ahead of any legislative response to aviation safety regulations.

Korea Times / special police investigation team
