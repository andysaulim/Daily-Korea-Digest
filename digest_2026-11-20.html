Yonhap, ROK Politics

Democratic Party formally adopts impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae as party line

The ruling Democratic Party voted at a general caucus meeting on Nov. 20 to pursue the impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, one day after the Supreme Court overturned former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo's 15-year insurrection sentence and remanded the case to the Seoul High Court.

The party cited Cho's handling of the martial law declaration, his earlier decision to send President Lee Jae-myung's election law case back to appeals court, and allegations of improper use of official funds. The DP holds 161 of 300 Assembly seats, enough to pass an impeachment motion without coalition partners, and plans to introduce the motion before year-end.

NK News, DPRK

Trump leaves Asia without meeting Kim Jong Un, says encounter will happen 'at some point'

President Donald Trump flew back to Washington on Nov. 20 after the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen without meeting North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, despite publicly raising the prospect during his trip.

Trump told NBC News: "The relationship is there, but we didn't meet. There were too many meetings. We'll have that meeting at some point." Secretary of State Marco Rubio had said days earlier that Trump was "serious" about engaging with Kim, and the Kremlin had indicated Putin would meet Kim if he attended APEC, Kim did not appear.

AP / Daily NK, DPRK

IAEA says North Korea has completed external works on Yongbyon enrichment facility extension

The International Atomic Energy Agency reported to its Board of Governors that North Korea has completed the external construction on an extension to its original uranium enrichment facility at Yongbyon, voicing "serious concern" over the upgrades.

The IAEA report, released Nov. 19, is the latest in a series of assessments documenting the facility's expansion; the BP Locations tracker recorded the enrichment building expansion as complete with a new centrifuge hall operational as of April 2026. Daily NK separately reported that satellite imagery of the Pyongsan uranium refinery shows a new chemical storage tank added alongside a freight train arrival and apparent wastewater discharge toward the Ryesong River.

The Value of U.S.-Japan-ROK Trilateralism: A View from the Three Capitals. Based on interviews in Washington, Tokyo, and Seoul, the RAND study assesses trilateral cooperation under two crisis scenarios: a North Korean provocation and a Chinese invasion of Taiwan. It finds information-sharing, cyberdefense, and logistics coordination are functional but that legal and political barriers impede wartime integration. The Camp David Summit of 2023 is identified as the paradigm shift the framework now needs to institutionalize. (RAND)
Toplines: Why and how the United States and its allies should prepare for a limited nuclear attack. The Guardian Tiger III study, based on engagements with over 150 US and allied government, military, and expert stakeholders, identifies cognitive and organizational biases as barriers to nuclear scenario planning. It notes North Korea could plausibly develop additional warhead types within five to ten years, expanding limited nuclear attack options. The report calls for specific allied preparation measures beyond the existing extended deterrence framework. (Atlantic Council)
