01Top stories
NK News, DPRK

North Korea expels Swiss diplomat, orders Pyongyang mission closed within 48 hours

The DPRK Foreign Ministry's European Department director general Pak Hyon Su declared the head of Switzerland's Pyongyang diplomatic office, Sylvain Astier, persona non grata and ordered him to leave within 48 hours, citing Bern's sanctioning of the Songdowon International Children's Camp over its alleged role in transferring children from Russian-occupied Ukraine.

Pak also submitted a memorandum demanding the closure of the office, which had opened only in October 2026. NK News analysis notes North Korea selectively targeted Switzerland and the United Kingdom while leaving EU member states that also sanctioned the camp unscathed.

Yonhap, US-Korea

Lee reaffirms Cheonghae unit expansion to Arabian Sea, bars Strait of Hormuz operations

Lee Jae-myung reaffirmed that South Korea's Cheonghae naval unit will expand its operational zone from the Gulf of Aden to the open sea south of Oman, approximately 650 kilometers from the Strait of Hormuz, after the National Assembly defense committee approved a revised dispatch motion.

The unit will add maritime patrol aircraft and mine-clearing support to its existing destroyer role; the Strait of Hormuz and adjacent waters are explicitly excluded from the area of operations. Minor opposition parties have criticized the expansion as a backdoor troop dispatch to the ongoing US-Iran conflict.

Yonhap, ROK Politics

KOSPI drops 6.16% as AI trade reverses; Democratic Party formally proposes five-party coalition to impeach Chief Justice

South Korean shares fell 308.52 points, or 6.16 percent, to close at 4,697.51 as an overnight Wall Street tech selloff erased the previous session's Jensen Huang-driven rally; Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix each fell roughly 10 percent.

Separately, Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok formally proposed a five-party coalition with progressive parties to accelerate the impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, with the Changwon District Court issuing the first formal judicial statement of opposition, warning the push undermines constitutional separation of powers.

Commentary
South Korea Cannot Police Its Way Out of AI Cheating. The piece documents the top scorer on the tax exam confessing to AI cheating and 111 alleged AI-assisted cheating cases at this year's college entrance exam. It argues that South Korea's heavy reliance on standardized exams as gatekeepers for social mobility makes the country uniquely vulnerable to AI-enabled fraud. Policing measures treat the symptom while leaving the structural incentive for cheating, the exam system itself, intact. (Diplomat)
Japan, South Korea, US Are Taking Their Trilateral to the Arctic. The piece examines how the Camp David trilateral framework, originally focused on North Korea deterrence and China competition, is now extending into Arctic governance, shipping routes, and resource access. It assesses the rationale for bringing the Arctic into a peninsula-centered partnership as Russia's Arctic posture intensifies. The article raises the question of whether Arctic cooperation can generate durable momentum for the trilateral given historical frictions between Seoul and Tokyo. (Diplomat)
Making the Most of South Korea's Semiconductor Boom. The piece argues that Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix have made South Korea an outsized beneficiary of the AI hardware boom but that concentration in one sector amplifies downside when sentiment shifts. It draws the connection between Korea's export dependency on advanced memory chips and the disproportionate KOSPI selloffs whenever Wall Street AI sentiment reverses. The author calls for industrial policy that diversifies the value chain rather than deepening semiconductor concentration. (Diplomat)
