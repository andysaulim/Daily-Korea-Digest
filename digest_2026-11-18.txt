This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Wednesday, November 18.

Three things today: Lee Jae-myung meets Xi Jinping in Shenzhen and clinches a trade deal; Marco Rubio says Trump is serious about meeting Kim Jong Un; and South Korea and the U.S. hold a senior military meeting on AI-driven all-domain operations.

The Lee-Xi summit is the first test of whether Seoul can lock in economic gains with Beijing while keeping Washington on side. Tomorrow, the Supreme Court delivers its verdict on former Prime Minister Han Deok-su and former first lady Kim Geun-hee, live on television. And Korea's national college entrance exam, the Suneung, takes place Thursday under emergency AI anti-cheating protocols.

First, the Lee-Xi summit in Shenzhen.

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung and Chinese President Xi Jinping met for about 35 minutes on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen today. The two leaders declared the second phase of Korea-China Free Trade Agreement negotiations on services and investment substantially concluded.

Xi called for stronger macroeconomic policy coordination and cooperation on emerging technology industries, including artificial intelligence. National Security Adviser Wi Sung-lac said Lee asked China to play a constructive role on issues concerning the peninsula. Xi, in turn, voiced support for U.S.-North Korea dialogue and for Seoul's efforts to ease tensions.

After the meeting, President Lee posted on X that the agreement, in his words, "will go beyond the full and substantial restoration of Korea-China relations, becoming the foundation for creating a new future for Korea-China relations."

The Korea Economic Daily reports both leaders emphasized what they called "strategic independence" and consolidating their "strategic cooperative partnership."

The day before, Lee met Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi for 20 minutes on the ASEAN summit sidelines in Manila. They discussed responses to North Korea and reaffirmed each country's alliance with the U.S. and their commitment to trilateral cooperation.

The policy question hanging over both meetings: whether Seoul can deepen ties with Beijing on trade and AI while holding the line with Tokyo and Washington on security. The FTA phase 2 conclusion is the headline from Shenzhen, but watch for how the alliance managers in Seoul and Washington read the fine print.

Second, Rubio on Trump and Kim.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio, speaking in Manila, said President Trump is, in his words, "serious" about meeting Kim Jong Un, because "the only way we're ever going to solve it is if we engage with them." Rubio explicitly declined to confirm any scheduled meeting.

The Kremlin added its own signal, saying President Putin would "of course" meet Kim if the North Korean leader attends the APEC summit in Shenzhen.

Analysts are watching two data points that have fueled speculation about an imminent Kim visit to China. First, Kim's inspection of the Samjiyon tourism zone near the Chinese border on November 15 and 16. Second, reports that Chinese social media platforms have been scrubbing North Korea-related content, a pattern sometimes seen ahead of a high-profile visit.

Meanwhile, North Korea's Workers' Party Secretary Jo Yong-won met Russian President Putin in Moscow in mid-November, backing Russia's fight in Ukraine. Daily N.K. reports that Pyongyang issued a gag order suppressing public discussion of the visit after it sparked speculation among residents. And KCNA reported this week that Russia will erect a monument honoring North Korean soldiers' contribution in the Kursk operation before year-end. That is the first time state media has publicly acknowledged the deployment of North Korean troops to the conflict.

The convergence is striking: a possible Kim trip to China, a Rubio signal on Trump-Kim engagement, and Pyongyang's first domestic admission of its soldiers fighting in Ukraine, all in the same week.

Third, South Korea-U.S. combined forces meeting on AI and all-domain operations.

South Korea's Joint Chiefs Chairman General Jin Yong-sung and U.S. Forces Korea Commander General Xavier Brunson co-led a Combined Forces Command strategy meeting at Camp Humphreys today, focused on combined joint all-domain operations, the concept of synchronizing land, air, sea, space, and cyber capabilities into a networked force.

General Jin called for AI-powered command-and-control systems to ensure faster decision-making than adversaries through real-time, cross-domain intelligence sharing. General Brunson, posting on X from a summit on combined joint all-domain operations the same day, said: "Speed is the thing that's going to make the difference on this peninsula. We've got to continue to press to ensure that we use attribute-based access control so that the data gets where it needs to be when it needs to be there."

The U.S. Ambassador to South Korea also marked the day at the War Memorial of Korea, joining Korean partners and Lockheed Martin to commemorate the alliance's shared sacrifices as the U.S. marks 250 years of independence.

The meeting was the latest in a regular series of high-level strategy sessions between the two allies. The focus on AI-driven speed and cross-domain data sharing reflects where both militaries say the competitive edge will be decided on the Korean Peninsula.

Now to Pyongyang.

State media led today with Kim Jong Un's inspection of the Samjiyon tourism zone in Ryanggang Province on November 15 and 16. Kim reviewed resort construction, Samjiyon Airport upgrades, and new railway infrastructure, and climbed Mount Paektu, calling tourism a "major stimulant to regional development." That framing keeps the focus domestic and developmental, even as his proximity to the Chinese border draws outside attention.

The bigger signal from KCNA this week is the report that Russia will erect a monument honoring North Korean troops' contribution in the Kursk operation before year-end. That is the first domestic acknowledgment in state media of North Korea's military participation in the Ukraine conflict. Workers' Party Secretary Jo Yong-won's Moscow visit, backing Russia's fight against what KCNA called U.S. "neo-Nazism," ran alongside a gag order at home suppressing public discussion of the trip.

The rest of the news, quickly.

On the courts, tomorrow is a consequential day in Seoul. The Supreme Court delivers its verdict on former Prime Minister Han Deok-su and former first lady Kim Geun-hee, broadcast live nationwide. Neither party will appear in court.

Separately, former National Assembly member Choo Kyung-ho was acquitted in the first trial over alleged martial law lift obstruction. The court ruled he was "not a member of an insurrection group." The ruling party welcomed the verdict.

In the National Assembly, the ruling Democratic Party passed the Unification Ministry's 2027 budget on a party-line vote after People Power Party members walked out. The budget includes an inter-Korean economic cooperation loan line increased by 237 percent and a new International Unification Foundation project at 3.306 billion won. The People Power Party called it a "North Korea submission budget."

Justice Minister nominee Lee Yong-chul has a confirmation hearing scheduled for November 24, the same week that a special prosecution bill targeting fabricated indictments cleared a cabinet meeting and awaits presidential signature.

On North Korea's economy, trade with China fell to 254 million dollars in October, down from 299 million dollars in September, despite the opening of the New Yalu River Bridge. That made October only the fourth-highest monthly trade figure this year. Daily N.K. reports that markets in Sinuiju are stacked with Chinese goods through the new bridge, but a severe cash shortage has effectively halted purchases. Traders are calling it a "trading cliff."

South Korea's household credit rose 9.8 trillion won in the third quarter, with card spending accounting for more than half the increase, raising Bank of Korea monitoring attention ahead of its next rate decision. The Bank of Korea's current rate stands at 3.00 percent.

South Korea joined the NATO-led Starlift initiative, a space launch cooperation framework among 11 countries, with the South Korean military contributing funding to help book commercial launches for military satellites. The memorandum of understanding was signed at NATO headquarters on October 29.

Hanwha Ocean launched South Korea's new 3,600-ton frigate Pyeongtaek, incorporating advanced defense systems and domestic weapons technology, as part of the navy's broader modernization drive.

South Korea sent its first fact-finding mission to the site of the 1987 Korean Air bombing crash in waters off Myanmar, 39 years after the November 29 attack. A Foreign Ministry and Korea Coast Guard team is conducting the inspection.

In polling, Gallup Korea's survey taken November 3 to 5 showed President Lee's approval at 41 percent, up two points. The Democratic Party stood at 36 percent, the People Power Party at 28 percent, level with unaffiliated voters also at 28 percent.

The Quincy Institute for Responsible Statecraft released a new volume arguing Washington should abandon complete denuclearization as a near-term goal and shift toward coexistence and risk reduction, drawing contributors from Carnegie, Stimson, the Korea Institute for National Unification, the Korea Institute for Defense Analyses, and universities in Pittsburgh, Nanjing, Seoul, and Wellington.

On Capitol Hill.

Congressman-elect Dan Koh posted a message to the people of North Korea saying, in his words, "I am thinking of you," and pledged to maintain the U.S.-Korea alliance ahead of the start of his House term. It signals an active Korea engagement posture from at least one incoming member.

Deputy Foreign Minister Kim Jin-a delivered the keynote at the 10th Korea-U.S. Joint Public-Private Economic Forum in Washington, co-hosted with the Hudson Institute, emphasizing shipbuilding and energy as priority sectors. She met Senator Bill Hagerty at the forum's closing session, requesting congressional support for smooth U.S. residency for Korean workers and bilateral shipbuilding cooperation.

Beyond the region.

South Korea is building warships in a Latin American country under a 462 million dollar deal to modernize that country's shipbuilding industry, extending Seoul's defense export footprint into the region, according to El Cronista.

Czech nuclear regulators have begun their first on-site quality assurance inspection of the Dukovany nuclear power plant project awarded to South Korea's KEPCO consortium. It is the first formal regulatory review since the contract was signed.

Finally, the markets.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed at about 5,067, up about 2 percent on November 18. The won strengthened slightly, trading at about 1,333 to the dollar. Brent crude fell about 3 percent to just under 90 dollars a barrel.
