DPRK

Warship That Capsized Finally Joins North Korea's Fleet

Kim Jong Un presided over the commissioning of the destroyer Kang Kon at Wonsan on September 8, declaring it part of the nuclear response system and calling for the navy to be fully nuclear-armed. The vessel capsized during its 2025 launch ceremony — an embarrassment for the regime — and was refloated and repaired before this second commissioning. Kim's daughter Ju Ae appeared alongside him, the first confirmed public appearance of both at a military commissioning.

NK-Russia-China

Russia and North Korea open the first road across their shared border

North Korea and Russia formally opened their first cross-border road bridge over the Tumen River on September 8, at a ceremony attended by Russian Prime Minister Mikhail Mishustin. The bridge is named after Soviet officer Yakov Novichenko, who threw himself on a grenade to protect Kim Il Sung during World War II — underscoring the ideological framing of the bilateral relationship. The structure provides the first permanent road link supplementing the existing rail crossing at Tumangang–Khasan, with direct implications for logistics of arms transfers tracked by the CSIS NK-Russia timeline.

S. Korea faces high-stakes decision on potential Hormuz Strait deployment

South Korea dispatched an investigation team to the Strait of Hormuz to assess deployment options after US strikes targeted multiple Iranian tankers near Kharg Island. Seoul's talks with France concern contribution options — not troop deployment — and a senior DP lawmaker on the National Assembly Defense Committee urged caution, warning that deploying under the US-led framework could make South Korean vessels targets for Iranian attack. A US official stated the strait is 'completely open, controlled by the US,' but the ROK government has not yet committed to any force contribution.
