This is the Korea Week in Review from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It covers November 14 to 20, 2026.

The week in brief.

The week's defining outcome was the first U.S.-China-Russia trilateral summit in Shenzhen producing a joint declaration that made no mention of the Korean Peninsula, despite Seoul's lobbying and Trump's public overtures toward Kim Jong Un. Simultaneously, the International Atomic Energy Agency confirmed Yongbyon's enrichment annex is externally complete and the reactor is operating, raising the baseline cost of any future diplomatic engagement. Next week, watch the Han Duck-soo political fallout and the Democratic Party's threatened impeachment of the chief justice as the dominant domestic story, alongside any post-APEC signals from Washington or Pyongyang on the Kim meeting question.

The top 10 stories of the week.

First: Trump-Xi-Putin first trilateral summit issues joint declaration; Korean Peninsula absent. Trump, Xi, and Putin met at the Kylin Villa in Shenzhen on November 19 on the sidelines of APEC, the first-ever U.S.-China-Russia trilateral summit, and issued a four-point joint declaration that did not address the Korean Peninsula. Seoul had warned Beijing against a 'second Yalta' outcome and Korean analysts flagged the risk of Peninsula decisions being made without Seoul. Despite Trump having floated a Kim Jong Un meeting at APEC as recently as November 17, North Korea did not appear on the trilateral declaration.

Second: The International Atomic Energy Agency confirms Yongbyon enrichment annex externally complete, reactor operating. The International Atomic Energy Agency Director General Rafael Grossi told the Board of Governors in Vienna on November 19 that North Korea had completed external construction of a new annex to the original Yongbyon uranium enrichment facility, with the reactor operating. The confirmation followed earlier reporting that the enrichment building expansion was complete and a new centrifuge hall was operational as of April 2026. N.K. News separately reported this week that the November 4 cluster warhead test attributed to North Korea was fabricated using 2022 imagery.

Third: Jo Yong-won leads North Korean delegation to Moscow, meets Putin, pledges support against U.S. 'neo-Nazism'. Jo Yong-won, Presidium member of the Political Bureau and secretary of the Workers' Party Central Committee, arrived in Moscow by private plane on November 15 leading a party and government delegation, and met President Putin at the Kremlin on November 17. Jo pledged North Korean support against what he characterized as U.S. 'neo-Nazism.' The visit coincided with the Shenzhen APEC summit and KCNA imposed a near-total gag order on the visit, with four competing theories circulating in South Korean media.

Fourth: Lee-Xi Shenzhen summit clinches Korea-China FTA phase 2 after eight years, eight months. Lee Jae-myung and Xi Jinping met for approximately 35 minutes on the APEC sidelines in Shenzhen on November 18, declaring the second phase of the Korea-China Free Trade Agreement covering services and investment, concluding negotiations after eight years and eight months. The two sides also backed U.S.-North Korea dialogue. Korean entertainment, gaming, and travel stocks surged on the FTA news.

Fifth: Trump floats Kim meeting at APEC; Rubio says Trump 'serious'; no preparation signs detected. Trump told reporters at the White House before departing for Shenzhen on November 17 that he may meet Kim Jong Un at APEC, calling Kim's potential attendance 'up to him.' Secretary of State Rubio, speaking in Manila on November 18, confirmed Trump was 'serious' about meeting Kim, saying engagement was the only path to a solution, but explicitly declined to give details. Experts told N.K. News they saw no signs of preparation for such a meeting despite public overtures, and China's Foreign Ministry said it had 'no information' on a possible North Korea-U.S. summit in China.

Sixth: South Korea hits 1 trillion dollars in annual exports, fourth country globally. South Korea's annual exports crossed 1 trillion dollars at 2:09 p.m. on November 19, making it the fourth country globally after Germany, the United States, and China to reach that threshold, with AI-related goods cited as a driver. Bloomberg separately reported this week that Korea's semiconductor output growth could lift 2027 GDP growth to 4 percent. The KOSPI had fallen sharply earlier in the week on U.S. semiconductor export control announcements before recovering.

Seventh: Supreme Court partially overturns Han Duck-soo sentence; Democratic Party moves to impeach chief justice. The Supreme Court on November 19 partially overturned ex-Prime Minister Han Duck-soo's 15-year prison sentence for his role in former President Yoon Suk Yeol's December 2024 martial law bid, finding that Han's participation met a lower threshold of culpability. The Democratic Party responded by moving to impeach the Supreme Court chief justice. The ruling was delivered one day before Han's detention was set to expire and was broadcast live on television nationwide.

Eighth: CSAT exam proceeds under emergency AI anti-cheating protocols after national scandal. South Korea's College Scholastic Ability Test for the 2027 academic year was administered on November 19 for approximately 550,000 students under emergency protocols including mandatory glasses inspections, Wi-Fi blocks near test sites, smart-device surrender requirements, and 8,400 extra proctors. The measures followed a confession by the top scorer in the national tax accountant certification exam who admitted to using AI-assisted smart glasses to cheat. A Seoul National University student with a perfect LEET score was found dead amid the AI cheating probe.

Ninth: Wang Yi briefs Seoul on Kim's Dandong visit; Seoul asks Beijing to help return North Korea to talks. Wang Yi elaborated on Kim Jong Un's October 24 visit to Dandong during talks with Foreign Minister Cho Hyun in Beijing on November 13, with a South Korean official saying China described its North Korea relationship as economic rather than political. Foreign Minister Cho asked Wang Yi to help bring North Korea back to the negotiation table, stressing that peninsula peace serves common interests. Foreign Minister Cho also warned Beijing against a 'second Yalta' outcome at the approaching U.S.-China-Russia trilateral.

Tenth: Kim inspects east-coast naval bases and Kim Jong Suk Naval University; North Korean naval buildup continues. Kim Jong Un conducted his second consecutive inspection of an east-coast naval base under construction, as reported by KCNA on November 13, ordering that construction be completed within party-mandated timelines and to party-mandated quality standards. Kim subsequently visited Kim Jong Suk Naval University on the east coast on November 18 to 19, criticizing enemy naval activities and stating the university's primary duty is to train professional naval commanders. KCNA also reported this week that Russia will erect a monument honoring North Korean troops' contribution to the Kursk operation, marking the first domestic acknowledgment by KCNA of North Korean military participation in the Ukraine conflict.

Now to Pyongyang.

Kim Jong Un made a public appearance on 4 of 6 days, most recently on Thursday November 19.

Kim appeared publicly on four days this week, focusing on east-coast naval construction and the Kim Jong Suk Naval University, maintaining a posture of military modernization while avoiding any public statement on Trump's APEC overtures. KCNA imposed a near-total gag order on Jo Yong-won's Moscow visit even as it reported it. The week's most significant KCNA departure from prior posture was the first domestic acknowledgment that Russia would erect a monument honoring North Korean troops for their role in the Kursk operation, the first KCNA acknowledgment of North Korean military participation in Ukraine.
