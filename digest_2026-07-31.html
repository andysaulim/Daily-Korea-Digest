KOSPI surges record 18% to reclaim 6,500-point level on semiconductor and AI chip rally

South Korean stocks skyrocketed by a record 18% on July 31, with the KOSPI reclaiming the 6,500-point level as foreign investors poured back into semiconductor and AI-linked equities, reversing the prior session's panic selloff that had triggered an unprecedented second circuit breaker. The New York Times and Financial Times both reported the surge, with the FT attributing the move to renewed global confidence in Korea's AI hardware exposure. The won strengthened sharply against the US dollar on the back of foreign equity buying.

So what — The extreme volatility — a panic selloff followed by a record 18% rebound within 48 hours — directly highlights South Korea's semiconductor concentration risk, a theme tracked in the CSIS Korea Chair's ongoing trade and technology work.

Yonhap (AEN20260731008152320), NYT, FT
US-Korea

National Assembly passes bill stripping prosecutors of all investigative powers; acting prosecutor general offers to resign

The National Assembly, led by the ruling Democratic Party, passed legislation removing all remaining investigative powers from South Korean prosecutors on July 31, with the vote coming minutes after the DPK terminated a 24-hour PPP filibuster using its three-fifths supermajority. Police and a newly created government agency will assume all frontline investigative authority, confining prosecutors to indictment tasks. Acting Prosecutor General Koo Ja-hyeon offered to resign following passage.

So what — The legislation restructures the ROK criminal justice system in a way that PPP critics argue will shield the ruling party from prosecutorial scrutiny — a flashpoint directly relevant to ongoing debates about rule of law under the Lee Jae-myung administration.

Yonhap (AEN20260731003252315), Korea Times
US-Korea

ROK forces nearly engaged US spy drone near DMZ after misidentifying it as enemy threat

South Korean Army's 1st Corps activated the Durumi anti-air defense alert system and deployed thermal observation devices and radar on July 30 after spotting an unidentified flying vehicle in the Civilian Control Line buffer zone south of the MDL, before the Joint Chiefs of Staff confirmed it belonged to the US military. The incident raised immediate questions about real-time allied coordination protocols near the border. The PPP called for the defense minister's resignation, characterizing the event as an unprecedented security failure.

So what — Directly relevant to the upcoming Ulchi Freedom Shield exercise in August, when allied coordination at the operational level will be under heightened scrutiny.

Yonhap (AEN20260731004053315), Korea Times
