This is the Korea Week in Review from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It covers October 31, November 6, 2026.

The week in brief.

North Korea's four weapons tests in a single week, spanning cluster-warhead ballistic and cruise missiles, a new surface-to-air missile, and a large drone exhibition, mark its most intense testing tempo in years and came alongside the FBI's formal attribution of the record 6.3 billion dollar Binance theft to Pyongyang. Kim Yo Jong's conditional dialogue signals paired with simultaneous launches and a flat denial of the hack attribution define the week's posture. Next week, watch Seoul's National Assembly action on the Cheonghae deployment expansion and any U.S. or allied response to the sustained North Korean testing pace.

The top 10 stories of the week.

First: North Korea conducts four weapons tests in one week, including ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, and a new SAM. North Korea launched several missiles from the Sondok area of South Hamgyong Province toward the East Sea on November 4, its fourth weapons test in a week. Earlier tests included an October 31 live-fire of the 'Standard Type 2' cluster-bomb warhead for tactical ballistic missiles and a November 3 cruise missile test with a 'middle-sized dispersal warhead' covering 5 to 6 hectares; a new surface-to-air missile system with 'intelligent multiple guided mode' was tested on the same day as the November 4 missile launch. The Wall Street Journal characterized the pace as North Korea's busiest missile-testing spree in years.

Second: FBI formally attributes 6.3 billion dollars Binance hack to North Korean unit TraderTraitor; Kim Yo Jong denies it. The FBI formally attributed the record-breaking theft of approximately 6.3 billion dollars from Binance to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-linked hacking unit. Kim Yo Jong issued a rare personal statement via KCNA calling the FBI finding a 'grave political provocation' and flatly rejecting Pyongyang's involvement. President Trump had previously said the U.S. was investigating North Korean responsibility, and blockchain intelligence firms had separately tied the theft to the same group.

Third: Kim inspects Nampho Shipyard, reveals new warship class larger than destroyers under construction. KCNA reported that Kim Jong Un inspected the Nampho Shipyard and was shown a new warship under construction that he described as having a 'huge and imposing appearance, which bears no comparison' to existing vessels. The inspection came the same week North Korea confirmed multiple weapons tests, including drone, missile, and naval programs. Analysts also found that Nampho Port's container yard is very likely expanding by at least 45 percent.

Fourth: Kim Jong Un attends drone expo displaying at least 15 drone types, including new stealth strategic UAV. Kim Jong Un attended the 'Unmanned Aviation Technology Development-2026' exhibition on October 29, where North Korea displayed at least 15 drone types across strategic, operational, and tactical categories, including mock attacks on a South Korean guard post and a U.S. tank. 38 North later reported that North Korea soft-revealed a new stealth strategic UAV at the exhibition. Kim stated that seizing absolute superiority in drone technology is 'an indispensable option in the light of the ever-changing patterns of modern warfare.'

Fifth: U.S., South Korea, Japan foreign ministers sign Trilateral Arctic-Pacific Partnership memorandum of understanding at G20 Atlanta, reaffirm North Korean denuclearization. Foreign Ministers Cho Hyun, Marco Rubio, and Toshimitsu Motegi met on the margins of the G20 in Atlanta on October 30 and signed a Memorandum of Cooperation for a Trilateral Arctic-Pacific Partnership. The three reaffirmed the goal of complete denuclearization of North Korea; the U.S. also raised China's 'coercive behavior in the West Sea' at the meeting, though South Korea and Japan did not. Foreign Minister Cho separately met EU, UK, India, and Mexico counterparts on the G20 sidelines.

Sixth: President Lee submits record 820.9 trillion won 2027 budget centered on AI, defense, and wartime operational control acceleration. President Lee Jae-myung presented an 820.9 trillion won budget to the National Assembly on November 2, up 12.8 percent from 2026 and the largest annual budget in South Korean history. The budget prioritized artificial intelligence, defense spending, and acceleration of the wartime operational control transfer. Seoul also separately signaled that wartime operational control transfer could advance in early 2027, with the December Security Consultative Meeting eyed for a timeline announcement.

Seventh: South Korea October exports hit 115.3 billion dollars on semiconductor boom; September current account surplus breaks record at 61.5 billion dollars. South Korea's October exports reached approximately 115.3 billion dollars, nearly doubling year-on-year on surging AI-driven semiconductor demand, putting a 1 trillion dollar annual export target within reach. The Bank of Korea reported a 61.5 billion dollar current account surplus in September 2026, breaking the prior monthly record of 49.7 billion dollar set in June. South Korea's economy also grew faster than expected in the third quarter of 2026, bolstering the case for a Bank of Korea rate hike in November.

Eighth: Lee-Erdogan summit agrees to expand nuclear plant and defense cooperation; passport data of 168 delegation members leaked. South Korean President Lee Jae-myung and Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan agreed during a summit in Seoul to deepen cooperation in nuclear power plants, defense manufacturing, and infrastructure. The summit was overshadowed by a security breach in which passport numbers, dates of birth, and other personal details of 168 people, including President Lee, First Lady Kim Hea-kyung, and Foreign Minister Cho Hyun, were leaked from the presidential aircraft manifest. Police opened a probe into the leak.

Ninth: Seoul expands Cheonghae anti-piracy mission toward the Strait of Hormuz, adds P-8A and EOD team. South Korea decided to expand its Cheonghae anti-piracy mission toward the Strait of Hormuz, adding a P-8A maritime patrol aircraft and an explosive ordnance disposal team. The move follows months of U.S. requests for support in the region after the U.S.-Iran war. The decision requires National Assembly consent under South Korean law on overseas military deployment.

Tenth: OpenAI CEO Altman makes unannounced Seoul visit, meets Samsung and SK Group on AI chip partnerships. Sam Altman, chief executive of OpenAI, visited South Korea over the weekend without prior announcement, meeting with approximately 50 Samsung executives in Yongin and separately having lunch with SK Group leadership to discuss AI chip partnerships. The visit came as South Korea's 2027 budget placed artificial intelligence at its core and as Samsung Biologics workers launched a second strike involving approximately 400 employees. Hanwha Defense USA also won a 269 million dollar U.S. Navy contract to build a medium landing ship at Philly Shipyard during the same period.

Now to Pyongyang.

Kim Jong Un made a public appearance on 0 of 6 days.

North Korea maintained a high operational tempo throughout the week, conducting four weapons tests covering ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, cluster warheads, and a new surface-to-air missile system, while state media led on Kim Jong Un's drone exhibition and Nampho Shipyard inspection. Kim Yo Jong issued a rare personal denial of the Binance hack attribution while simultaneously leaving the door conditionally open to U.S. talks, pairing diplomatic signaling with the same day's missile launches. North Korea also formally rejected its status as a non-nuclear-weapons state and dismissed sanctions as ineffective.

Kim Yo Jong, in their words: Grave political provocation.
