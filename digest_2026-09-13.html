US-Korea

First US investment project under tariff deal nears closure — minister targets this-week announcement

Industry Minister Kim Jung-kwan said Sunday that Seoul is aiming to finalize and announce the first concrete US investment project under the Korea-US Strategic Investment Corporation framework before the week ends, with a video call with American counterparts planned early this week to resolve a handful of remaining issues. Kim, speaking at Incheon Airport upon his return from negotiations, confirmed the $200 billion annual cap and the overall strategic investment ceiling will be held as agreed, and declined to specify whether a gas combined-cycle power plant, a nuclear plant project, or another sector would be the first announced deal. The development marks the first implementation step since the National Assembly passed the Special Investment Act in March 2026 with a 226-8-8 vote, creating the legal vehicle for channeling the $350 billion government-level pledge.

Technology

South Korea's expanded espionage law takes effect, targeting chip technology protection

South Korea's revised espionage law entered into force Sunday, broadening the definition of protected national secrets to cover semiconductor technology, battery manufacturing processes, and other strategic industrial assets — a direct response to documented foreign intelligence efforts against Korean chipmakers. The law, reported in depth by Reuters and Bloomberg last week, creates new criminal exposure for industrial spying by foreign agents and tightens penalties for domestic leaks. The reform accompanies new Foreign Agent of Concern screening provisions aligned with US BIS export control frameworks, and follows Bloomberg's Sep 11 reporting that the National Intelligence Service had lobbied hard for the expanded scope.

China-Korea

Seoul wins 264.1 billion won ISDS case against Chinese investor at ICSID

An ad hoc committee of the International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes ruled fully in South Korea's favor, rejecting a 264.1 billion won compensation claim filed by Chinese national Min — who had been convicted by Korean courts in 2017 of embezzlement and fraud related to a loan obtained from Woori Bank. The ruling allows Seoul to recover more than 6.4 billion won in litigation costs from the claimant; President Lee Jae-myung posted on X that 'foreign investors cannot be protected for investments that violate Korean law,' signaling the government will take an aggressive posture against similar claims. The case is notable as the claimant had alleged Korean bank and court actions constituted treaty violations under the Korea-China bilateral investment agreement.
