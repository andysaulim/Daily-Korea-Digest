This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Wednesday, November 25.

Three things today: North Korea expels a Swiss diplomat, South Korea's KOSPI suffers its sharpest drop in months, and Seoul expands its naval unit toward the Arabian Sea. Tomorrow, a Korean railway workers' work-to-rule action begins at 9 a.m., the first step toward a threatened general strike in December.

First, North Korea has expelled Switzerland's top diplomat in Pyongyang and ordered the Swiss diplomatic office closed within 48 hours.

The North Korean Foreign Ministry's European Department director general, Pak Hyon Su, formally declared the head of Switzerland's Pyongyang office, Sylvain Astier, persona non grata. Pak also submitted a memorandum demanding the office itself close. The office had opened only in October 2026, making it one of the shortest-lived diplomatic presences in Pyongyang in recent memory.

The trigger was Switzerland's decision to sanction the Songdowon International Children's Camp over its alleged role in transferring children from Russian-occupied Ukraine. Kim Yo Jong had threatened this step on November 24. The expulsion came the next day.

What makes this significant for people watching North Korea's diplomatic posture is the selectivity of it. N.K. News analysis points out that North Korea targeted Switzerland and the United Kingdom while leaving EU member states that also sanctioned the camp entirely unscathed. That suggests Pyongyang is making a calibrated move, not a blanket retaliation. Switzerland, for its part, confirmed its sanctions remain in effect.

This episode also arrives against a backdrop of reported North Korean missile activity. N.K. Pro analysis of satellite imagery suggests North Korea may have conducted a long-range missile static engine test around the time President Donald Trump visited China and floated a meeting with Kim Jong Un at the APEC summit in Shenzhen. KCNA has remained silent on both that reported test and the failed summit offer. Trump, for his part, said he could resume personal correspondence with Kim Jong Un immediately.

The expulsion of Switzerland's mission head is the fact to hold onto: a Swiss diplomatic office, opened just weeks ago, is now closed on 48 hours' notice.

Second, South Korea's main stock index suffered one of its worst single-day drops of the year, and a domestic political confrontation over the Supreme Court is intensifying at the same time.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, fell 308.52 points, or about 6 percent, to close at 4,697.51 on Wednesday. An overnight Wall Street tech selloff wiped out the gains from the previous session's artificial intelligence rally. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix each fell roughly 10 percent. The dollar traded at about 1,343 won.

The selloff underlines a vulnerability that an analysis in The Diplomat argues is structural: South Korea's economy is so concentrated in advanced memory chips that whenever Wall Street's sentiment on artificial intelligence reverses, the KOSPI takes a disproportionate hit.

On the political front, Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok formally proposed building a five-party coalition with progressive parties to accelerate the impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae. The judiciary pushed back directly. The Changwon District Court issued the first formal judicial statement of opposition, warning that the impeachment push undermines the constitutional separation of powers.

People Power Party leader Jang Dong-hyuk rallied supporters in Gyeongju, North Gyeongsang Province, near Chief Justice Cho's hometown. At that rally, Jang called President Lee Jae-myung a criminal hiding in the Blue House afraid of going to prison and a dictator who is dismantling the Republic of Korea from the ground up. The People Power Party has planned a relay of regional outdoor rallies through November 29.

Two things to watch ahead: on November 27, the National Judges' Conference meets, and judges may put motions about the Chief Justice impeachment on the floor. A national court presidents' conference is scheduled for December 4.

Third, South Korea is expanding the operational reach of its Cheonghae naval unit into the open sea south of Oman, and the decision is already drawing fire at home.

President Lee Jae-myung reaffirmed the expansion after the National Assembly defense committee approved a revised dispatch motion. The Cheonghae unit's zone will widen from the Gulf of Aden to open waters approximately 650 kilometers from the Strait of Hormuz. The unit will add maritime patrol aircraft and mine-clearing support to its existing destroyer role. Critically, the Strait of Hormuz and adjacent waters are explicitly excluded from the area of operations.

Minor opposition parties have pushed back, calling the expansion a backdoor troop dispatch to the ongoing U.S.-Iran conflict. The government's framing draws a line at the Hormuz strait, but critics argue 650 kilometers is close enough to matter.

The expansion is happening as South Korea's Foreign Ministry deepens its engagement with the region. Foreign Minister Cho Hyun met with Omani Foreign Minister Al Busaidi in what Seoul describes as the first visit by an Omani foreign minister in 26 years. The two discussed maritime security, shipbuilding, and port logistics alongside traditional energy ties. Oman is South Korea's sixth-largest naphtha supplier and seventh-largest liquefied natural gas supplier.

The number to remember: 650 kilometers from the Strait of Hormuz, the outer edge of where the Cheonghae unit can now operate.

Now to Pyongyang.

State media's dominant story Wednesday was the one you just heard: the expulsion of Switzerland's mission head and the demand for the office's closure. KCNA described the Swiss sanctions on the Songdowon camp as a "blatant hostile act." Domestic content, by contrast, covered mushroom production and health unit achievements.

What KCNA did not cover is also worth noting. State media stayed silent on N.K. Pro's reported intercontinental ballistic missile engine test and on the failed Trump-Kim summit offer at APEC in Shenzhen. Kim Jong Un has not made a public appearance in two days.

Satellite imagery from Planet Labs, analyzed by Daily N.K., shows North Korea's two new destroyers moored at separate bases: the Kang Kon at Mayang Island naval base and the Choe Hyon at Nampo. Shipyard expansion at Chongjin and Nampo is consistent with additional large-warship construction.

The rest of the news, quickly.

Russian Tu-160 strategic bombers, capable of delivering nuclear weapons, entered South Korea's Korea Air Defense Identification Zone without prior notice on November 24 and flew near Ulleungdo's airspace. The Defense Ministry's international policy chief, Kim Hak-jo, summoned Russian defense attaché Colonel Nikolai Marchenko to lodge a formal protest and demand recurrence prevention.

A Russian court document has disclosed that a North Korean worker employed at a Russian drone manufacturing facility was arrested for theft. It is the first documented instance placing a North Korean national inside a Russian weapons production plant. Separately, Daily N.K. reports Russia paid North Korea in fuel for a construction contract after Pyongyang rejected Indian rupees and Moscow could not meet the demand for U.S. dollars or Chinese yuan.

Chinese Ambassador Dai Bing publicly defended South Korea-China relations against People Power Party attacks on National Intelligence Service director-designate Kang Keum-sil, whose advisory roles for Chinese local governments have drawn criticism. People Power Party leader Jang Dong-hyuk labeled Kang "pro-China, anti-American." The Democratic Party called the attack "outdated McCarthyism."

An expert panel reviewing Japan's major security documents has included in a draft recommendation a proposal to revisit Japan's Three Non-Nuclear Principles, which bar Japan from possessing, producing, or permitting entry of nuclear weapons, according to Yonhap's Tokyo bureau.

Seoul prosecutors summoned sitting ambassador to China Ro Jae-hun as a suspect for questioning in a probe related to an alleged Roh Tae-woo slush fund. The summons is expected around the end of November.

Samsung Display is acquiring a 9.98 million share stake in European chipmaker ams OSRAM at 21 euros per share, a total of 210 million euros, to co-develop next-generation microLED technology, becoming the company's largest shareholder.

Saudi Aramco and its South Korean subsidiary S-OIL inaugurated the Shaheen petrochemical complex, adding approximately 3.2 million tonnes of annual production capacity. It is the first commercial deployment of Aramco's proprietary thermal crude-to-chemicals technology.

A cyberattack on Korea Investment Savings Bank exposed the personal data of 86,433 customers, including passport and account numbers. That is the largest data breach in the domestic savings bank industry on record.

Gallup Korea's survey taken November 3 to 5 put President Lee's approval at 41 percent, up from the prior reading. The Democratic Party stood at 36 percent, also up. The People Power Party was at 28 percent, down. Independents were also at 28 percent, stable.

On Capitol Hill, there are no hearings listed for today.

Beyond the region, South Korean Foreign Minister Cho Hyun met with Omani Foreign Minister Al Busaidi in Seoul, the first Omani foreign minister visit in 26 years. The two sides covered energy trade, maritime security, shipbuilding, and port logistics. South Korea and Oman are expanding a relationship built on naphtha and liquefied natural gas into new areas, including renewable energy.

Finally, the markets. Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed down about 6 percent at 4,697.51. The won traded at about 1,343 to the dollar. Brent crude fell about 3 and a half percent to close near 88 dollars. The Bank of Korea's benchmark rate stands at 3 percent, last set in October.
