Kim Jong Un commissions Kang Kon destroyer, declares it part of nuclear response system

North Korea formally commissioned the Kang Kon (No. 52), a 5,000-ton Choe Hyon-class destroyer, at a ceremony on the east coast on September 7 — one day before the DPRK's 78th founding anniversary. Kim Jong Un declared the vessel part of the DPRK's nuclear response system, stated he wanted enemies to 'be afraid' of its nuclear weapons, and called the commissioning a 'clear signal to enemies.' The Kang Kon is the second Choe Hyon-class destroyer; it capsized during its 2025 launch ceremony and was subsequently refloated and recommissioned. The DPRK defense minister separately condemned the Freedom Edge 2026 trilateral exercise opening on the same day.

Russia and North Korea open first Tumen River road bridge in completion ceremony

Russia and North Korea officially opened their first road bridge over the Tumen River on September 7, per Russian state media TASS and Interfax — creating the first permanent vehicle crossing between the two countries. Interfax reported Russia simultaneously completed its border checkpoint facility on the Russian side. The bridge opening follows NK Pro satellite imagery from September 4 that showed landslide and flood damage in the border region from August 31 to September 1.
