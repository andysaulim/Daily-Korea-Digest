This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair. It's Saturday, October 10.

Today is the 81st anniversary of North Korea's Workers' Party — and Kim Jong Un has not been seen in public for five straight days. Satellite imagery shows parade preparations in Pyongyang, but no sign of Kim himself. Meanwhile, Washington has issued its sharpest public rebuke of Seoul's intelligence practices since the Lee government took office, and South Korea's ambassador to Ukraine is back in Seoul after an 18-year-first diplomatic recall. Three things today: the U.S.-Korea intelligence rift, the Ukraine envoy dispute, and what North Korea is — and isn't — doing on its biggest political holiday.

Start with the alliance. A senior U.S. official said Friday that Washington expects Seoul to, quote, "uphold alliance standards," unquote — a direct reference to concerns about South Korea's intelligence and counterintelligence management. That is the sharpest public U.S. statement about the bilateral intelligence relationship since President Lee Jae-myung's government came to office in June 2025. Seoul has not issued a formal response.

The statement didn't come from nowhere. It follows what Yonhap reported on October 9 as a spy agency snub, and a separate episode involving a visa delay affecting a senior South Korean lawmaker. SBS this week examined what it described as multiple friction indicators across the alliance — beyond just the visa episode.

That context matters for you. This isn't a single irritant. U.S. officials appear to be signaling, publicly, that they see a pattern. And Seoul's silence so far only sharpens the question of how the Lee government intends to respond.

The bottom line: the public U.S. demand that Seoul meet alliance standards is now on the record. Watch for a formal South Korean response, and watch whether this language escalates or gets walked back in the days ahead.

The second story is tied to the first in a specific way. South Korea's ambassador to Ukraine, Park Ki-chang, landed back in Seoul on Saturday morning — two days after his formal recall was announced. Hankyoreh reports this is the most assertive South Korean diplomatic move in 18 years.

The dispute is about North Korean prisoners of war. Kyiv publicly disclosed the identities of two captured North Korean soldiers. Seoul says that violated an intelligence-sharing agreement and could endanger those soldiers and their families. Ukraine's foreign ministry responded that, quote, "diplomacy must prevail over emotion," unquote.

The recall has also cracked open a domestic political fight. PPP lawmaker Na Kyung-won sent a personal letter of thanks to President Zelensky over the disclosure. The ruling Democratic Party called that, quote, "acts tantamount to treason," unquote. Na fired back, calling the ambassador recall, quote, "a performance for Kim Jong Un," unquote, and accusing the ruling party of behaving like the Korean Workers' Party.

So you have a live diplomatic rupture with Ukraine, a parallel brawl in the National Assembly, and — layered on top — the U.S. alliance concerns. These three threads are all pulling at Seoul's foreign policy credibility at the same time. Ambassador Park is home. The dispute with Kyiv is not resolved.

Now to Pyongyang on its biggest day. Today is the Workers' Party of Korea's 81st founding anniversary — and North Korea's state media marked it by declaring the country's, quote, "dignity and prestige have reached the highest level," unquote. There was no new policy announcement and no weapons activity reported.

What's missing is Kim Jong Un. He has not appeared in confirmed public reporting for five consecutive days, which the North Korean statement history tracker flagged as a watch condition heading into today. N.K. Pro satellite imagery published October 9 showed hundreds of military trucks at North Korea's main parade training base, raising the possibility of a conventional military parade. But the same analysis found no signs of launch preparations or major weapons displays.

Kim Yo Jong has been active in his absence. She issued a statement around October 9, invoking Kim Jong Un's authorization, labeling Seoul's proposed medical aid to North Korea a, quote, "provocation," unquote — her latest in a series of anti-Seoul statements ahead of the anniversary.

The key facts as of now: Kim has been absent five days. Parade preparations were visible from space. No launch indicators. Watch the next 24 hours for either a Kim appearance or a military display announcement.

North Korea's state media output today. KCNA's top item was a sharp denunciation of the U.S. Trafficking in Persons report, calling it a, quote, "grave provocation," unquote — timed to the anniversary, consistent with Pyongyang's pattern of anti-U.S. statements on politically significant dates. The second major item was the satellite imagery story N.K. Pro published — parade training activity at the main base, hundreds of trucks, no launch signs. Third, state media this week hailed what it called a strong fall harvest, calling on workers to devote themselves to the harvest, though no verified production figures were provided.

The bottom line from the KCNA tracker: Kim Jong Un is five days absent on the party's founding anniversary, parade preparations are visible, rhetoric is escalating across multiple tracks, and the next 24 hours are the window to watch.

A few other things worth your time today.

In Daegu, at least 38 people were hurt when ceiling panels collapsed at a commercial building on Saturday morning. Police have opened an investigation. President Lee Jae-myung ordered all relevant agencies to mobilize all available equipment and personnel. The Prime Minister separately ordered full resource deployment. The National Forensic Service is scheduled for a joint inspection of the site on Sunday, October 11.

On the domestic political front, PPP lawmaker Ahn Cheol-soo pressed President Lee on Saturday over reports that North Korean land mines have washed ashore near a South Korean beach, asking how long the president would remain silent on the DMZ mine incidents.

On South Korea's defense capabilities, World Politics Review this week examined South Korea's first successful test of a domestically developed hypersonic glide vehicle, attended by President Lee in early October. The piece places the test within Seoul's broader strike architecture and notes that North Korea conducted its own intermediate-range missile drill — featuring claimed AI-guided maneuvering — the same week.

In the economy, Seoul residential property prices have continued to rise despite the government's tightening of land transaction permit zones, with analysts split on whether the policy is working. Separately, a Chosun Ilbo investigation found that South Korean liquefied natural gas power plant operating costs have roughly doubled because those plants must repeatedly start and stop to compensate for gaps in renewable energy supply. And Korea's trade promotion agency KOTRA flagged expanding Chinese product-certification requirements as a growing export barrier for Korean goods.

In the region, Vietnamese nationals have overtaken Koreans to become Japan's largest foreign resident community as Japan's overall foreign population expands. Closer to home, the Japanese Embassy in Seoul held its annual Korea-Japan Festival under the theme "Toward a Better Future." And the South Korean Embassy in Beijing hosted the seventh annual K-Food Festa in the Wangjing district, with 20 Korean restaurants and 11 food companies taking part.

Gallup Korea's October 6-to-8 poll — the first since the Chuseok holiday — shows President Lee's approval holding at 37 percent, unchanged from mid-September. Disapproval eased from 56 to 51 percent. Democratic Party support fell four points to 35 percent, while the People Power Party rose to 26 percent and independents climbed to 31 percent.

National Assembly Speaker Cho Jeong-sik is on an overseas visit, meeting Croatian counterparts on energy infrastructure and defense cooperation.

Foreign Minister Cho Hyun posted Saturday to offer condolences after a major earthquake in Panama, noting he had met Panama's foreign minister on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in September.

Markets to close. The dollar bought about 1,341 won on Friday, down about a tenth of a percent. Seoul's main stock index closed most recently on October 8 at just over 6,625, down about 2 and a half percent. Brent crude was just above 104 dollars a barrel on October 9, up slightly. The Bank of Korea's policy rate stands at 2.50 percent.
