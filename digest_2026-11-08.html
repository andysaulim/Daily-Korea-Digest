01Top stories
JTBC, DPRK

North Korea fires two ballistic missiles from Muncheon toward East Sea

North Korea launched two ballistic missiles from the Muncheon area toward the East Sea, with a reported flight distance of approximately 350 km, JTBC reported Sunday.

The launch marks the fifth weapons test in roughly a week, following an air-defense surface-to-air missile test and a cruise missile airport-strike drill confirmed by state media on Nov. 6. The US State Department separately called for international coordination against North Korean cyber threats following FBI attribution of the Binance hack to Pyongyang.

Yonhap, US-Korea

Senior US Navy and Marine Corps officials assess HD Hyundai's Ulsan shipyard under MASGA

A US delegation including Assistant Secretary of the Navy Brendan P. Rogers, Marine Corps Lt. Gen. Andrew M. Niebel, and Maj. Gen. Valerie A. Jackson visited HD Hyundai Heavy Industries' Ulsan shipyard on Nov. 4 to evaluate naval shipbuilding capabilities, installation infrastructure, and logistics support. The visit was posted on the Pentagon's Defense Visual Information Distribution Service and cited opportunities to strengthen maritime sustainment and operational readiness. HD Hyundai signed a joint venture agreement with US construction firm Kiewit in October to build a new US shipyard, with operations targeted for 2029.

Yonhap TV, US-Korea

FM Cho Hyun flies to Paris for G7 foreign ministers meeting, first face-to-face with Rubio since midterms

Cho Hyun departed for Paris to attend the G7 foreign ministers meeting, where he is expected to hold his first in-person talks with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio since the Democratic Party's midterm victory.

Korean media flagged the meeting as an opportunity to advance discussions on the nuclear submarine cooperation file and broader security consultations stalled by US domestic politics. Multiple outlets also noted that South Korea's Army and Navy chiefs of staff are scheduled to visit Japan this month for talks with Japan Self-Defense Forces leadership.

GNA, ROK Politics

National Assembly passes 'fabricated prosecution' special counsel bill

The ROK National Assembly plenary passed the Special Counsel Act to investigate alleged fabrication of criminal prosecutions under the Yoon Suk Yeol administration, covering 12 cases including the Daejang-dong, Wirye, and Baekseok-dong development scandals in which President Lee Jae-myung had been indicted.

The Justice Ministry's own investigative panel found no evidence of fabricated prosecution in a first-round review of three cases, a finding that legal scholars say burdens the special counsel before it launches. Critics in the opposition People Power Party contend a structural conflict of interest exists because the president, himself a defendant in the cases, will appoint the special counsel.
