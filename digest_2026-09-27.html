DPRK

North Korea Is Testing Swarm Attacks Mixing Drones and Missiles

North Korea is testing coordinated swarm attacks that combine drone formations with ballistic missile launches, the WSJ reports, describing a doctrinal evolution aimed at overwhelming multi-layered air-defense systems. The testing, which follows the Sep 21 new-type weapon demonstration overseen by Kim Jong Un, complicates ROK-US terminal and mid-course intercept planning simultaneously. The tactic mirrors adaptations observed in the Russia-Ukraine conflict, where drone-missile pairing has been used to saturate defense batteries, and parallels KCNA's recent 'collective punishment' threat framing against South Korea.

US-Korea

S. Korean military, U.N. Command conduct joint probe on DMZ blast site

A 40-person joint investigation team comprising ROK operational troops, UN Command Military Armistice Commission officials (including the secretary), and forensic investigators reached the near-vicinity of the Sep 21 suspected mine-blast point on Sep 27 — six days after two ROK soldiers were injured — but halted short of the exact site due to uncharted minefields and personnel exhaustion. Precision mine-detector sweeps ran from 0900 to sundown; the JCS said a follow-on investigation will be conducted and results disclosed transparently. The PPP is pressing the government to explain the delay, with independent lawmaker Han Dong-hoon alleging that a decision to begin investigation only on Sep 29 — after forecast rainfall — constitutes dereliction of duty.
