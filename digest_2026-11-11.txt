This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Wednesday, November 11.

Three things today: North Korea fires two ballistic missiles and publishes its revised constitution, South Korea's foreign minister meets Secretary of State Rubio in Paris, and bond dealers face a record fine. The missile launches are the latest in a string of weapons tests over the past week, and North Korea has kept every one of them off its own domestic news agenda. Also today, the Korea Fair Trade Commission has announced that record fine on bond-auction colluders.

First, North Korea launched two ballistic missiles from Muncheon into the East Sea today, with a flight distance of approximately 350 kilometers. That makes this the latest in a series of weapons tests over the past week, all of which have gone unreported inside North Korea.

Separately, North Korea's official web portal Naenara published the full text of a revised constitution. The Supreme People's Assembly amended the document on March 23, 2026. It formalizes Kim Jong Un's expanded authority and redefines the country's territory to exclude South Korea. That is a formal constitutional codification of the two-state framework. N.K. News reports this is the first time the constitution section had been restored to the Naenara portal since North Korea pledged to rewrite the document in early 2024.

The two developments together put the constitution and the missile program on the same page. A Foreign Affairs analysis out today argues that North Korea Trump now faces is fundamentally different from the one he engaged in 2018 and 2019. The piece points specifically to the expanded nuclear arsenal, the March 2026 constitutional revision, and deepened ties with Russia as factors that have sharply narrowed diplomatic space for any potential Trump-Kim engagement.

What to watch: whether South Korea or the United States issues a formal response to the launches, and whether the constitutional text draws a reaction from Washington.

Second, South Korea's Foreign Minister Cho Hyun met U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio on November 10 on the sidelines of the G7 Foreign Ministers' Meeting in Paris. The two exchanged views on overall bilateral relations, high-level exchanges, and North Korea, according to South Korea's Foreign Ministry. They agreed to continue working toward implementing the agreements reached by President Lee.

Foreign Minister Cho posted after the meeting: "I met with U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio on the sidelines of the G7 Foreign Ministers' Meeting. We exchanged views on South Korea-U.S. bilateral relations, high-level exchanges, and issues related to North Korea. We agreed to continue working closely together to make progress on the implementation of the agreements reached by President Lee."

Rubio confirmed South Korea's presence at the gathering. At a press conference at the George C. Marshall Center in Paris, he noted that partners from Saudi Arabia, Brazil, Egypt, and South Korea had joined the G7 session.

Also in Paris on November 10, Foreign Minister Cho met Canadian Foreign Minister Anita Anand. The two agreed to deepen cooperation in energy, specifically expanding imports of Canadian crude oil and liquefied natural gas, as well as in artificial intelligence, critical minerals, and defense. The two sides described Canada and South Korea as comprehensive strategic partners.

Cho also held his first in-person meeting with Ukrainian Foreign Minister Sybiha. The Korea Herald notes it was the first face-to-face encounter since a dispute over North Korean prisoner-of-war disclosures erupted in September. Reuters reports that Sybiha said ties with South Korea are on the road to improvement after those talks.

Third, South Korea's Fair Trade Commission announced a record fine of 1.2 trillion won on fifteen bond-auction colluders today. President Lee Jae-myung posted his reaction, saying no one should dream of bid-rigging and that the commission would continue building a fair market.

That is the domestic economic story of the day. The fine lands against a backdrop of mixed signals from South Korea's broader economy. South Korea's jobless rate has hit a three-year low, though Bloomberg reports that overall hiring momentum is slowing. At the same time, inflation remained above target in October, which the Wall Street Journal says constrains the Bank of Korea's room for near-term rate cuts. The Bank of Korea's benchmark rate currently stands at 3.00 percent, last changed in October 2026.

The ruling and opposition parties have also reached a tentative deal in the National Assembly to delay South Korea's digital asset tax by two more years, pushing it to 2029. That would be the fourth such postponement.

That directly contradicts Kim Yo Jong's repeated public denials. and South Korean diplomatic pressure than China or Russia.

The bottom line from state media: Naenara confirmed the revised constitution formalizing Kim's expanded powers and the two-state framework, while the ballistic missile launches from Muncheon remain entirely absent from domestic output. Kim Jong Un has not made a public appearance in three days.

The rest of the news, quickly.

Unification Minister Chung Dong-young said today that North Korea's military demonstrations are also signals directed at South Korea, and that reducing hostility is the core of the Lee government's peace policy. Chung said easing adversarial relations must come first before substantive dialogue can be pursued.

Joint Chiefs of Staff Chairman General Jin Yong-sung flew a KF-21 fighter jet and inspected South Korea's Korea Air and Missile Defense readiness. He called it a "core asset" of the three-axis deterrence system and ordered an around-the-clock early warning posture.

In the National Assembly, Speaker Cho Jeong-sik outlined a framework for a constitutional amendment, stating that barring the current president from re-election is a core principle of a sovereignty-based revision. He said 2027, a year without scheduled elections, would be an appropriate time to pursue it.

South Korea's Defense Ministry briefed lawmakers on a revised plan to relocate the Korea Military Academy to the Chungcheong region, keeping the Naval Academy in Jinhae and the Air Force Academy in Cheongju. Implementation is expected from the 2029 academic year.

On North Korea's diplomatic footprint, N.K. News reports the North is listing its Mexico City embassy building for sale at over two million dollars. The flag and identifying plaque have been removed, though staff remain on-site. Separately, the Czech Republic has restored its Pyongyang embassy, with Karel Srol taking up the ambassador post in October, the fifth European Union member state to reestablish a diplomatic presence there.

A North Korean citizen has reunited with a family member in the South, the first such case in two years. New data in the report shows over three-quarters of South Koreans separated from family in the North have already died.

North Korean envoy Kim Sung-nam's third Beijing visit fueled summit speculation, but Seoul's Foreign Ministry assessed the trip was aimed at sharing results of a Chinese party meeting and maintaining high-level exchanges, not primarily arranging a summit.

A Russian youth delegation is conducting a historical tour in Pyongyang, the latest in a pattern of expanding people-to-people exchanges between Russia and North Korea through 2026.

South Korea's Finance Ministry has pledged 20 million dollars to an Asian Development Bank trust fund supporting artificial intelligence for development. Turkey is reported to be narrowing its shortlist for a new nuclear power deal, with Bloomberg naming Canada and South Korea as the leading candidates. And South Korean battery makers LG Energy Solution, Samsung SDI, and SK On are facing heightened regulatory scrutiny in Hungary.

A Gallup Korea poll taken October 27 to 29 shows presidential approval at 39 percent, stable. The Democratic Party stands at 35 percent, down. The People Power Party is at 30 percent, up.

On Capitol Hill.

The lame-duck session of Congress has opened with the National Defense Authorization Act, North Korea-related bills, and committee chair transitions all in play, as Democrats prepare to assume majority control in January. House Republicans blocked a Democratic effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. An expert cited by SBS assessed that Democratic control of Congress could complicate or slow South Korean corporate investments in the United States, as Democratic committees may apply greater scrutiny to deals tied to the 350 billion dollar pledge and the Korea-U.S. Strategic Investment Corporation.

Beyond the region.

South Korea's Defense Acquisition Program Administration held a Korea-Indonesia Defense Industry Cooperation Committee meeting today, discussing joint development and joint exports of defense products beyond the disputed KF-21 program. Poland received its final 2026 delivery of South Korean K2 tanks and K9 self-propelled howitzers, completing a shipment for deployment on NATO's eastern flank under a multi-year framework contract.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed at about 5,375, up about 2.6 percent. The dollar bought about 1,334 won, with the won up about 0.7 percent. Brent crude was at about 92 dollars a barrel, up about 1.3 percent.
