01Top stories
NK News / Reuters / France 24, DPRK

Kim Yo Jong sets 'days' deadline for border loudspeakers, personally denounces ROK lawmaker

Kim Yo Jong declared in a Korean Central News Agency statement that North Korea's border loudspeakers will be operational 'within a few days,' after she and senior military officials inspected frontline fortification sites.

She separately demanded a South Korean presidential adviser 'stop rambling and shut his mouth' after he criticized Pyongyang's loudspeaker plan. Seoul's military said no new North Korean weapons had been spotted at the border but indicated proportional measures were under consideration.

WSJ / Reuters / CNBC, ROK Economy

Bank of Korea holds rate at 3.00% after back-to-back hikes, BOK governor warns on fiscal expansion

The Bank of Korea held its benchmark rate at 3.00% as expected, pausing after consecutive increases as policymakers assessed the pass-through effects of prior hikes.

BOK Governor Shin Hyun-song issued unusually direct criticism of expansionary fiscal policy, saying authorities should be cautious about increasing spending using temporary revenue sources. Goldman Sachs assessed the next hike is likely in the first quarter of 2027, not November.

Yonhap / Korea Herald, DPRK

Seoul sends UNSC letter demanding North Korea account for DMZ land mine blast

South Korea plans to send a letter to the president of the UN Security Council urging North Korea to take responsibility for the recent demilitarized zone land mine explosion, per Yonhap. The Korea Herald reported Seoul would simultaneously appeal to the Security Council over the armistice violation allegation.

The ROK Ministry of Foreign Affairs briefed ambassadors from 16 countries, including the Korea Club and Peace Club diplomatic groups, on the government's North Korea policy posture and the DMZ incident at a session held in Seoul.

Nikkei, US-Korea

Seoul and Washington spar over wartime operational control transfer

South Korea and the United States are in dispute over the transfer of wartime operational control (OPCON) from a US general to a South Korean commander, per Nikkei.

The friction adds to alliance strains already flagged by a top US envoy who cited 'turbulence' in the relationship over diverging security and trade priorities. The Quincy Institute noted Seoul's push for OPCON transfer reflects a broader bid to reduce dependence on American command authority.
