This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair. It's Monday, October 12.

In today's brief: NK fires 4th missile salvo in a month; Russian emergency minister in Pyongyang; KOSPI -3.65% on tech selloff; and Bank of Korea rate hold defended.

Our top story: North Korea fires multiple ballistic missiles — fourth test in a month — after Workers' Party anniversary. Kim Jong Un's military fired multiple ballistic missiles from the Pyongyang area toward the East Sea at approximately 9:20 a.m. KST on October 12, with South Korea JCS confirming at least some flew over 900 kilometers; precise specifications remain under joint U.S.-South Korea analysis. The launch came one day after the subdued 81st Workers' Party founding anniversary concert and nine days after the previous intermediate-range missile firing drill. South Korea Presidential National Security Office convened an emergency meeting and demanded North Korea immediately halt what it called illegal launches; China's Foreign Ministry spokeswoman said Beijing had "no new comment" on the firing.

Also making news: Alexander Kurenkov, head of Russia's Emergencies Ministry, EMERCOM, landed at Pyongyang's Sunan International Airport around 10 p.m. KST Sunday, according to Flightradar24 data and North Korean state media, marking the ministry's first-ever visit to North Korea. The trip follows a Moscow-Pyongyang agreement to train North Korean rescuers in Russia; Yonhap reported the delegation will discuss cooperation on disaster management projects. The visit extends the pattern of Russian ministerial engagement with Pyongyang across civilian technical domains alongside ongoing military-technical cooperation.

And one more top story: Seoul shares plunged more than 3 percent Monday, extending losses driven by technology sector weakness amid Middle East tensions and inflation concerns, with the KOSPI closing at 6,384.24, -3.65%. Bank of Korea Governor Rhee Chang-yong said back-to-back rate hikes had bought the central bank time to respond to financial risks, defending the recent tightening cycle even as equity markets fell sharply. The won simultaneously hit a near two-year high against the dollar, driven by lower oil prices and dollar sales, creating a split-screen of currency strength and equity weakness.

Here's what else moved overnight. Seoul denies U.S. has curbed most NK-related intelligence sharing. The Defense Ministry: 'No restrictions on intel sharing needed to maintain combined defense posture.'

Unification Minister Chung Dong-young says South Korea-U.S. alliance intact despite conflicting interests. Chung dismissed concerns amid U.S. pressure over intelligence disclosure.

North Korean defector appointed head of Korea Hana Foundation for first time. First defector to lead the settlement-support foundation.

South Korean Defense Ministry opens military's first AI transformation base at Pangyo Techno Valley. Physical AI hub for humanoid and quadruped robots, data collection, and field testing.

Now to Pyongyang. State media's top stories today. Kim attended the anniversary concert but issued no speech, message, or public statement — the most subdued WPK founding observance in recent years. North Korea fires missiles days after South Korea's hypersonic test. North Korea marks 81st anniversary of ruling party on modest scale. The bottom line: Kim Jong Un attended the subdued October 10 WPK anniversary concert without issuing any statement, then North Korea fired multiple ballistic missiles on October 12 — the fourth test in a month. Watch for KCNA's official characterization of the launch, which typically arrives within 24 to 48 hours and may include a Kim guidance claim or technical assertion.

The number of the day. 900+ km: Minimum flight distance of North Korea ballistic missiles fired October 12. South Korea JCS confirmed at least some of the multiple ballistic missiles fired from Pyongyang toward the East Sea flew over 900 kilometers — the fourth North Korean missile test in a month.

In Seoul, the government side. Clarifies data on military academy integration — 11 of 30 large-army nations use integrated model. The Defense Ministry's spokesperson issued a public correction to a media report claiming no country with over 200,000 troops operates an integrated military academy, stating that of 30 countries worldwide with armed forces above that threshold, 11 use an integrated academy model and 19 use a separated model, including Japan and Turkey in the integrated category.

At the National Assembly's Administration and Security Committee: The committee will hold a confirmation hearing October 14 for nominee Kim Ji-yong as head of the new Serious Crime Investigation Agency; nominee submitted written answers defending his independence from political influence and pledging to end 'targeted investigation' practices.

On the economy. KDI warns South Korea's economic recovery has yet to benefit households broadly. The Korea Development Institute said growth driven by robust chip exports has not yet translated into broad household income gains, signaling uneven distribution of the recovery and flagging risks for domestic consumption.

China's CXMT said it will release next-generation DRAM within 2026; Samsung's equivalent product is not expected until 2028 mass production. Industry sources warn the claim may overstate current capability but urge Korean chipmakers not to underestimate China's technology catch-up pace, particularly as BIS export controls limit CXMT's access to leading U.S. equipment.

South Korea's Supreme Court dismissed the tax office's appeal, ruling in favor of Google Korea in a long-running corporate tax dispute, in a decision with implications for how platform-economy multinationals are taxed on Korean-sourced revenue.

How is the public reading all this? In the latest Gallup Korea poll, from October 6-8, President Lee's approval is 37 percent. By party: the Democratic Party at 35 percent, the People Power Party at 26 percent, and independents at 31 percent.

In their own words. President Lee Jae-myung said: "Drones, lasers, and robots are already crossing the boundaries of land, sea, and air. As the battlefield changes, the way we train officers must also change. The government will build the best integrated military academy suited to South Korea's security environment."

What's coming up? Oct 14: National Assembly confirmation hearing for CSIS director nominee Kim Ji-yong. Oct 12 ongoing: South Korean National Assembly annual parliamentary audit — multiple committees active. Oct 28: The Industry Ministry minister field tour: Daegyeong region, battery and robotics industry,.

A few more things worth knowing. N.K. Pro analysis examines why the inter-Korean boundary in the DMZ remains physically unmarked, following the landmine explosions that injured South Korean soldiers last month and raising questions about whether North Korea mines were deliberately placed south of the boundary line.

President Lee Jae-myung ordered authorities to investigate and respond sternly to reported abuse of workers at a car battery plant, in a directive that intersects with ongoing labor-standards scrutiny of Korean battery supply chains linked to U.S. EV investments.

President Lee's office announced plans for him to relocate to the Cheong Wa Dae, Blue House, official residence before year-end, reversing the Yoon-era decision to relocate the presidential office to Yongsan.

Finally, the markets. Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, is at 6,384, down about 3.6 percent. The dollar is at 1,332 won, down about 0.8 percent. Brent crude is at 105.8 dollars a barrel, up about 1.1 percent. The Bank of Korea's base rate is 2.50 percent.
