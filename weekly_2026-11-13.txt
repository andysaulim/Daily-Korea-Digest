The week in brief.

The defining pattern of the week was North Korea's sustained weapons tempo, six tests in seven days spanning ballistic missiles and a new torpedo, conducted without any domestic acknowledgment, alongside an intensifying naval modernization drive personally directed by Kim Jong Un. Seoul's attention was split between managing post-midterm U.S. congressional dynamics, the Cho-Rubio Paris meeting, and an escalating domestic political fight over the special counsel bill. Next week's APEC summit in Shenzhen is the critical watch point: President Lee's attendance, Foreign Minister Cho's Beijing talks with Wang Yi, and the slim but noted prospect of Trump-Kim contact all converge there.

The top 10 stories of the week.

First: North Korea conducts six weapons tests in one week, maintains domestic blackout. North Korea launched two ballistic missiles from Muncheon toward the East Sea on November 8, flight distance approximately 350 kilometers, tested a new high-speed torpedo on November 10 with an announced early-2027 Navy deployment timeline, and launched two more ballistic missiles from Muncheon on November 11. Throughout the week, state media confirmed none of these tests in domestic reporting, maintaining a complete internal blackout on all recent weapons activity. Zelensky separately confirmed Russia used North Korean KN-23 missiles to strike a Ukrainian energy facility.

Second: KOSPI plunges 6 percent on November 6, recovers partially by week's end. The KOSPI fell 6.02 percent on November 6, briefly breaching 5,000 intraday, as Democratic Party victories in U.S. gubernatorial races sparked fears of tighter AI data-center regulation; Goldman Sachs assessed 45 percent of U.S. data-center projects at risk of review or halt. The Financial Security Supervisory Service suspended brokerage margin collateral requirements as an emergency volatility measure. The index partially recovered, rising 4.39 percent on November 10 and a further 2.6 percent on November 11, closing at 5,375.36 on November 11.

Third: Kim Song Nam completes five-day Beijing visit; Kim-Xi summit speculation intensifies ahead of APEC. Workers' Party international secretary Kim Song Nam returned home November 7 after a five-day China visit during which he met Politburo Standing Committee members, his third high-level Beijing meeting. Wang Yi separately met North Korean officials and pledged closer ties. The visit fueled speculation about a Kim Jong Un-Xi Jinping summit ahead of the APEC summit in Shenzhen scheduled for November 18.

Fourth: Foreign Minister Cho meets Rubio in Paris; agrees to advance Lee-Trump summit agreements. Foreign Minister Cho Hyun traveled to Paris for the G7 Foreign Ministers' Meeting and held a bilateral meeting with U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio on November 10, their first in-person talks since the Democratic midterm sweep. The two agreed to advance Lee-Trump summit agreements, exchanging views on the overall bilateral relationship. Cho also met French and Canadian counterparts, with the Canadian meeting producing an agreement to expand Canadian oil and liquefied natural gas imports.

Fifth: Kim Jong Un inspects Nampho Shipyard and east coast naval base, orders accelerated naval buildup. Kim Jong Un inspected Nampho Shipyard and ordered strengthening of surface and underwater naval capabilities alongside modernization of the facility, with warship construction reportedly spreading to a third shipyard. On November 11 he inspected construction of a major naval base on North Korea's east coast, ordering completion within schedule and to the quality standard set by the party central. KCNA confirmed both appearances.

Sixth: National Assembly passes special counsel bill; People Power Party holds mass rally against it. The South Korean National Assembly plenary passed the Special Counsel Act to investigate alleged fabrication of criminal prosecutions under the Yoon Suk Yeol administration, covering 12 cases including the Daejang-dong matter. The People Power Party held a mass rally against the bill and called for presidential impeachment. The Blue House indicated the promulgation order would be submitted to the cabinet meeting the week of November 17.

Seventh: Foreign Minister Cho to visit Beijing for Wang Yi talks on North Korea and bilateral ties. Foreign Minister Cho Hyun announced a two-day visit to Beijing starting Friday November 13 to meet Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi, covering North Korea-related issues, bilateral relations, and regional matters. The visit follows the renovation of North Korea's Beijing embassy amid intensifying Pyongyang-Beijing exchanges. A dinner with Wang Yi was also scheduled.

Eighth: North Korea publishes revised constitution formalizing Kim's expanded powers and two-state framework. North Korea's state outlet Naenara published the full text of the revised constitution on November 11, formalizing Kim Jong Un's expanded powers and the two-hostile-states framework. Daily N.K. reported separately that North Koreans are avoiding the word 'unification' as Kim's two-hostile-states doctrine enters the constitution. Kim Yo Jong attacked U.N. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres over his call for restoration of inter-Korean dialogue, accusing him of echoing Seoul's 'unilateral and provocative sophism.'

Ninth: Seoul Cabinet approves Cheonghae unit expansion toward Hormuz; Iran warns South Korea off. The South Korean cabinet approved expansion of the Cheonghae anti-piracy unit's operational scope toward the Hormuz region, adding support for restoration of freedom of navigation to the unit's mandate, with the motion sent to the National Assembly where the ruling Democratic Party holds a majority. Iran warned South Korea to refrain from Hormuz involvement, citing 60 years of bilateral ties. Seoul separately confirmed it was expanding the unit's operational scope toward the Arabian Sea.

Tenth: Samsung chip packaging expansion groundbreaking; Lee says it will spur 30 billion dollars in additional investment. President Lee Jae-myung said Samsung Electronics' advanced semiconductor packaging fab expansion in Asan, South Chungcheong Province, would spur 30 billion dollars in additional investment. The Chungcheong Mega Project broke ground with eight anchor firms accelerating investment. Lee also told a cabinet meeting that South Korea must maintain bipartisan cooperation with the U.S. Congress following the Democratic midterm sweep.

Now to Pyongyang.

Kim Jong Un made a public appearance on 3 of 6 days, most recently on Thursday November 12.

Kim Jong Un appeared on three days, personally overseeing naval modernization at both Nampho Shipyard and an east coast naval base under construction. State media confirmed the revised constitution's publication and Kim Yo Jong's rebuke of the U.N. Secretary-General, while maintaining a complete domestic blackout on all six weapons tests conducted during the week. The North Korean Foreign Ministry also formally rejected the G7's denuclearization call.

Kim Jong Un, in their words: Modern naval base construction is a major core project directly linked to our state's safety and defense of maritime sovereignty, and must unconditionally be completed within the period decided by the party central and at the quality level demanded by the party central.

Kim Yo Jong, in their words: Prejudiced and irresponsible attitude, echoing South Korea's unilateral and provocative sophism.
