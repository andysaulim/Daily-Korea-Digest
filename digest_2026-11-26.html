01Top stories
NK News, DPRK

Kim inspects nuclear warheads and enrichment sites, rejects recognition from any country

Kim Jong Un inspected three nuclear sites on November 25, a weapons-grade nuclear materials production base, a nuclear-powered equipment production base, and the Nuclear Weapons Institute, where KCNA published photos of him examining warheads, possible naval nuclear reactors, and rows of centrifuge cascades at two uranium enrichment facilities.

He ordered wider deployment of tactical nuclear weapons across multiple operational domains and declared North Korea's nuclear status "irreversible," explicitly rejecting the notion of seeking "recognition" from any country. The inspection followed Trump's offer of a bilateral meeting at APEC in Shenzhen, which Kim ignored, and came on the same day NK News reported a ballistic missile engine test occurred during Trump's outreach.

WSJ, ROK Economy

Bank of Korea raises rate to 3.25%, third hike this year, as AI boom fuels growth and inflation

The Bank of Korea raised its benchmark interest rate by 25 basis points to 3.25% on November 26, the third such increase in 2026, with the BOK governor citing AI-sector expansion and inflationary pressure as drivers. The central bank also upgraded its 2027 growth outlook. The rate now stands at its highest level in the current cycle.

Korea Herald, US-Korea

Trump's new Section 301 tariff proposal targets 14 economies but preserves Korea's 15% ceiling

The Trump administration proposed new tariffs under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 against 14 trading partners over industrial overcapacity, including South Korea, while a senior US official confirmed the proposed duties would not exceed the 15% tariff ceiling agreed under last year's bilateral trade deal.

Japan, the European Union, and Taiwan also retain their 15% caps; China faces the highest rate at 20% on top of earlier tariffs. The Korea Herald reports that digital regulations remain a concern for Seoul alongside the overcapacity framing.

South Korea Favorability Remains Stable Overall as Partisan Views Diverge. The 2026 KEI survey finds two-thirds of Americans view South Korea favorably, essentially unchanged since the question was first asked in 2020. Democrats have grown more favorable (69% to 75%) while Republicans have declined from a peak of 76% in 2023 to 64% in 2024, creating a partisan divergence with potential implications for alliance politics in a polarized US environment. Food is the top-cited Korean cultural export, drawing nearly double the interest of beauty products, movies, and pop music combined. (Korea Economic Institute of America (KEI) / YouGov, KEIA)
The United States Breaks a Decade of UN Consensus on North Korean Human Rights. The United States abstained on the UN Third Committee vote November 18 that adopted a resolution critical of North Korea's human rights record, the first US abstention since the annual process began in 2005 under George W. Bush. Robert King, a former US Special Envoy for North Korean Human Rights, connects the timing to ongoing signals about a potential Trump-Kim diplomatic opening. (Robert King, KEIA)
Technology Companies Dominate Advertising at North Korean Football Matches. 38 North's Martyn Williams finds that advertising at North Korean football matches is dominated by domestic technology companies, an unusual phenomenon in a planned economy where there is typically little need for commercial promotion. The findings point to a nascent internal consumer technology market that the regime is apparently permitting to develop. Williams' analysis provides a rare data point on the DPRK's internal economic evolution at a time when external trade data are limited. (Martyn Williams, 38 North)
