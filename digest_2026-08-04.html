Japan-Korea

Seoul lodges 'strong' protest over Japan's renewed Dokdo claim, demands 'immediate withdrawal'

South Korea's foreign ministry filed a formal strong protest against Japan's renewed territorial claim to Dokdo on August 4 and demanded its immediate withdrawal. The protest was lodged 11 days before Liberation Day (August 15), when Japan-Korea historical tensions routinely intensify. Japan's annual Defense White Paper or related policy documents are among the recurring triggers for Seoul's Dokdo protests.

So what — The protest directly precedes President Lee Jae-myung's August 15 Liberation Day address, which historically sets the tone for Japan-Korea diplomatic posture for the remainder of the year.

Pattern: Japan's annual defense white papers and related documents regularly trigger South Korean Dokdo protests in July-August, a pattern documented across multiple years in the CSIS database.

US-Korea

Cabinet approves prosecution reform bill; law strips prosecutors of all investigative powers from October 2

President Lee Jae-myung chaired a Cabinet meeting that approved the revised prosecution reform law, following its passage by the National Assembly on July 31. The law, effective October 2, ends more than seven decades of prosecutorial authority to both investigate and indict. Lee declined to exercise a veto, citing the principle of separation of powers, while the People Power Party and Reform Party called the move unconstitutional.

So what — The People Power Party has signaled a Constitutional Court challenge; the court's ruling on constitutionality will determine whether the October 2 effective date holds.
