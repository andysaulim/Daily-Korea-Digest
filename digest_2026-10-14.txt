This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair. It's Wednesday, October 14.

In today's brief: NK museum flaunts Hwasong-20; New Yalu Bridge opens Thu; DOJ North Korea IT crackdown; Sue Mi Terry charges dropped; and Lee names new CoS.

Our top story: Kim Jong Un opened the National Defense Revolution Museum on October 13, showcasing the Hwasong-20 intercontinental ballistic missile and other strategic weapons capable of reaching the continental United States, vowing to 'more vigorously develop Juche weapons.' President Donald Trump simultaneously said he 'may' meet Kim during his upcoming November Asia trip. N.K. Pro analysis found the museum contains over 25 missile launch vehicles and 15 types of drones and naval weapons, and may be located at a suspected nuclear site.

Also making news: The U.S. Department of Justice charged 14 people, arrested two, and seized 187 computer devices in a crackdown on North Korean remote IT worker schemes that penetrated over 70 American companies. The operation targeted individuals who helped North Korean workers obtain fraudulent employment and funnel revenue to Pyongyang. A laptop farm operation in Texas was among the targets.

Also making news: China and North Korea will officially open the New Yalu River Bridge connecting Dandong and Sinuiju on Thursday, October 15, according to Xinhua — more than a decade after the span was constructed. The bridge completion signals a new phase of NK-China cross-border economic connectivity, arriving as NK-Russia road infrastructure, Tumangang–Khasan bridge, opened September 8, 2026, also became operational.

And one more top story: U.S. federal prosecutors agreed to drop charges against Sue Mi Terry, a former CIA analyst and prominent North Korea expert accused of secretly working for South Korea's intelligence agency. The case, which sent shockwaves through the Korea policy community when announced approximately two years ago, was headed for trial when the agreement was reached. Terry had faced charges of acting as an unregistered agent of a foreign government.

Here's what else moved overnight. Seoul and Washington agree to schedule Security Consultative Meeting after U.S. midterm elections. Key bilateral defense talks deferred until post-November midterms.

Kamala Harris at Asan Institute slams Trump's cuts to U.S.-South Korea drills, calls for stronger extended deterrence. Cites deepening NK-China-Iran-Russia ties as rationale.

President Lee appoints National Tax Service chief as new presidential chief of staff. Predecessor Kang Hun-sik departed after 499 days.

South Korea finds hacking incidents at 16 financial firms; ~100,000 personal data records presumed leaked. FSS confirms breach across 16 institutions; investigation ongoing.

Russia announces state-funded feature film starring South Korea-born actor as North Korea soldier fighting in Kursk. Film titled 'Sudzha. Brothers in Arms'; production underway.

Now to Pyongyang. State media's top stories today. KCNA officially characterized the October 12 multi-missile launch as a 'tactical' ballistic missile test aimed at improving weapons systems — the fourth test in a month. Kim Jong Un opens new weapons museum as North Korea claims missile test success. North Korea's Kim showcases missile capable of reaching U.S. as Trump signals possible meeting. Reuters' KCNA-sourced report: Kim used the museum opening to display U.S.-range missiles as Trump publicly floated the possibility of meeting Kim during his upcoming Asia trip. The bottom line: KCNA officially designated the October 12 multi-missile salvo as a 'tactical' ballistic missile test aimed at weapons improvement — the fourth test in a month — while Kim's museum opening vow to 'more vigorously develop Juche weapons' signals the testing surge will continue. Watch for North Korea response to Trump's 'may meet Kim' signal and any North Korean diplomatic or rhetorical signal ahead of the November Asia trip. Kim Jong Un was last seen in public 1 day ago.

The number of the day. 187: Devices seized in DOJ North Korean IT laptop farm bust. DOJ's Texas crackdown arrested two people and seized 187 computer devices as part of charges against 14 individuals helping North Korea IT workers fraudulently infiltrate over 70 American companies.

In Seoul, the government side. President Lee declared a restructuring of population policy to focus on quality of life rather than birth rates, pledging to strengthen state care responsibilities and reform labor, housing, and education systems to meet demographic change. The government plans to train 100,000 youth care professionals.

Foreign Minister Cho Hyun issued a condemnation of recent Houthi attacks on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh and Saudi Aramco's Dhahran headquarters, calling for an immediate halt to attacks on civilian infrastructure. Separately, Cho met with South Sudan's presidential envoy Adut Salva Kiir Mayardit to discuss bilateral cooperation and the situation in South Sudan.

At the National Assembly's National Assembly, unspecified committee,: A parliamentary committee passed a formal resolution denouncing North Korea for the detonations that cut off the Gyeongui and Donghae inter-Korean roads, assessed as aimed at severing the links.

At the National Assembly's National Assembly, PPP,: The People Power Party called for an investigation into alleged infiltration of the Democratic Party by followers of the Shincheonji religious movement, demanding a public explanation from a senior DP official.

On the economy. SK Hynix projected Wednesday that AI infrastructure performance will increase 20-fold by 2030, underpinning its strategic rationale for continued HBM investment and its LOI with NVIDIA for HBM4 supply announced at the San Francisco AI Summit in July 2026.

Bank of Korea Governor said monetary policy will remain restrictive for now, citing inflationary pressures from the AI investment surge and elevated energy prices linked to the Middle East crisis, with Brent crude at $106.33. The Bank of Korea rate stands at 2.50% following its October 2026 action.

Samsung Electronics CTO Song Jae-hyuk acknowledged the competitive challenge from Chinese memory maker CXMT's next-generation 4F² DRAM — which reported is expected to launch before year-end, two years ahead of Samsung — while confirming Samsung is also developing the technology. The disclosure, at a public technology forum, is the first senior Samsung acknowledgment of the specific CXMT threat.

Elsewhere in the region. Senior diplomats of South Korea and Norway met in Seoul to discuss expanding bilateral defense and technology cooperation, part of Seoul's effort to diversify its security partnerships in Europe as South Korea-Russia bilateral relationship remains frozen over the Ukraine conflict.

How is the public reading all this? In the latest Gallup Korea poll, from October 6-8, President Lee's approval is 37 percent. By party: the Democratic Party at 35 percent, the People Power Party at 26 percent, and independents at 31 percent.

In their own words. Lee Jae-myung said: "Hiding abroad cannot help criminals escape the law. We will track them down to the end and repatriate them."

South Korean Ministry of National Defense said: "Defense Minister Kang Shin-chul visited the 1st Fighter Wing in Gwangju and the Army Ground Operations Command on October 14 to inspect readiness and encourage troops. He emphasized that 'when accumulated strength comes together, our military can win, and when our military wins, the Republic of Korea wins.'".

What's coming up? October 15: New Yalu River Bridge, Sinuiju–Dandong, officially opens to traffic. October 24: South Korean government to host second state-sponsored Itaewon disaster memorial ceremony. Post-US midterms: South Korea-U.S. Security Consultative Meeting, SCM — Seoul and Washington in talks to schedule after U.S. midterm elections.

A few more things worth knowing. Special rapporteur Elizabeth Salmón presented the report to the UN General Assembly on Tuesday, calling capital punishment moratorium a first step toward abolition and repeating concerns about possible executions of North Korean workers who were forcibly repatriated from Russia.

New White House Press Secretary Katie Zachariah confirmed the Alaska gas figure, the first official White House dollar amount attached to the liquefied natural gas component of the 350 billion dollar pledge framework.

KCNA carried a statement marking the centennial of an anti-imperialist organization stressing the need to keep developing nuclear deterrence and ruling out any negotiation over the weapons program — the latest in a consistent rhetorical line running alongside the museum opening and missile tests.

And from the analysts. From CSIS: North Korea's military partnership with Russia constitutes a new axis of impunity that actively undermines international accountability for North Korea human rights abuses, as the revenue and political cover Moscow provides insulates Pyongyang from pressure.

From CSIS: Both the U.S. and Korea should move beyond cautious, incremental investment pledges and execute bold, concrete commitments across energy, technology, and defense sectors to lock in the strategic partnership.

From World Politics Review: The piece examines French and South Korean efforts to build a coalition of mid-sized powers to defend their film and audiovisual industries from U.S. platform dominance and AI-driven content disruption. It frames the film industry as a vehicle for testing whether middle powers can build effective multilateral coalitions on cultural economy issues. The Korea angle connects to the broader debate — reflected in today's NYT piece on 'The Assassin, s,' culture wars — about how South Korea cultural policy intersects with political identity.

Finally, the markets. Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, is at 6,009, down about 0.4 percent. The dollar is at 1,336 won, up about 0.1 percent. Brent crude is at 106.3 dollars a barrel, up about 0.6 percent. The Bank of Korea's base rate is 2.50 percent.
