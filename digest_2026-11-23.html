01Top stories
Korea Herald / Yonhap, ROK Politics

Lee reshuffles security team, names first female spy chief in NIS history

President Lee Jae-myung nominated former Justice Minister Kang Kum-sil as National Intelligence Service director, a move that, if confirmed, would make her the first woman to head South Korea's spy agency since its 1961 founding. Second Deputy National Security Adviser Lim Woong-soon, a career diplomat with extensive experience in US affairs and former ambassador to Canada, was tapped as foreign minister, while former floor leader Hong Ihk-pyo was nominated as unification minister.

Hankyoreh reported that a Blue House official characterized the reshuffle as aimed at redefining the NIS's legal and institutional role rather than expanding its inter-Korean function, with outgoing officials labeled as the 'self-reliance faction' and their replacements aligned more closely with the alliance-first camp.

Korea.net, ROK Economy

Seoul activates market stabilization measures as KOSPI drops 3.3% on AI selloff fears

Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Finance and Economy Lee Hyoung Il convened an emergency market review on November 22 and announced phased stabilization measures, including preparations to activate the Stock Market Stabilization Fund and a three-month easing of daily share-repurchase order limits for listed companies effective November 24.

The Financial Services Commission formally approved the buyback rule change at its 20th regular meeting on November 23. The government characterized the selloff as driven by uncertainty over AI and semiconductor industry profitability, US data-center regulations, tighter semiconductor export controls, and potential US interest rate increases.

NK News / Korea Times / SBS, DPRK

Kim Jong Un holds first direct ambassador credentials ceremony in over 30 years, cites East Asia security threat

Kim Jong Un personally received credentials from ambassadors of 10 countries, Russia, Sweden, Indonesia, Vietnam, Brazil, the Czech Republic, India, the Philippines, Thailand, and Bangladesh, at the Workers' Party of Korea Central Committee headquarters on November 22, the first such direct reception in over 30 years according to Korea Times and financial news reports. Kim delivered a speech declaring that North Korea pursues no hegemony or expansion, framing the country's military buildup as the exercise of its sovereign right, and said the security situation in the DPRK and the East Asian political and military environment demand that Pyongyang provide for itself the power and capability to defend itself. KCNA reported Foreign Minister Choe Son Hui also attended.
