This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Thursday, November 5.

Three things today: North Korea's fourth weapons test in a week, Seoul expanding its anti-piracy mission toward the Strait of Hormuz, and a record current account surplus. That fourth test in seven days, a new air-defense missile and a cruise missile airport-strike drill, comes the same day Kim Yo Jong is signaling the door to talks isn't entirely shut. The Bank of Korea Governor departs tomorrow for a Bank for International Settlements meeting running through November 11.

First, North Korea's weapons tests are piling up fast.

On Wednesday, Kim Jong Un's Missile Administration tested a new surface-to-air missile system, confirming what Pyongyang described as its "intelligent multiple guided mode" and target-identification capability. Separately, North Korea staged a long-range strategic cruise missile strike drill simulating an attack on an enemy airport using cluster munitions on a runway. N.K. News found that images published by North Korea's state news agency were heavily doctored.

Those tests follow short-range ballistic missile launches from the Sondok area of South Hamgyong Province on November 4. That makes four weapons tests in a single week.

The same day those missiles flew, Kim Yo Jong issued a statement. She is the Director of the General Affairs Department of the Workers' Party of Korea. She said, and this is a direct quote: "Dialogue is clearly one of the options, but it does not necessarily serve our national interests." She branded the FBI's attribution of a 6.3 billion dollar Binance hack to North Korea as an extension of Washington's unchanged hostile policy, and said there is no need to sit down with, in her words, "untrustworthy and hostile people" absent a policy change.

Unification Minister Chung Dong-young read that differently. He said that looking at Kim Yo Jong's statements over the past month, more than ten appearances, she has used the word "dialogue" for the first time, and he read it as a signal that the door is not entirely closed.

What to watch: whether Pyongyang's combination of tests and conditional talk signals a negotiating posture or simply escalation. Either way, four tests in seven days is the tempo you need to keep in mind.

Second, Seoul is pushing its naval reach toward the Strait of Hormuz.

South Korea approved expanding its Cheonghae anti-piracy unit's operational zone from the Gulf of Aden to waters south of Oman. The expansion adds a P-8A maritime patrol aircraft and an explosive ordnance disposal team. The Defense Ministry says Somali piracy has surged more than three times over the previous year, with coalition forces concentrated in the Gulf and Red Sea following the U.S.-Iran war and Houthi clashes since early this year, leaving the Gulf of Aden under-protected.

The motion passed a vice-ministerial meeting Wednesday. It still requires National Assembly consent under South Korean law before the deployment can proceed.

The move follows months of U.S. requests for regional support that Seoul had effectively declined. The lawmakers expressed support for Korean soldiers injured in a DMZ landmine blast.

Experts say the Democratic sweep of Congress complicates a separate but related ambition: South Korea's plans for a nuclear submarine, which would require U.S. transfer approvals. Democratic control, those experts warn, makes that harder to secure.

The key number: the Cheonghae unit's operational radius will roughly double, to approximately 1,930 kilometers.

Third, South Korea's trade account just broke a record.

The Bank of Korea reported that South Korea's current account surplus reached 61.5 billion dollars in September 2026, breaking the previous monthly record of 49.7 billion dollars set in June. The year-to-date cumulative surplus through September reached 340.7 billion dollars, more than four times the same period in 2025.

The Korea Herald argues that Democratic control of Congress is unlikely to unravel the U.S.-Korea strategic investment framework, because bipartisan support for shipbuilding, semiconductors, and power grid industries provides structural continuity regardless of which party controls the legislative agenda. KOTRA's post-midterm analysis reached the same conclusion.

That said, South Korean defense stocks tell a different story. The combined market cap of Hanwha Aerospace, LIG D and A, Hyundai Rotem, and Korea Aerospace fell to roughly 62 trillion won from a 52-week peak of 170 trillion won by the end of October, as the drone era reshapes what buyers are spending on.

The headline number to remember: 61.5 billion dollars in a single month, a new record.

Now to Pyongyang.

State media's main story today is a statement from Kim Yo Jong conditioning any U.S. talks on abandonment of hostile policy, framing the FBI's Binance accusation as proof Washington hasn't changed. The brief's bottom line captures it: North Korea confirmed its fourth weapons test in a week, Kim Yo Jong publicly conditioned any talks on a U.S. policy reversal, and Workers' Party International Secretary Kim Song Nam led a party delegation to Beijing, where he met China's Wang Yi and separately received a delegation from the Party School of the Chinese Communist Party Central Committee. Kim Jong Un has not appeared publicly in six days.

The rest of the news, quickly.

The Seoul High Court has set January 26, 2027 as the sentencing date for former President Yoon Suk Yeol's insurrection appeal. Special counsel sought the death penalty; Yoon maintained in his final statement that his December 3, 2024 martial law declaration did not constitute insurrection.

Separately, the Seoul High Court upheld Yoon's election law conviction, leaving an 18-month suspended sentence intact. The court found his false statements violated voters' right to informed choice. If the sentence is finalized, the People Power Party may owe 39.7 billion won in campaign reimbursements.

In the National Assembly, the ruling Democratic Party brought what it calls a "fabricated indictment special prosecutor bill" to the floor. The People Power Party responded with a filibuster, blocking a vote.

President Lee Jae-myung visited a public redevelopment project in Seongbuk District on November 4 and ordered faster housing supply, instructing officials to allow conversion of private redevelopment projects to public ones and to strengthen incentives including floor area ratio bonuses for early-stage districts.

South Korea's consumer price inflation stayed above the Bank of Korea's target in October, the Wall Street Journal reported, keeping pressure on the central bank ahead of its next rate decision. The Bank of Korea's current rate is 3 percent, set in October.

Seoul plans to introduce cornerstone investor lockup rules to stabilize share prices after stock listings.

A Seoul court rejected Coupang's bid to block a 423 billion won data breach fine, upholding the largest fine ever imposed on a single company in South Korea, over a breach affecting more than 37 million users.

Brazil's first ambassador to North Korea in a decade, Ricardo Primo Portugal, has taken up his post in Pyongyang, confirmed by a Chinese Embassy announcement.

A UNESCO evaluation body has recommended inscribing taekwondo as intangible cultural heritage representing North Korea, ahead of a final committee decision next month, with no South Korean involvement.

38 North analysis documents a first public showing of a new North Korean stealth strategic drone at an October 30 exhibition, following Kim Jong Un's call for absolute superiority in drone technology at the same event.

ArmsControlWonk analysis of October 30 imagery finds Nampho Port is very likely expanding its primary container storage yard by at least 45 percent, which the analysis flags as atypical since North Korea does not generally transport goods domestically in standard intermodal containers.

The Lowy Interpreter argues that Ukrainian warnings of massive North Korean troop deployments proved overstated, with Pyongyang extracting military experience, revenue, and technology access from the Russia partnership at a smaller scale than predicted.

Gallup Korea's October 27 to 29 poll put President Lee Jae-myung's approval at 39 percent. Democratic Party support stood at 35 percent, the People Power Party at 30 percent, and independents at 28 percent.

Coming up: the Unification Ministry holds its "Peace On: dam" inter-Korean cultural event at Cheomseongdae in Gyeongju on November 7, running 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. The APEC Leaders' Summit is scheduled for November 18 and 19 in Shenzhen.

On Capitol Hill.

Senator Jon Ossoff secured re-election in Georgia. The brief notes he has been a vocal advocate on Korean Peninsula policy and Korea-related trade issues. That re-election keeps a pro-Korea voice in the Senate. Meanwhile, experts cited by Yonhap warn that Democratic control of Congress complicates both the nuclear submarine transfer question and prospects for a Trump-Kim summit.

Beyond the region.

North Korea told the European Union to "mind its own business" after the EU passed a human rights resolution targeting Pyongyang. South Korea joined the U.N. General Assembly resolution as co-sponsor for the fifth consecutive year. South Korea's Foreign Ministry also rejected Moscow's claim that U.S.-South Korea military exercises rehearse nuclear preemptive strikes, calling the exercises defensive, annual, and aimed at maintaining combined readiness against North Korea's nuclear and missile threats.

Finally, the markets.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed at about 5,384, down about 5 percent on the day. The won traded at about 1,344 to the dollar. Brent crude was at about 96.50 dollars a barrel, up about 2 percent.
