This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Sunday, October 25.

Three things today: Kim Jong Un returns from a surprise China visit; South Korea posts a record current account surplus; and the justice minister shortlist narrows to two names. Kim crossed into China with no warning and no meeting with Xi Jinping, and the question for Korea watchers is what that deliberate gap signals. The national audit concludes this week, with the probe into Chief Justice Cho Hui-dae still the sharpest fault line.

First, Kim Jong Un's visit to Dandong.

On October 24, Kim crossed the New Yalu River Bridge into the Chinese border city of Dandong. The trip was a day-trip, unannounced, and Kim met no one at the top: there was no meeting with Chinese leader Xi Jinping, a clear departure from his previous visits to China, which were full state-level summits.

What Kim did do: he toured the Memorial Hall to the Chinese People's Volunteers, the hall dedicated to Chinese troops who fought in the Korean War. He met Wang Huning, the fourth-highest-ranking Chinese official, and separately held talks with Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi. Kim said North Korea is, in his words, "inspired" by China's success, and he vowed to advance a relationship "forged in blood." The framing was deliberate, the visit coincided with the 76th anniversary of China's entry into the Korean War.

Kim's delegation was senior but not a summit lineup. It included party presidium member Jo Yong-won, Foreign Minister Choe Son-hui, and Kim Yo-jong.

State media in Pyongyang confirmed the trip. International wire services, Reuters, the AP, and the Wall Street Journal, all reported the visit, with analysts suggesting it may reflect a new cold-war diplomatic posture, or possibly a pre-coordination move ahead of any future contact with the United States. No U.S.-North Korea talks are currently scheduled.

The Financial Times separately reports that Beijing is watching the deepening Pyongyang-Moscow axis with concern, and that Kim's Dandong stop lands in that context.

The bottom line: Kim made a pointed symbolic gesture toward Beijing, invoking shared wartime sacrifice, while keeping the visit below the level of a bilateral summit.

Second, South Korea's economic headline.

President Lee Jae-myung posted Sunday that South Korea's current account surplus in the second quarter exceeded the combined surpluses of Germany and Japan. He called it "a great wave of opportunity" driven by semiconductor exports, and said South Korea is now a leading economy rather than a follower of advanced nations. Lee called for the surplus to translate into jobs and income gains for ordinary people.

Maeil Business reported separately that the surplus placed South Korea second globally in that quarter. And September export data underlines the momentum: exports hit a record 121.1 billion dollars for the month, with semiconductors again the primary driver.

Deputy Prime Minister and Finance Minister Lee Hyeong-il held a consultation meeting on October 25 to discuss tax credit reforms for domestic production and three key social policy agendas, a sign the government is already thinking about how to sustain the trend beyond exports.

Analysts cited in the reporting flag domestic demand recovery as the next variable to watch. Export records are one thing; whether that strength flows through to household incomes is the question President Lee himself is asking.

The number to remember: 121.1 billion dollars in exports last month, a monthly record.

Third, the justice minister shortlist.

A presidential office source told Yonhap that President Lee Jae-myung is now reviewing two Democratic Party lawmakers as candidates to lead the Ministry of Justice: Park Kyoon-taek and Yang Bu-nam. Both are former prosecutors who moved into politics. A formal nomination is expected soon, once vetting is complete.

This seat has been empty since last month, when Lee's previous nominee, Representative Kim Seung-won, withdrew after lobbying allegations surfaced during his parliamentary confirmation hearing.

The opposition People Power Party is already pushing back. Na Kyung-won and other People Power Party figures have accused Lee of looking for a justice minister who would move to drop the legal charges Lee himself faces.

The national audit, meanwhile, enters its final week with the probe into Chief Justice Cho Hui-dae still live. And People Power Party lawmaker Ju Jin-woo this week alleged that handwritten tally sheets from 41 polling stations in the 22nd general election showed results different from the National Election Commission's electronic records, calling for an investigation, adding one more volatile thread to an already charged political environment.

The nomination, when it comes, will land straight into that atmosphere.

Now to Pyongyang.

KCNA led its coverage with Kim's Dandong visit, framing the "blood alliance" with China around the 76th Korean War anniversary, that story is the first lead today. The second major KCNA report covers a different track running simultaneously: Kim chaired the Third Meeting of Company Leaders of the Korean People's Army in Pyongyang on October 22 and 23, calling for a "military revolution" and demanding the training of, in state media's words, the world's strongest military units. KCNA also carried a routine agricultural update, reporting that rice harvesting has been largely completed across North Korea.

Two tracks at once, a diplomatic signal to Beijing and a military mobilization message at home.

The rest of the news, quickly.

At the demilitarized zone, tensions are not easing. North Korean forces conducted artillery drills at coastal border areas, Yonhap reported. Separately, South Korean military investigators and the U.N. Command examining the site of a recent DMZ mine explosion recovered one North Korean-origin mine. And at the United Nations, North Korea's mission asked publicly whether South Korea was developing its own nuclear weapons, escalating the two sides' verbal confrontation at the U.N. over the mine blasts and broader inter-Korean military tensions.

The U.N. Command's deputy commander, Lieutenant General Scott Winter, visited Oslo on October 25 and met with Lieutenant General Ingrid Gjerde, Chief of Defence Norway, part of ongoing engagement with U.N. Command member states.

The Defense prisoner of war and MIA Accounting Agency has officially accounted for U.S. Army Pvt. Joseph E., a Korean War missing servicemember. The agency described it as a sacred promise kept.

On the economy, Seoul apartment prices rose 0.61 percent in October, according to KB Real Estate data, the slowest monthly increase of the year, as government tightening measures temper gains. Household loans at South Korea's five largest banks grew by 2.6 trillion won in October, a trend the Bank of Korea has flagged as a financial stability risk.

The presidential office is leaning toward a two-year deferral on cryptocurrency taxation, with ruling party and government deliberations still ongoing.

The weekly president-prime minister lunch meeting is shifting from Monday to Tuesday this week, aligned with the Tuesday cabinet session.

South Korea's Defense Acquisition Program Administration is reviewing whether to revoke defense contractor LIG D and A's designation following a bribery investigation. That would block the firm from new drone procurement contracts.

In polling taken October 13 to 15, Gallup Korea put President Lee's approval at 39 percent, trending up. The Democratic Party stood at 36 percent, the People Power Party at 27 percent, and independents at 29 percent, trending down.

The International Institute for Strategic Studies published a piece this week arguing that Canada's submarine procurement decision opens a concrete opportunity for South Korea to expand defense-industrial cooperation with Ottawa. A Korea Institute for Defense Analyses paper separately examines structural reinforcements for South Korea's three-axis deterrence system amid escalating North Korean threats.

On Capitol Hill.

The Hill covered Kim Jong Un's October 24 Dandong visit, focusing on the inauguration of the New Yalu River Bridge and its significance for North Korea-China trade ties and regional diplomacy. No Korea-related hearings are listed on the Hill calendar this week.

Beyond the region.

Uzbekistan's Ambassador to Korea, Alisher Abdusalomov, said at a reception marking his country's 35th independence day that Korean investment in Uzbekistan has surpassed approximately 8 billion dollars, with more than 700 Korean companies operating there. A UPI analysis this week asks whether the United States and its allies have a coherent strategic policy that accounts for the interplay between North Korea and Iran, both of which face U.S.-led sanctions regimes and have been linked to weapons-transfer discussions.

Finally, the markets.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed at about 5,550 on October 23, up about 0.3 percent. Brent crude stood at about 111.70 dollars a barrel, down about 1.3 percent. The won traded at about 1,325 to the dollar as of today. The Bank of Korea's policy rate stands at 2.50 percent, last moved in October.
