Trump flew home from the APEC summit without the Kim meeting he had raised publicly, leaving the diplomatic track undefined.

First, the no-show in Shenzhen.

President Donald Trump flew back to Washington on November 20 after the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen without meeting North Korean leader Kim Jong Un. Trump had raised the prospect publicly during the trip. Speaking to NBC News on his way home, Trump said: "The relationship is there, but we didn't meet. There were too many meetings. It wouldn't have worked out. We'll have that meeting at some point."

Secretary of State Marco Rubio had said days earlier that Trump was "serious" about engaging with Kim. The Kremlin had also indicated that Putin would meet Kim if he attended APEC. Kim did not appear in Shenzhen at all.

At the same summit, President Lee Jae-myung used the APEC platform to appeal for multilateral support for resuming talks with North Korea, framing engagement as a regional stability imperative. The Diplomat reports Lee made explicit appeals to fellow leaders to back renewed inter-Korean and U.S.-North Korea dialogue.

Also at APEC, Trump, Xi, and Putin held a first-ever trilateral summit and pledged nuclear nonproliferation cooperation. The joint declaration, however, does not mention North Korean nuclear weapons by name.

The bottom line: Trump is home, Kim didn't show, and no meeting date has been set. The diplomatic track remains undefined.

Second, South Korea's ruling party moves against the chief justice.

The ruling Democratic Party voted at a general caucus meeting on November 20 to pursue the impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae. The vote came one day after the Supreme Court overturned former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo's 15-year insurrection sentence and sent the case back to the Seoul High Court.

The party cited three grounds: Cho's handling of the martial law declaration, his earlier decision to send President Lee Jae-myung's election law case back to appeals court, and allegations of improper use of official funds. The Democratic Party holds 161 of 300 National Assembly seats, enough to pass an impeachment motion without coalition partners. The party plans to introduce the motion before year-end.

Separately, the full Assembly passed the Democracy Meritorious Persons Act on November 20 in a ruling-party-led vote, while the People Power Party boycotted the session, calling it a "privilege bill for the pro-democracy movement faction." The bill provides medical and welfare support to victims and bereaved families of pro-democracy movements. It had failed to pass for 26 years.

A Gallup Korea poll taken November 3 to 5 showed presidential approval at 41 percent, up. The Democratic Party stood at 36 percent, also up, while the People Power Party was at 28 percent, down.

The push to impeach the chief justice is the story to watch as the Assembly heads toward year-end.

Third, Yongbyon and the nuclear watchdog's warning.

The International Atomic Energy Agency reported to its Board of Governors on November 19 that North Korea has completed external construction on an extension to its original uranium enrichment facility at Yongbyon, voicing what it called "serious concern" over the upgrades. The report is the latest in a series documenting the facility's expansion.

Satellite imagery tracked by the BP Locations tracker recorded the enrichment building expansion as complete, with a new centrifuge hall operational. Daily N.K. separately reported that imagery of the Pyongsan uranium refinery shows a new chemical storage tank added alongside a freight train arrival and apparent wastewater discharge toward the Ryesong River.

The same International Atomic Energy Agency Board of Governors session brought a different finding on Seoul's side. The agency said it was "satisfied" with South Korea's cooperation on its nuclear-powered submarine development program and maintained its conclusion that all nuclear material in South Korea is being used for peaceful purposes. This is the International Atomic Energy Agency's first report on that program since President Lee and President Trump announced the project.

The Yongbyon expansion is complete on the outside. What matters next is what is happening inside.

Now to Pyongyang.

State media led today with Kim Jong Un's inspection of Kim Jong Suk Naval University and east-coast construction sites on November 19. Kim criticized what he called hostile naval activities by adversaries. At the university, Kim stated: "The university's primary duty is to train professional naval commanders." That is how state media framed a visit that also touched on construction progress on the east coast.

The second top article covers diplomacy with Laos. The North Korean premier met a Lao government delegation, while senior official Ju Chang Il separately received a Lao People's Revolutionary Party delegation that also visited the Kumsusan Palace of the Sun.

The brief's bottom line: state media also highlighted North Korea's display of a domestically developed AI chatbot at a national information technology exhibition, marking a new public step in the country's technology signaling.

The rest of the news, quickly.

South Korea crossed one trillion dollars in annual exports, becoming the fourth country globally to reach that milestone, after the United States, China, and Germany. The Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy cited semiconductors, cosmetics, and food as key contributors. That achievement came 19 years after China reached the same threshold.

South Korean markets did not celebrate. Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, fell about 5 percent on November 20, driven by investor concern over potential U.S. tightening of semiconductor export restrictions on China. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix were among the hardest-hit.

The Bank of Korea's final Monetary Policy Committee meeting of 2026 is scheduled for November 26. Market consensus points to a rate increase from 3.00 percent to 3.25 percent, which would be the first back-to-back hike at consecutive meetings in the bank's history.

Kakao founder Kim Beom-soo was acquitted on appeal over stock price manipulation charges tied to the 2023 SM Entertainment acquisition battle. An appeals court ruled that "artificial manipulation was unproven," overturning the lower court's finding.

South Korea's Ministry of Land decided to hold the housing appraisal-to-market-value ratio at 69 percent for 2027, unchanged from 2026. Areas where property prices have risen will still see higher appraisal values and heavier property taxes as a result.

President Lee Jae-myung approved Kim Jong-chul as commissioner general of the Korean National Police Agency on November 20, ending a vacancy of nearly two years. The post had been empty since the former police chief was suspended in December 2024 in connection with former President Yoon Suk Yeol's failed martial law bid.

On the AI cheating crisis: the Prime Minister chaired the first meeting of the Suneung Cheating Response Task Force on November 20, the day after 111 examinees were caught with smart devices at the college entrance exam. The task force said it will analyze suspicious Wi-Fi and Bluetooth signals recorded on exam day and, if necessary, request police investigations. Separately, the Air Force Military Academy expelled a third-year cadet who covertly photographed exam questions with a smartphone, the first publicly confirmed military academy dismissal for a digital exam violation.

Unification Minister Chung Dong-young met U.S. Ambassador Steel and General Brunson of U.S. Forces Korea for the first time on November 20. General Brunson posted from Panmunjom: "Standing at Panmunjom, you see why the Armistice matters. It has kept the peace here for generations, and keeping it takes sustained effort and close coordination with our Republic of Korea partners." Foreign Minister Cho Hyun chaired a second inter-ministerial meeting on overseas expansion of the K-Initiative, with representatives from 11 ministries and agencies.

A petty theft case exposed a North Korean worker at a key Russian drone production facility, confirming North Korean labor presence at a sensitive Russian defense site.

A RAND study based on interviews in Washington, Tokyo, and Seoul finds that U.S.-Japan-South Korea trilateral cooperation is effective in peacetime and crisis management but constrained by legal and political barriers in wartime. An Atlantic Council study called Guardian Tiger III, drawing on engagements with U.S. and allied government, military, and expert stakeholders, warns that the risk of a limited North Korean nuclear attack will likely increase over the coming decade and that allies are insufficiently prepared.

Beyond the region.

South Korean defense firms are shifting in the Middle East from pure arms sales toward production partnerships and technology cooperation agreements. North Korea and Laos signed a protocol on a joint economic committee at the Mekong-South Korea summit, where Laos also called for stronger digital and green cooperation with Seoul. Uzbekistan's Uzcosmos signed a space cooperation agreement with South Korea's Nara Space.

Finally, the markets.

The won was trading at about 1,339 to the dollar on November 20, up about 0.67 percent. The KOSPI closed at about 4,870, down about 5 percent. Brent crude was near 89 dollars a barrel, down about 3 percent.
