0:00Today's headlines

This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Saturday, November 7.

Three things today: South Korea's stock market takes its worst single-day hit in years, North Korea expands its warship construction to a third shipyard, and President Lee Jae-myung names a new justice minister nominee. Also today, the Labor Inspector Act is set to take effect December 8, and the Combined Forces Command marks its 48th anniversary.

0:34KOSPI plunges 6% as Democratic midterm sweep triggers AI selloff

First, the markets. South Korea's main stock index, the KOSPI, fell 6.02 percent on November 6, its steepest single-day drop in recent memory. The index briefly broke through 5,000 points intraday. The trigger was Democratic Party victories in gubernatorial races in Texas, Ohio, and Georgia. Those results sparked fears that a Democratic-controlled Congress will tighten regulation of artificial intelligence data centers, which would dampen demand for semiconductors, the backbone of South Korea's export economy. Taiwan and Japan equity markets fell in the same session, reflecting their shared exposure to AI infrastructure investment.

The won weakened too, with the dollar buying about 1,356 won by the close. The Bank of Korea's policy rate stands at 3.00 percent following its October cut, and consumer price inflation remained above the bank's 2 percent target in October, adding to the pressure on policymakers.

Foreign direct investment figures, released through the third quarter of this year, offered a counterpoint: inbound investment notifications reached 22.9 billion dollars, up 10.8 percent year-on-year, with actual inflows of 14.9 billion dollars, up 30.6 percent. That suggests foreign investors had confidence in South Korea even before Wednesday's selloff. The question now is whether the KOSPI's collapse changes that calculation.

2:04Kim Jong Un orders Nampho Shipyard expansion as warship construction…

Second, North Korea's navy. Kim Jong Un inspected Nampho Shipyard and ordered the strengthening of both surface and underwater naval capabilities, alongside modernization of the facility. Speculation is mounting, according to N.K. News, that a vessel under construction there may exceed 10,000 tons. And crucially, North Korea has now expanded warship construction to a third shipyard, beyond Nampho and Sinpo.

That expansion comes in the same week North Korea conducted four weapons tests. Yonhap reports that state media carried no domestic reporting on those tests, no coverage of the surface-to-air missile test, the cruise missile drill, or the ballistic launches. The domestic blackout is itself a signal: the tests appear aimed at external audiences, not at rallying the North Korean public.

On the diplomatic side, Workers' Party of Korea International Secretary Kim Sung-nam held a third consecutive high-level meeting in Beijing, this one with Politburo Standing Committee member Cai Qi, to discuss party-to-party exchanges and strategic communication. Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi also met a senior North Korean official and vowed to deepen bilateral ties. Reuters reports that the Trump administration may seek a Pyongyang opening following the midterms, but analysts say Kim Jong Un is unlikely to respond quickly, given Kim Yo Jong's conditional dialogue offer and the pace of North Korea's own weapons activity.

3:34Lee names Lee Yong-cheol justice minister nominee, plus tax and…

Third, South Korean politics. President Lee Jae-myung has nominated Lee Yong-cheol, 66, as justice minister candidate, 48 days after the previous nominee withdrew. Lee Yong-cheol currently heads the Defense Acquisition Program Administration and previously served as a legal aide under the Roh Moo-hyun administration. The People Power Party immediately demanded the nominations be withdrawn, citing Lee Yong-cheol's ties to what it calls the Seongnam circle and alleged connections to the Daejangdong case. The Democratic Party rejected those claims as false.

The justice minister nomination landed in the middle of an already volatile political week. The Democratic Party-led National Assembly passed a special counsel bill targeting alleged prosecutorial manipulation of criminal charges. The ruling People Power Party calls it Lee Jae-myung's prison escape law. People Power Party leader Jang Dong-hyuk and more than 70 lawmakers rallied at Gyeongbokgung station in protest. Jang called presidential impeachment the answer. Legal scholars have raised constitutional concerns about the bill's provisions.

President Lee also nominated Ahn Deok-su, 55, chief of the Seoul Regional Tax Office, to head the National Tax Service, and named Kim Jong-cheol, 56, as the next Commissioner General of the Korean National Police Agency. The police agency had been without a permanent chief for 23 months, operating under acting leadership since the December 3 martial law declaration.

5:06Pyongyang

Now to Pyongyang. State media confirmed two items today. First, a plenary meeting of the Supreme People's Assembly Standing Committee was held, a routine legislative session that followed Kim's military inspection activities. Second, KCNA reported Kim Sung-nam's meeting in Beijing with the head of the CPC Central Committee's Office of Foreign Affairs Commission, his second high-level contact of the visit, with the third, the meeting with Cai Qi, confirmed separately by Chosunbiz. Kim's naval inspection at Nampho, already covered in the second lead, was the other major activity. The bottom line: state media confirmed the legislative session and the Beijing diplomacy while maintaining a complete domestic blackout on the week's four weapons tests.

5:51The round-up

The rest of the news, quickly.

The U.N. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres called for the resumption of inter-Korean communication channels, including military-to-military contacts, citing concerns over potential escalation along the Demilitarized Zone.

South Korea's nuclear-powered submarine program, designated Jangbogo-N, has been exempted from a mandatory business feasibility review. That allows formal development to begin next year, with an operational target in the late 2030s. Experts say the program depends partly on U.S. congressional authorization for nuclear propulsion technology transfer.

A senior U.S. Navy official visited South Korea following the midterms, in what SBS describes exclusively as the first visible movement in the Maritime and Shipbuilding Growth Alliance shipbuilding cooperation project since concerns arose after the election. Officials identified include Brendan Rogers and Andrew Nibell. HD Hyundai Heavy Industries and Hanwha Ocean were among the Korean counterparts.

Defense Minister Kang Shin-cheol attended the 13th ASEAN Defense Ministers' Meeting Plus, engaging with counterparts on maritime security and humanitarian assistance.

South Korea's Defense Ministry acknowledged a Civil Control Line security breach and vowed corrective measures, stating the incident should not be characterized as a broader failure of forward security posture.

North Korea told the European Union to mind its own business, rejecting the European Union's human rights resolution as interference in internal affairs.

North Korea also issued a statement criticizing Japan's plan to increase defense spending, characterizing it as a burden on the Japanese people.

South Korea's trade minister met with Korean content companies to explore strategies for entering the Chinese market, in a continued government effort to restore cultural and commercial access to China.

Korea Hydro and Nuclear Power plans hydrogen cooperation through its Czech nuclear partnership, with an eye on the European hydrogen market.

A Gallup Korea poll taken October 27 to 29 put President Lee's approval rating at 39 percent. The Democratic Party stood at 35 percent, down. The People Power Party was at 30 percent, up. Independents were at 28 percent, down.

The Sejong Institute assesses that North Korea's post-9th Congress economic strategy targets what it calls stabilization and gradual qualitative development through 2030, with 2026 to 2030 designated as a consolidation phase combining state planning with enterprise-level production.

8:30Capitol Hill

On Capitol Hill. Policy experts told the Korea Times that South Korea must build independent communication channels with the now-Democratic Congress on issues including nuclear submarine technology transfer and tariffs. Analysts assessed by KBS say the Democratic congressional takeover affects South Korea's nuclear-powered submarine program authorization prospects, with new committee chairs expected to shift legislative priorities affecting the alliance.

9:00Korea and the World

Beyond the region. A South Korean company's artificial intelligence artillery system trials in Ukraine were halted due to a bilateral dispute. The Korean chief executive separately denied a report that Ukraine had dropped the system entirely. The episode follows Seoul's cautious positioning on the Russia-Ukraine conflict. Separately, Lockheed Martin's Sikorsky division will supply S-92A-plus helicopters to serve as South Korea's presidential transport fleet.

9:30Markets

Finally, the markets. Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed at about 5,059, down just over 6 percent on November 6. The dollar bought about 1,356 won. Brent crude was at about 98 dollars a barrel.
