0:00Today's headlines

This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Sunday, November 22.

Three things today: Kim Jong Un snubs Trump's summit offer, South Korea's education minister resigns over AI cheating chaos, and new satellite imagery confirms North Korea is expanding its uranium enrichment plant at Yongbyon.

The Trump administration went all the way to exploring a Korean War end-of-war declaration to get Kim to the table. Kim said no. Tomorrow, Justice Minister nominee Lee Yong-cheol faces a National Assembly confirmation hearing.

0:42Kim Jong Un spurned Trump's meeting offer

First, Kim Jong Un turned down a meeting with President Donald Trump during Trump's Asia trip, and the terms North Korea set made the gap clear.

The Wall Street Journal and Yonhap report that the Trump administration actively pursued a summit and explored multiple inducements, including a declaration formally ending the Korean War. North Korea ultimately presented conditions Washington found unacceptable, and no meeting happened.

At a press conference, Trump said the meeting, quote, "didn't work out," but vowed to, quote, "have that meeting" in the future. North Korea's state media made no mention of the offer at all. Instead, the dominant image out of Pyongyang on the day was Kim inspecting educational factory sites.

U.S. senators told VOA Korean that a summit is unlikely in the near term. That assessment lands in a complicated regional moment: Trump, China's Xi Jinping, and Russia's Vladimir Putin had just held their first trilateral summit in Shenzhen, where Trump said Korean Peninsula dangers were discussed. The CSIS Korea Chair's Capital Cable podcast, with Victor Cha, Sydney Seiler, and host Mark Lippert, unpacks what the three leaders agreed to and what it means for the peninsula.

The bottom line: Kim passed on the meeting, the conditions were unacceptable to Washington, and state media said nothing.

2:06New satellite imagery confirms additional expansion at Yongbyon…

Second, new commercial satellite photographs confirm that North Korea is expanding its uranium enrichment capacity at Yongbyon, and not just in ways analysts had already flagged.

Jeffrey Lewis at ArmsControlWonk analyzed imagery published by the Wall Street Journal. The photos show a small but concrete addition to North Korea's original uranium enrichment plant at Yongbyon. That is separate from a previously reported new centrifuge hall expansion. Both are happening at the same complex.

Lewis's analysis says the imagery corroborates a sustained upward trend in enrichment-related construction that analysts have tracked through 2026. This is not a single new building. It is a pattern.

The expansion is unfolding as the failed Trump-Kim diplomacy described in our lead story plays out, and as Russia has formally abandoned its past positions on North Korean denuclearization and sanctions at the United Nations. The Diplomat reports that Moscow, which once accepted sanctions reluctantly, now opposes them outright.

The picture is of a North Korean nuclear program continuing to grow, with international pressure mechanisms weakening, and diplomacy stalled. The addition to the original enrichment plant at Yongbyon is the new detail you need to hold onto from this story.

3:25Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin resigns over AI cheating chaos ahead…

Third, South Korea's education minister resigned on November 22 over rules that caused widespread confusion before the country's most important university entrance exam.

Choi Kyo-jin stepped down after last-minute anti-AI-cheating rules scrambled preparations for the College Scholastic Ability Test, known as the Suneung, which was held November 19. President Lee Jae-myung formally approved the resignation within one hour of Choi announcing it.

Lee instructed Prime Minister Han Seong-sook to take full responsibility for follow-up measures and ordered reforms to block exam-room photo transmission. On X, Lee wrote that, quote, "the fairness of exams is a principle that cannot be compromised."

The opposition People Power Party leader Jang Dong-hyuk called the resignation insufficient. At the National Assembly member hall on November 22, Jang said, quote, "The most important national exams have become a venue for AI cheating competitions." Jang demanded the government explain the root cause of the failure, not just accept a minister's departure.

The government is separately fast-tracking permits for AI data center construction, easing assessment requirements to accelerate deployment. The two moves together show a government trying to embrace AI infrastructure while struggling to manage AI's effects in the classroom. Choi is out. The policy question is not.

4:52Pyongyang

Now to Pyongyang.

State media's dominant image today was Kim Jong Un personally receiving the credentials of Andrei Leonidovich Podolshev, the new Russian ambassador to North Korea. KCNA described this as the first such direct reception in over 30 years, a notable protocol shift toward Moscow. The failed Trump summit offer was entirely absent from KCNA output.

Daily N.K., which monitors North Korean internal conditions, reported two domestic stories today. One described two North Korean police officers getting into a public shouting match outside Hyesan Station over a trader lacking a travel permit, with a crowd forming and a railway bribery network exposed. The other reported that Kangdong County Hospital, approaching its first anniversary, is functioning better than comparable facilities, though supply and staffing gaps remain.

The bottom line from KCNA: Kim is visibly aligning with Moscow while ignoring Washington's overture entirely.

5:55The round-up

The rest of the news, quickly.

The Democratic Party is moving to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae before the end of December. Representative Yang Bu-nam says the party's task force has outlined grounds and is seeking a vote by then.

South Korea's stock market had a rough week. Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed November 20 down about 5 percent, falling below the 5,000 level after a global AI-sector selloff triggered by a rout in NVIDIA's stock. Circuit breakers were activated. Deputy Prime Minister Lee Hyung-il convened an emergency meeting that afternoon with Financial Services Commission Chairman Lee Eok-won, Financial Supervisory Service Director Lee Chan-jin, and Bank of Korea Governor Shin Hyun-song. The government is preparing to activate a market stabilization fund and ease daily share buyback limits for three months starting November 24.

South Korea's car trade deficit with China hit a record 4.23 billion dollars in the first ten months of 2026, driven by a surge in China-made car imports while Korean exports to China fell.

Unification Minister Chung Dong-young visited Panmunjom alongside U.N. Command Commander General Xavier Brunson, days after Chung met the new U.S. ambassador. The joint visit follows reported turbulence in the U.S.-South Korea alliance.

A Japanese survey found 62 percent of respondents feel affinity toward South Korea, a record high, while 93 percent described China-Japan relations as poor. The Gallup Korea poll taken November 3 to 5 put President Lee's approval at 41 percent, with the Democratic Party at 36 percent and the People Power Party at 28 percent. Gallup also found that 47 percent of respondents cited the economy as the most important issue heading into future elections.

The National Assembly passed the Democratic Merit Persons Act, extending medical, care, and welfare support to 635 recognized democracy movement figures. North Korea has postponed its 2027 Pyongyang International Marathon from April to October, shifting away from the traditional April 15 date. Looking ahead, the Justice Minister nominee confirmation hearing is November 24, and the ruling party's special committee on retirement-age extension faces a deadline on November 27.

8:18Capitol Hill

On Capitol Hill.

U.S. senators told VOA Korean that a Trump-Kim summit is unlikely in the near term, following Kim's refusal to meet Trump during the Asia trip. On the alliance more broadly, Asia Times reports that the midterm Democratic wave brought new members sympathetic to the U.S.-South Korea alliance into Congress, though both governments have acknowledged turbulence in the relationship, with structural tensions over defense spending targets persisting.

8:47Korea and the World

Beyond the region.

South Korea is transferring K9 Thunder howitzer expertise to Poland, deepening the defense export relationship that has made Poland Seoul's largest European arms customer. Saudi Arabia is diversifying its air-defense procurement to include South Korean suppliers amid ongoing drone and missile threats. And a South Korean consortium has secured the design contract for Vietnam's high-speed rail project, extending Seoul's overseas infrastructure push.

9:18Markets

Finally, the markets.

The won was trading at about 1,338 to the dollar on November 22. The KOSPI closed November 20 at 4,869.96, down about 5 percent, with Brent crude also down about 2 percent that day. The Bank of Korea's policy rate stands at 3 percent, last set in October.
