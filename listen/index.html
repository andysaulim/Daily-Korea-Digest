0:00Today's headlines

This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Tuesday, November 24.

Three things today: Russian warplanes trigger an air raid alert near Ulleung Island, Kim Yo Jong threatens to shut down Switzerland's newly opened Pyongyang mission, and a massive KOSPI rebound on news about Nvidia's CEO. Five Russian warplanes approached close enough to South Korean territory that the military issued a live air raid alert, and the government sent conflicting emergency messages to the public. Tomorrow, three Seoul Metro labor unions plan an indefinite general strike from the first trains, after final talks broke down today.

0:46Russian warplanes approach Ulleung Island, triggering air raid alert…

First, the Russian warplanes.

About five Russian aircraft entered South Korea's air defense identification zone over the East Sea early Tuesday and flew close enough to Ulleung Island that the Joint Chiefs of Staff pre-emptively issued an air raid alert and scrambled fighter jets. No airspace violation was confirmed, but the Joint Chiefs said the aircraft, quote, "approached right up to the edge of violating territorial airspace."

What followed made a tense situation worse. The interior ministry's emergency alert incorrectly told the public that an airspace violation had occurred. Then North Gyeongsang Province's cancellation notice compounded the error by describing the aircraft as suspected North Korean planes. Two separate factual mistakes, sent to millions of people, in the span of one incident.

The Defense Ministry summoned Russian Embassy military attaché Nikolai Marchenko to lodge a formal protest. Spokesperson Chung Binna confirmed the summoning. SBS reported this was the third time Russian aircraft have entered the zone this month alone.

For Korea policy watchers, the pattern is what matters. Three intrusions in November. And the government's response, however forceful diplomatically, was accompanied by public communications that got basic facts wrong. That's the story: the warplanes approached the edge of South Korean airspace, the military scrambled jets, and the public was misinformed about what happened.

2:16Kim Yo Jong threatens to force closure of Switzerland's newly opened…

Second, Kim Yo Jong's threat against Switzerland.

Kim Yo Jong, deputy director of the Workers' Party of Korea's Propaganda and Agitation Department, issued a statement Tuesday through state media saying she would urge North Korea to consider withdrawing the Swiss diplomatic office in Pyongyang. The office had opened only weeks earlier.

The trigger: Switzerland sanctioned a North Korean children's camp that had hosted Russian students. Kim called the move, quote, "an open hostile act" and "a serious infringement upon the sovereignty of North Korea."

The timing is striking. Pyongyang had recently issued a similar warning to the United Kingdom over separate sanctions, and the brief notes a pattern: North Korea is using newly opened diplomatic channels as leverage rather than goodwill gestures. Switzerland opening a mission in Pyongyang was itself significant. Now that opening is being treated as something Pyongyang can threaten to revoke.

State media's dominant output Tuesday was this threat. It is, as the brief notes, the first such coercive diplomatic move against a European neutral since Switzerland opened the office.

Watch whether Bern responds, and whether the Swiss mission stays open. The threat may be leverage rather than a final decision. But the message from Pyongyang is clear enough: engagement is conditional, and conditions change.

3:40KOSPI surges 6.32% to reclaim 5,000 as Jensen Huang returns to Nvidia

Third, the KOSPI surge.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, gained 297 points Tuesday, or about 6.3 percent, to close at just over 5,006. That reclaims the 5,000-point mark for the first time in three sessions. The Korea Exchange activated a buy-side sidecar at 9:06 in the morning, suspending program-trading buy orders for five minutes. It was the first such activation since November 10.

The driver: Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president and CEO. Huang had stepped down on November 12 after reports of an extramarital relationship with a woman suspected of links to Chinese intelligence. The board said it consulted senior U.S. government officials before concluding his reinstatement posed no national security risk.

South Korean chipmakers surged because they are directly tied to Nvidia's supply chain. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix both supply high-bandwidth memory for Nvidia's AI processors, and Nvidia has pledged to supply 260,000 graphics processing units to South Korea. When Huang's future at Nvidia looked uncertain, so did that relationship. His return reversed the sell-off.

The bottom line: a single U.S. corporate governance decision moved South Korea's stock market by more than six percent in one session.

5:05Pyongyang

Now to Pyongyang.

State media carried Kim Jong Un's speech at a credentials ceremony, in which he defended North Korea's military buildup as self-defensive and said Pyongyang seeks neither hegemony nor regional expansion. Kim said, quote, "The security situation in North Korea and the political and military environment in East Asia urgently demand that we provide for ourselves the power and capability." That framing, arms as necessity not ambition, was KCNA's secondary story Tuesday.

The dominant output was Kim Yo Jong's threat against Switzerland, already covered in the leads, which the brief describes as the first coercive diplomatic move against a European neutral since the Swiss mission opened.

Kim Jong Un has not made a public appearance today. His last appearance was one day ago.

5:56The round-up

The rest of the news, quickly.

The U.S. Department of Justice filed a civil forfeiture complaint Monday seeking to recover 1.17 billion dollars in USD Coin stolen by North Korean cyber actors from Binance. The complaint was filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia. Of that total, 823 million dollars was held in a single virtual wallet address. The theft occurred in October and was attributed to North Korea-linked hackers.

On the cabinet reshuffle announced Monday, an N.K. News analysis by Jeongmin Kim argues that the joint exit of Unification Minister Chung Dong-young and National Intelligence Service Director Lee Jong-seok, both leading figures of what the analysis calls the pro-autonomy camp, is the signal to Washington. The incoming unification minister nominee, Hong Ihk-pyo, is a former progressive lawmaker. The analysis says his nomination does not mean Seoul is abandoning its engagement-first approach to Pyongyang.

The Cabinet met Tuesday, chaired by President Lee Jae-myung, and approved a plan to relocate 31 agencies employing 5,460 public servants to Sejong by 2031 at an estimated cost of about 1.65 trillion won.

Justice minister nominee Lee Yong-chul appeared before the National Assembly's Legislation and Judiciary Committee for his confirmation hearing. He declined to commit to directing prosecutors to drop the 12 pending cases against President Lee, saying he would not "excessively exercise" prosecutorial authority.

The Education Ministry activated a dedicated AI cheating response team Tuesday, announcing radio-frequency detectors, mobile cameras, and supervisor body cameras for national qualification exams. President Lee ordered a cross-government investigation. The government says penalties for all 111 alleged cheating cases from the November 19 college entrance exam will be announced before scores are released on December 11.

Satellite imagery analyzed by SI Analytics shows what appears to be a new luxury barge at a private pier in Wonsan used by Kim Jong Un's family, the Korea Herald reported.

In an overnight item, N.K. News analysis found that at the APEC summit, the Trump team considered North Korea's conditions for engagement excessively high, while Pyongyang found the U.S. offer insufficient.

A Gallup Korea poll taken November 3 to 5 put President Lee's approval rating at 41 percent. The Democratic Party stood at 36 percent, the People Power Party at 28 percent.

8:34Korea and the World

Beyond the region.

Saudi Arabia has entered a partnership with South Korea and Ukraine to boost its air defenses, with a combined value reported at 3 billion dollars, as the Kingdom faces continued attacks from Iran and Houthi forces.

South Korea's nuclear project consortium reaffirmed its commitment to completing the Czech Dukovany nuclear plant on schedule, pledging to, quote, "restore trust as one team," despite an ongoing construction contractor dispute.

9:05Markets

Finally, the markets.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed at just over 5,006, up about 6.3 percent. The dollar bought about 1,343 won, down slightly on the day. Brent crude stood at 87 dollars a barrel, down 2.65 percent.
