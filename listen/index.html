Transcript

0:00Today's headlines

This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Thursday, October 29.

Three things today: North Korea fires roughly ten ballistic missiles hours after Seoul called for dialogue; South Korea's spy agency says Kim Jong Un's China trip was about signaling on U.S. talks; and prosecutors seek the death penalty for former President Yoon Suk Yeol.

North Korea's salvo is the sharpest answer yet to a public dialogue offer from Seoul's defense minister, made just days ago. Today is also the fourth anniversary of the Itaewon disaster, marked this morning by President Lee Jae-myung at a commemoration in Seoul.

0:46North Korea fires ~10 short-range ballistic missiles from Wonsan…

First, the missiles.

North Korea launched approximately ten short-range ballistic missiles from the Wonsan area toward the East Sea at around twelve-thirty this afternoon, Korea Standard Time. South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff announced the launches, and the U.S. and South Korea are conducting joint precision analysis. The missiles flew approximately 250 kilometers.

The timing is striking. Just days ago, Defense Minister Kang Shin-chul stood at the ASEAN Defence Ministers' Meeting Plus in the Philippines and publicly called on Pyongyang to return to dialogue. That was Kang's first multilateral security forum since taking office. North Korea's answer was a ten-missile salvo.

Also at that forum in the Philippines, U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth held bilateral talks with Kang, with both sides emphasizing the alliance is built on peace through strength. General Xavier Brunson, the commander of U.S. Forces Korea, was in Seoul today laying a wreath at the War Memorial of Korea, honoring troops from the 1950 Chosin battle. He posted today that their courage helped forge the alliance, and that their sacrifice endures and the commitment must too.

Hours after the salvo, President Lee Jae-myung pledged to reinforce the U.S.-South Korea alliance at a Korean War battle ceremony.

The bottom line: roughly ten short-range ballistic missiles, fired from Wonsan, on the same day Seoul's top military ally honored a shared sacrifice and days after Seoul asked Pyongyang to talk.

2:21NIS: Kim Jong Un may have used Dandong visit to convey Pyongyang's…

Second, Kim Jong Un's China trip and what it may have been about.

South Korea's National Intelligence Service told lawmakers at a closed-door intelligence committee audit that Kim Jong Un may have used his October 24 visit to Dandong, China to convey Pyongyang's position on potential talks with the United States.

The National Intelligence Service pointed to who traveled with Kim. His delegation included Foreign Minister Choe Son-hui and other senior diplomats, not military officials. That composition, the National Intelligence Service argued, suggests the trip had a diplomatic purpose beyond commemorating China's Korean War anniversary participation.

The National Intelligence Service added one cautious note: China could relay Kim's position to Washington, but does not appear to have the will to act as a broker.

CSIS published an analysis today reaching a similar conclusion. It argues that Kim's use of the newly opened New Yalu River Bridge for a same-day crossing, accompanied by diplomats rather than generals, points to a U.S.-engagement agenda rather than simple alliance reaffirmation with Beijing.

38 North analyst Rachel Minyoung Lee also weighed in, noting Kim's October 24 trip was unusual and diverges from the pattern of extended visits that preceded past diplomatic breakthroughs.

Democratic Party Representative Yoon Kun-young and People Power Party Representative Yoo Yeong-ha both confirmed the National Intelligence Service assessment on the Dandong visit at the National Assembly intelligence audit. The National Intelligence Service also told lawmakers that reports of North Korea testing loudspeakers aimed at the South were false: a border-area resident made an erroneous report, and Paju City officials said they could not determine what had happened.

The key question for policymakers: if Kim used Dandong to signal on U.S. talks, and Beijing won't broker, who carries that signal forward?

4:19Prosecutors seek death penalty for former President Yoon Suk Yeol…

Third, the Yoon Suk Yeol trial.

South Korean prosecutors have requested the death penalty for former President Yoon Suk Yeol in connection with his December 3 declaration of martial law. That is the most severe penalty the prosecution has sought in the case. Yoon was impeached and removed from office after the failed declaration. The trial is ongoing in Seoul.

Separately, the National Intelligence Service announced today that a special audit found approximately ten National Intelligence Service staff members followed unlawful orders from former National Intelligence Service Director Jo Tae-yong and other political appointees on the day of the martial law declaration. Those staff members will face disciplinary action.

The National Assembly's Operations Committee also saw disruption today. A secretariat audit broke down over the participation of Kim Tae-gyu, with the Democratic Party demanding Kim apologize for denying that Yoon and former President Chun Doo-hwan had staged a coup. The session ended before any agreement was reached.

The death penalty request is the sharpest legal escalation yet in the martial law case.

5:29Pyongyang

Now to Pyongyang.

State media today covered two items worth your attention. First, 38 North reports that satellite imagery shows North Korea continuing to install new fencing along its border, extending a fortification trend documented throughout this year. Second, Daily N.K. reports that journalists at the provincial party newspaper in Ryanggang province have been pulled from reporting duties to attend mandatory ideological sessions marking the journalists' union's 80th anniversary, with loyalty pledges consuming more time than normal work.

As of this digest, state media had not reported on today's missile launches. Kim Jong Un has not appeared publicly in four days. The bottom line from the brief: ten missiles out of Wonsan, silence from Pyongyang's state channels.

6:18The round-up

The rest of the news, quickly.

On North Korea's overseas labor networks, two investigations published today expose the depth of the Russia-North Korea pipeline. A report links North Korean workers to Russia's military-industrial supply chain, with employment disguised as education to evade sanctions. The Financial Times found Russian firms and individuals advertising North Korean labor online to Russian factories.

The U.S. Treasury designated eight North Koreans and two Russians for running an information technology worker fraud scheme that generated revenue for North Korea's weapons programs.

South Korea's Korea Hydro and Nuclear Power says it will not delay the 25 trillion won Dukovany nuclear project in the Czech Republic, even after its construction consortium talks collapsed. The company is seeking new builders.

The U.S.-South Korea annual Security Consultative Meeting has been deferred to early 2027. A Lowy Institute analysis published today says the delay sets back Seoul's timeline for wartime operational control transfer, and argues that even if the transfer happens, Seoul would not gain a say over how U.S. forces are used beyond the Korean Peninsula.

South Korea's communications regulator has fined Google and Apple over app payment practices. A Seoul court rejected Tesla owners' lawsuit seeking refunds for delayed Full Self-Driving features. And South Korea's financial sector was hit by up to 4.4 million cyber scanning attempts in a single day, traced to North Korean-linked addresses.

On the economy: SK Innovation posted third-quarter operating profit of 3.7 trillion won, up 533 percent year-on-year. A Bank of Korea board member told Reuters the won is moving faster than anticipated, a signal of concern about exchange rate volatility. Federal Reserve Governor Kevin Warsh cited South Korea's semiconductor export performance as a key indicator he monitors for global technology demand.

In polling, Gallup Korea surveyed voters from October 13 to 15: presidential approval stood at 39 percent, the Democratic Party at 36 percent, the People Power Party at 27 percent.

Today President Lee Jae-myung spoke at the fourth anniversary commemoration of the October 29, 2022 Itaewon crowd crush. He said: "There was no state on that day. In the place where the state should have reached out first, the bereaved families had to demand truth and accountability from the state." People Power Party leader Jang Dong-hyuk did not attend.

Foreign Minister Cho Hyun departed for the United States today for a G20-related meeting and bilateral talks with U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio. South Korea's G20 Sherpa also met with Indonesia's counterpart on October 29 to coordinate ahead of the December 14 to 15 Miami summit.

9:14Capitol Hill

On Capitol Hill.

The House introduced legislation to prevent artificial intelligence technology theft, explicitly naming North Korea as a country of concern. The bill would restrict AI-related technology transfers that could benefit designated adversaries. Separately, a report cited in congressional circles states South Korea has spent 363 million dollars lobbying U.S. lawmakers, a figure drawing attention amid broader scrutiny of foreign government lobbying disclosures.

9:46Korea and the World

Beyond the region.

South Korea confirmed it is reviewing military deployment options for the Strait of Hormuz, including aircraft, as part of broader maritime security discussions. On Ukraine, Seoul has not excluded the possibility of severing diplomatic relations if the dispute over North Korean prisoners of war held by Kyiv continues to escalate. South Korea previously withdrew eight diplomatic personnel from Ukraine. The Financial Times also reports that Seoul's diplomatic posture toward Russia has alarmed European allies.

10:21Markets

Finally, the markets.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed down about one and a quarter percent today at 5,937. The won traded at about 1,338 to the dollar, up less than one percent, though a Bank of Korea board member flagged concern about the pace of that move. Brent crude fell about four and a half percent to around 110 dollars a barrel.
