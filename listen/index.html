0:00Today's headlines

This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Thursday, November 26.

Three things today: Kim Jong Un inspects nuclear sites and orders more tactical weapons, the Bank of Korea raises rates for the third time this year, and the Trump administration proposes new tariffs targeting fourteen economies including South Korea. On the first story: Kim made the inspections the same day that North Korea apparently ignored a direct outreach from President Trump at the APEC summit, and that tension goes to the heart of where diplomacy stands right now.

0:41Kim inspects nuclear warheads and enrichment sites, rejects…

First, Kim Jong Un's nuclear inspection tour.

On November 25, Kim visited three sites: a weapons-grade nuclear materials production base, a nuclear-powered equipment production base, and the Nuclear Weapons Institute. State media published photos of him examining warheads, what appear to be naval nuclear reactors, and rows of centrifuge cascades at two uranium enrichment facilities.

From those sites, Kim ordered wider deployment of tactical nuclear weapons across multiple operational domains. He declared North Korea's nuclear status, in his words, "irreversible." His exact phrasing, in a statement carried by KCNA: "The nuclear forces of North Korea have been built up as a powerful treasured sword to protect the sovereignty and dignity of the state and people, and their status will never change."

The timing is striking. N.K. News reports that Trump offered Kim a bilateral meeting at the APEC summit in Shenzhen. Kim ignored the offer. The inspections happened on the same day, and N.K. News separately reported that a ballistic missile engine test occurred during Trump's outreach. North Korea's state media made no mention of either Trump's overture or the missile test.

Kim also explicitly rejected the idea of seeking recognition of North Korea's nuclear status from any country, which signals that Pyongyang is not positioning itself for the kind of arms-control-framed talks that some in Washington have floated.

The bottom line on this story: Kim inspected nuclear warhead and enrichment facilities, ordered broader tactical nuclear deployment, and did all of it while publicly ignoring a direct diplomatic signal from the incoming U.S. president.

2:23Bank of Korea raises rate to 3.25%, third hike this year, as AI boom…

Second, the Bank of Korea raises rates again.

The Bank of Korea raised its benchmark interest rate by 25 basis points to 3.25 percent on November 26. That is the third such increase in 2026. The Bank of Korea governor cited expansion in the artificial intelligence sector and inflationary pressure as the drivers. The central bank also upgraded its growth outlook for 2027. The rate now stands at its highest level in the current cycle.

For Korea policy watchers, the rate decision matters because it reflects an economy performing strongly enough that the central bank is tightening, even as trade pressures from Washington are building. That dynamic is worth keeping in mind as the tariff story develops.

The rate is now 3.25 percent, the third hike this year.

3:14Trump's new Section 301 tariff proposal targets 14 economies but…

Third, the Trump administration's new Section 301 tariff proposal.

The administration has proposed new tariffs under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 against fourteen trading partners over what it calls industrial overcapacity. South Korea is on that list. But a senior U.S. official confirmed the proposed duties on South Korea will not exceed the 15 percent ceiling agreed under last year's bilateral trade deal. Japan, the European Union, and Taiwan also retain their 15 percent caps. China faces the highest rate, at 20 percent on top of earlier tariffs.

The Korea Herald reports that Seoul has a second concern sitting alongside the overcapacity framing: South Korea's pending platform regulatory legislation. Twenty-two House Republicans sent a letter to the U.S. Trade Representative on the same issue, urging scrutiny of Seoul's proposed rules and citing potential harm to U.S. technology companies operating in the Korean market. That letter connects directly to the third story you need to watch from Seoul this week.

The cap holds at 15 percent for South Korea, but digital regulation is now a live irritant in the relationship.

4:28Pyongyang

Now to Pyongyang.

The one article at the top of North Korea's state media today is not about Kim's nuclear inspection, which tells you something about how KCNA is managing its messaging. Daily N.K. reports that residents of Kimchaek in North Hamgyong Province faced continuous labor mobilization throughout 2026, working state construction projects seven days a week and taking on low-paying side jobs to afford food.

As for Kim's inspection, state media led with his visit to the warhead and enrichment facilities and his order for wider tactical nuclear deployment. KCNA said nothing about Trump's APEC outreach or the ballistic missile engine test reported by N.K. News. The Workers' Party cell secretaries conference, attended by senior officials Jo Yong Won, Jong Kyong Thaek, and Ju Chang Il, reinforced ideological control, with Kim warning in a letter to the meeting that hostile forces would escalate their schemes against North Korean youth.

5:28The round-up

The rest of the news, quickly.

North Korea expelled the head of the Swiss diplomatic mission in Pyongyang, declaring Sylvain Astier persona non grata over what Pyongyang described as sanctions related to a children's camp. Switzerland said it "regrets" the decision. The closure removes one of the very few diplomatic presences the country had permitted.

Russia's FSB and North Korea's State Intelligence Bureau signed a classified information pact in March, covering Moscow's highest state secrecy tier. The agreement was posted quietly on a Russian legal portal in April. Separately, Ukraine's military intelligence directorate says North Korea paused deliveries of KN-23 missiles and artillery to Russia amid a dispute that began in October. Russia earlier settled a currency dispute by paying Pyongyang in fuel.

South Korea's Foreign Minister Cho Hyun spoke by phone with Iranian Foreign Minister Seyyed Abbas Araghchi on November 26, explaining that Seoul's planned expansion of the Cheonghae naval unit's mission is aimed at protecting South Korean nationals and economic interests, not participation in the war. Cho requested Iranian attention to the safety of South Korean nationals. His post-call statement described it as the tenth direct communication with Araghchi over approximately one and a half years.

Russian aircraft that entered South Korea's Korea Air Defense Identification Zone on November 24 near Ulleung Island have been identified as Tu-160 supersonic strategic bombers capable of carrying nuclear weapons. Footage released by Russia's Defense Ministry also captured what appeared to be a Su-57 stealth fighter, though Seoul's Defense Ministry denied its presence. Defense experts said the Tu-160 exercise appeared aimed at countering U.S. Pacific forces and establishing access to Arctic routes.

On personnel: President Lee approved confirmation hearing requests for four ministers and the National Intelligence Service director nominee. Former Democratic Party floor leader Hong Ik-pyo has been nominated as Unification Minister and told reporters he will pursue incremental inter-Korean engagement rather than high-level summits. Former Justice Minister Kang Kum-sil is nominated to lead the National Intelligence Service. Democratic Party Representative Han Jun-ho is nominated as Interior Minister. The National Assembly must complete the hearings within 20 days.

The Democratic Party clarified that its impeachment push targets only Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, responding to collective pushback from other judges. The party is building civil society support for the drive.

The Supreme Court on November 26 upheld the acquittal of former President Yoon Suk Yeol on charges he perjured himself during the martial law trial of former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo.

On the economy, Korean consumer goods exports reached a record 28.65 billion dollars from January 1 through November 19, up 21.5 percent year on year. The United States surpassed China as the top market for Korean consumer goods for the first time since 2004. A new Korea Economic Institute of America and YouGov survey finds that two-thirds of Americans view South Korea favorably, essentially unchanged since 2020, but a ten-point partisan gap has opened, with Republican favorability dropping from 76 percent in 2023 to 64 percent in 2024.

A Gallup Korea poll taken November 3 to 5 put President Lee's approval at 41 percent, with the Democratic Party at 36 percent and the People Power Party at 28 percent.

9:12Capitol Hill

On Capitol Hill.

Twenty-two House Republicans sent a letter to the U.S. Trade Representative urging scrutiny of South Korea's pending platform regulatory legislation, citing concerns about its impact on U.S. technology companies in the Korean market. That concern also appears in the Korea Herald's coverage of the Section 301 tariff proposal, where Seoul's digital rules are listed alongside overcapacity as a live issue.

9:39Korea and the World

Beyond the region.

South Korea is accelerating the selection of a new contractor for the Czech nuclear plant project after the Daewoo-Doosan consortium agreement was terminated on November 20. Korea Hydro and Nuclear Power is now directly managing some permitting work. The industry minister said keeping the promise to the Czech Republic is the first principle of all Team Korea decisions. Seoul and Riyadh have expanded their joint oil stockpiling arrangement. And OCBC Malaysia and South Korea's Hana Bank signed an agreement to jointly facilitate South Korean corporate investment into Malaysia.

10:19Markets

Finally, the markets.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed at about 4,711, up about 0.3 percent. The dollar bought about 1,345 won. Brent crude fell sharply, down nearly 4 percent, to about 91 dollars a barrel.
