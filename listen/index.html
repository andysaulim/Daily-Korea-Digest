0:00Today's headlines

This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Sunday, November 8.

Three things today: North Korea fires two more ballistic missiles, U.S. and South Korean officials deepen shipyard and security ties, and Seoul's National Assembly passes a contentious special counsel bill. That missile launch is the fifth weapons test in roughly a week, and state media haven't said a word about any of them. Coming up this week, the justice minister nominee faces a confirmation hearing on November 12.

0:38North Korea fires two ballistic missiles from Muncheon toward East Sea

First, North Korea fired two ballistic missiles from the Muncheon area toward the East Sea on Sunday. The reported flight distance was approximately 350 kilometers. This is the fifth weapons test in roughly a week. The previous tests included an air-defense surface-to-air missile test and a cruise missile airport-strike drill, both confirmed by state media on November 6.

The launch comes alongside a separate U.S. move on North Korean cyber activity. The State Department called for coordinated international action after the FBI attributed the Binance cryptocurrency theft to North Korea. A State Department spokesperson said it is, quote, "critical for governments to work together" to curb North Korean cyber revenue.

That call exposed a split inside Seoul's own government. The Unification Ministry said South Korea has not yet begun discussing additional sanctions because, in its words, the full circumstances of the theft have not been established. The Foreign Ministry said separately it was "reviewing" options. The ministry itself described the gap as a dilemma between international cooperation and inter-Korean engagement.

War on the Rocks argues this week that the Binance heist is part of North Korea's pattern of using cyber operations to fund weapons programs while simultaneously probing for diplomatic openings, and that it complicates any near-term diplomatic opening with Pyongyang in the post-midterm landscape.

2:07Senior US Navy and Marine Corps officials assess HD Hyundai's Ulsan…

Second, the U.S.-South Korea defense relationship is moving on two parallel tracks this week.

On the military-industrial side, a U.S. delegation visited HD Hyundai Heavy Industries' Ulsan shipyard on November 4 to evaluate naval shipbuilding capabilities, installation infrastructure, and logistics support. The delegation included Assistant Secretary of the Navy Brendan P. Rogers, Marine Corps Lieutenant General Andrew M. Niebel, and Major General Valerie A. Jackson. The visit was posted on the Pentagon's Defense Visual Information Distribution Service, which cited opportunities to strengthen maritime sustainment and operational readiness. HD Hyundai signed a joint venture agreement with U.S. construction firm Kiewit in October to build a new U.S. shipyard, with operations targeted for 2029.

On the diplomatic side, Foreign Minister Cho Hyun departed for Paris to attend the G7 foreign ministers meeting. Korean media flagged the trip as his first expected in-person talks with U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio since the Democratic Party's midterm victory. The meetings are seen as an opportunity to advance discussions on nuclear submarine cooperation and broader security consultations that have been stalled by U.S. domestic politics. Separately, South Korea's nuclear submarine program is set to formally launch in 2027 after the government waived the standard feasibility study requirement, allowing the project to proceed on an accelerated schedule.

Reinforcing the alliance's trilateral dimension, General Xavier Brunson, commander of U.S. Forces Korea, the Combined Forces Command, and the U.N. Command, posted Sunday that he met with Japan's defense minister in Tokyo. He said, quote, "Readiness depends on access, sustainment, and committed partners." South Korea's Army and Navy chiefs of staff are also scheduled to visit Japan in mid-November for talks with Japan Self-Defense Forces leadership.

Shipyard visit, Paris talks, and a nuclear submarine program now formally on track for 2027.

4:14National Assembly passes 'fabricated prosecution' special counsel bill

Third, South Korea's National Assembly passed the Special Counsel Act to investigate alleged fabrication of criminal prosecutions under the previous Yoon Suk Yeol administration. The bill covers 12 cases, including the Daejang-dong, Wirye, and Baekseok-dong development scandals, cases in which President Lee Jae-myung had been indicted.

Critics in the opposition People Power Party contend a structural conflict of interest exists because President Lee, as the defendant in the covered cases, will appoint the special counsel who investigates those very prosecutions. The Chosun Ilbo editorial board makes the same argument, noting that the Justice Ministry's own internal review found no evidence of fabricated prosecution in a first-round review of three cases, which legal scholars say burdens the special counsel before it even launches.

The personnel dimension sharpens that tension. President Lee nominated Lee Yong-cheol as justice minister. The People Power Party alleges Lee Yong-cheol was a hidden architect of the Daejang-dong project and argues the appointment is designed to facilitate withdrawal of charges against the president. His confirmation hearing is set for November 12.

A Gallup Korea poll taken October 27 to 29 put presidential approval at 39 percent. The Democratic Party stood at 35 percent, down from the previous reading. The People Power Party was at 30 percent, up slightly. Independents were at 28 percent, down.

The special counsel is passed, the conflict of interest argument is on the record, and the confirmation hearing is four days away.

5:49Pyongyang

Now to Pyongyang.

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi met the delegation and pledged to strengthen bilateral ties.

Kim Jong Un himself appeared on November 7, inspecting a new warship under construction at Nampho Shipyard and ordering strengthening of surface and underwater naval capabilities alongside modernization of the facility.

The bottom line from state media: the Nampho inspection and naval modernization orders are on the domestic agenda. All four recent weapons tests are not.

6:20The round-up

The rest of the news, quickly.

North Korea held the tenth Congress of the Union of Agricultural Workers, pledging innovation in grain and livestock production, including orchards and vegetables. Separately, N.K. News confirms North Korea has expanded warship construction to a third shipyard, adding to existing capacity at Nampho and Sinpo.

Inside North Korea, Daily N.K. reports firewood and coal prices have doubled in Ryanggang Province after temperatures dropped below freezing in late October, leaving low-income families unable to prepare for winter. And North Koreans are increasingly avoiding the word "unification" in daily speech as Kim Jong Un's two-hostile-states doctrine enters the constitution, with fear of a verbal slip driving widespread self-censorship.

Japanese Prime Minister Takaichi Sanae said she is committed to resolving the Japanese abductee issue through a summit with Kim Jong Un within her term.

On the South Korean economy, the Financial Services Supervisory Service suspended margin collateral ratio requirements for securities firms as an emergency measure to prevent forced margin calls from amplifying the market selloff after Thursday's sharp decline.

The government is also planning to release a draft Digital Assets Act this month, establishing a regulatory framework for virtual asset businesses and investor protection, in part following scrutiny of exchange security after the Binance hack.

Prime Minister Han Seong-sook chaired a senior government-ruling party consultation Sunday, agreeing to review approximately 2 trillion won in central government funding to relieve provincial firefighting budget burdens, with additional funding for equipment and training to be incorporated into next year's budget review.

The Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy minister completed a three-day trip to China, holding a Korea-China commerce ministers meeting in Shanghai and meeting Korean and Chinese business leaders. The minister said he was, quote, "more alarmed" by the pace of Chinese industrial development after visiting the Shanghai International Import Expo, where Samsung, SK, and over 150 small- and medium-sized Korean companies were exhibiting.

Seoul National University selected mechanical engineering professor Choi Hae-cheon as its next president, the first engineering-background president in 20 years. His term begins in February 2027.

On November 16, the Hoover Institution Northeast Asia History Working Group hosts journalist and North Korean defector Joo Sung Ha for a public discussion of North Korean society, education, and culture at Stanford University.

8:59Capitol Hill

On Capitol Hill, U.S. lawmakers are racing to make a ban on Chinese-manufactured vehicles permanent before the current congressional session ends. South Korean automakers would benefit from continued exclusion of Chinese rivals in the U.S. market if the measure is codified.

9:16Korea and the World

Beyond the region, South Korea and Turkey are expanding defense industrial cooperation, with reporting describing the two as complementary defense powers, reflecting Seoul's effort to diversify its defense export base.

Kazakhstan raised crude oil exports to South Korea by 12 percent in the January-to-August period compared with the prior year, as Seoul works to diversify energy supply sources.

South Korea will conduct a training program for ASEAN government officials focused on disaster safety technology, expanding its development assistance footprint across Southeast Asia.

9:52Markets

Finally, the markets. Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, fell about 6 percent on November 6, closing at about 5,059, its sharpest single-session drop, tied to concern over U.S. data center restrictions affecting demand for high-bandwidth memory. The won weakened to about 1,356 to the dollar. Samsung is on track to become the first South Korean company to hold 200 trillion won in cash reserves, even as its stock bore the brunt of the selloff alongside SK Hynix.
