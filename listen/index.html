Transcript

0:00Today's headlines

This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Wednesday, November 4.

Three things today: North Korea fires more missiles, a court upholds Yoon Suk Yeol's conviction, and Kim Jong Un reveals a new class of warship. The missile launches land on U.S. midterm election day, paired with a message from Kim Yo Jong that keeps the door to talks open just a crack, while the weapons keep flying.

0:31North Korea fires several missiles toward East Sea, fourth weapons…

First, North Korea's fourth weapons event in a week.

South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff reported several missiles launched from the Sondok area of South Hamgyong Province toward the East Sea on Wednesday morning. Analysis of the weapons' specifications is still underway. Japan's Defense Ministry issued no alert, which suggests the weapons may have been shorter-range or non-ballistic types.

The timing is pointed. This follows approximately a dozen short-range ballistic missiles fired from Wonsan on October 29. The Wall Street Journal describes this as North Korea's busiest missile-testing spree in years.

Running alongside the launches, Kim Yo Jong issued a statement through state media saying U.S. talks are possible, but not a necessity for North Korea's national interests. She also rejected the FBI's attribution of a 6.36 billion dollar Binance cryptocurrency heist to North Korean cybercriminals, calling it a reflection of Washington's, in her words, "invariable hostile policy," and demanding the U.S. abandon that posture before any engagement.

The Washington Post and Wall Street Journal both reported the Binance denial. South Korea's Foreign Ministry suspects the relevant document leaked through an overseas diplomatic mission, not through a cyberattack.

Also in Pyongyang's diplomatic lane: Kim Song Nam, director of the Workers' Party of Korea International Department, arrived in Beijing on Tuesday at the invitation of the Chinese Communist Party Central Committee, ten days after accompanying Kim Jong Un to Dandong. A Chinese Communist Party delegation was simultaneously in Pyongyang.

What to watch: whether the Sondok launch specifications, once analyzed, point to a new weapons type, and whether Kim Yo Jong's conditional dialogue signal draws any response from Washington.

2:25Seoul High Court upholds Yoon's suspended prison term in election law…

Second, the Seoul High Court upholds Yoon Suk Yeol's conviction.

The court confirmed an 18-month prison term, suspended for three years, against the former president for making false statements during the March 2022 presidential campaign. If that ruling survives a further appeal to the Supreme Court, it would nullify his election. It would also legally require his People Power Party to return 39.7 billion won in campaign reimbursements under election law.

The People Power Party says it will fight the ruling at the Supreme Court. That fight is now running on two tracks. In the National Assembly, the Democratic Party-led judiciary subcommittee passed what's called the fabricated-prosecution special counsel bill over People Power Party opposition. The Democratic Party announced it would bring the bill to a full plenary session. The People Power Party responded by threatening a filibuster against all legislation, and bipartisan negotiations in the chamber collapsed.

Meanwhile, in the budget committee, the two parties clashed over a government proposal to create a new Future Response Fund. The Democratic Party calls it a fiscal safety net. The People Power Party calls it an election-campaign slush fund.

In the latest Gallup Korea poll, taken October 27 to 29, presidential approval sits at 39 percent. The Democratic Party stands at 35 percent, the People Power Party at 30 percent, and independents at 28 percent.

The number to remember: 39.7 billion won at stake for the People Power Party if the Supreme Court does not intervene.

4:05Kim inspects Nampho Shipyard, reveals new warship class larger than…

Third, Kim Jong Un inspects a new and larger warship.

State media reported that Kim Jong Un visited the Nampho Shipyard on Sunday and was shown a warship under construction. Kim described it as having a, in his words, "huge and imposing appearance, which bears no comparison with the destroyer built last year." N.K. News analysis of the state media report concludes the vessel represents a third, larger class of warship. That same analysis found that work on a third destroyer at the same facility appears to have been abandoned in favor of this new, bigger type.

Kim ordered rapid naval buildup and major infrastructure upgrades at the shipyard.

Separately, state media confirmed North Korea tested a dispersal warhead for a strategic cruise missile. That follows a cluster bomb warhead test for tactical ballistic missiles announced on October 31. Both are new warhead types announced within days of each other.

A 38 North analysis published today argues that North Korea's upgraded 240 millimeter multiple-launch rocket system now incorporates precision guidance, automation, and extended range, representing a qualitative leap in long-range artillery capability, not an incremental improvement.

The pattern across all three developments: Pyongyang is simultaneously expanding its naval reach, adding new warhead types, and improving the precision of its ground-based rocket forces.

5:34Pyongyang

Now to Pyongyang.

State media led today with Kim Jong Un's inspection of the Nampho Shipyard and Kim Yo Jong's dual-track statement pairing conditional dialogue with the same day's missile launches, the central story of this morning's brief. KCNA also confirmed a cruise missile dispersal warhead test, following the October 31 cluster warhead announcement. North Korea's propaganda loudspeakers have been installed at two inter-Korean border sites, at Odusan and Yeoncheon, though no audible broadcasts were detected from the South Korean side. The bottom line from state media: Pyongyang is projecting both military momentum and a managed, conditional openness to diplomacy.

6:15The round-up

The rest of the news, quickly.

On the diplomatic front, Deputy Foreign Minister Chung Eui-hae held the seventh round of Korea-Switzerland bilateral policy consultations in Seoul on Wednesday with Swiss Assistant State Secretary Markus Leitner, reviewing Swiss Foreign Minister Cassis's October 29 to 30 visit to Pyongyang, where Cassis met North Korean Foreign Minister Choe Son Hui. Seoul called for Switzerland's continued contributions through its Neutral Nations Supervisory Commission role. A Sejong study published today finds that Stockholm and Bern offer potential channels for Seoul to reach Pyongyang, partly explaining why South Korea is investing in this relationship.

South Korea's trade minister met China's commerce minister in Shanghai and agreed to accelerate stalled Korea-China Free Trade Agreement talks on services and investment. Deputy Minister Park Jong-han met Poland's deputy minister in Warsaw Tuesday, reviewing cooperation on K2 tanks, K9 howitzers, and Chunmoo rocket launchers, with talks on local production and technology transfer.

South Korea launched a new drone development agency on November 3 to close the gap with North Korea's low-cost drone capabilities, which the South China Morning Post says expose regulatory bottlenecks in Seoul's development process.

Nikkei reports North Korea is leaning on China and Russia to drive 3 percent economic growth. N.K. News separately assesses that growth is unsustainable without structural reform.

Unification Minister Chung Dong-young said Wednesday that wild boars crossing the military demarcation line are spreading African swine fever, and that Seoul is preparing inter-Korean joint quarantine cooperation covering swine fever, foot-and-mouth disease, and avian influenza.

A police investigation is underway into a leak of a presidential aircraft passenger manifest that exposed 168 names and passport numbers. South Korea's Foreign Ministry suspects the document leaked through an overseas diplomatic mission.

President Lee Jae-myung visited a public redevelopment site in Seoul's Seongbuk district Wednesday, calling the standard 20 to 30 year redevelopment timeline excessive and pledging higher floor-area ratios and streamlined approvals. Lee called metropolitan housing price stabilization the most important current policy priority.

The Foreign Ministry marked the 30th anniversary of South Korea's OECD accession with a seminar in Seoul on Wednesday. Prime Minister Han Seong-sook and OECD Secretary-General Mathias Cormann both participated by video.

In business: imported vehicle sales rose to 31,117 units in October, up 29.3 percent from a year earlier, with Tesla taking the top two spots. The Financial Services Commission concluded a three-year enforcement action on Hong Kong equity-linked securities sales, cutting the final penalty from 4 trillion won to approximately 360 billion won. SK Group chairman Chey Tae-won has been named board chair of SK Hynix's new U.S. artificial intelligence investment vehicle, HYAICO Corporation. Tomorrow, the DaouKiwoom-CJ consortium signs a 520 billion won agreement to acquire JoongAng Ilbo, resolving the media group's debt workout.

9:40Korea and the World

Beyond the region.

South Korea and the African Union held their seventh policy consultation in Addis Ababa on November 3, covering the Korea-Africa cooperation fund and areas including trade, investment, health, agriculture, climate, and peace and security. Both sides affirmed the African Union's role as a channel for deeper Korea-Africa partnership. The Foreign Ministry also held a meeting of Mekong region mission chiefs in Hanoi on Wednesday, marking the 15th anniversary of Korea-Mekong cooperation.

10:14Markets

Finally, the markets.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed up about 0.6 percent at 5,683. The won traded at about 1,339 to the dollar, up slightly. Brent crude fell, closing at just over 102 dollars a barrel. The Bank of Korea's policy rate stands at 3 percent, last set in October.
