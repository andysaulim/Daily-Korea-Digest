0:00Today's headlines

This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Friday, November 6.

Three things today: Korean markets in freefall after Democrats take Congress, North Korea expands its warship buildup to a third shipyard, and President Lee nominates a new justice minister. On the first story: a single election result on the other side of the Pacific has just wiped out more than a trillion dollars in Korean market value, and Seoul's financial authorities are in emergency mode.

0:36Korea's $1.7 trillion market rout burns day traders as AI selloff…

First, the markets.

South Korea's main stock index, the KOSPI, fell 6 percent on Friday, as a Democratic Party sweep of the U.S. midterm elections sent investors scrambling out of Korean chip stocks. Bloomberg reports a one-point-seven-trillion-dollar rout in Korean markets. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix each sank roughly 10 percent in pre-market trading. The selloff spread from semiconductors into batteries, autos, and shipbuilding.

The Democratic Party in Seoul, which also happens to be President Lee Jae-myung's governing party, convened an emergency party-government meeting. Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok said the crash was driven primarily by investor fears that a Democratic-controlled Congress would tighten regulation of artificial intelligence and data centers, and that South Korea's heavy structural exposure to semiconductors amplified the market's reaction.

Financial authorities held their own emergency meeting and announced a three-month suspension of the rule requiring brokerages to maintain a 140 percent collateral ratio on credit loans. The goal is to prevent forced liquidations from cascading through the market.

For Korea policy watchers, the question is whether this is a one-day shock or the beginning of a longer reoganization of how global investors view Korea's tech sector. Korean experts, cited in Korean media this morning, are warning that Seoul now needs to engage a Democratic-controlled Congress separately from the Republican White House, particularly on issues like nuclear submarine acquisition and tariff policy.

The bottom line: a six percent single-day drop is the KOSPI's sharpest fall of the year, and it happened entirely because of a foreign election.

2:19North Korea expands warship construction project to third shipyard

Second, North Korea's naval buildup.

N.K. News is reporting that North Korea has expanded its warship construction program to a third shipyard. That follows activity already identified at Nampho and at least one other site. Earlier reporting described vessels under construction as cruiser-class ships potentially exceeding 10,000 tons.

That expansion sits alongside a broader acceleration of North Korea's weapons program. The Wall Street Journal reports Pyongyang is on its busiest missile-testing spree in years, with four weapons tests in just one week, including surface-to-air missile drills and cruise missile exercises. KCNA confirmed the latest test: a new anti-aircraft missile system and a cruise missile attack drill targeting an airport.

Here is the detail that stands out for analysts: North Korea has not reported any of this week's tests to its own domestic audience. KCNA has stayed completely silent to people inside North Korea about all four launches. That gap between what Pyongyang is doing and what it is telling its own citizens is a signal worth watching.

Separately, 38 North's Vann Van Diepen has published analysis documenting what he describes as a deliberate soft public disclosure of a new North Korean stealth strategic unmanned aerial vehicle, revealed at an exhibition last month called Unmanned Aeronautical Technology Development-2026.

Kim Yo Jong issued a statement on November 4 saying U.S.-North Korea talks are possible but not a necessity, conditioning any dialogue on what she called Washington abandoning its hostile policy. And a senior Workers' Party of Korea official traveled to Beijing and met with China's Foreign Minister Wang Yi, who vowed closer ties. That visit follows Kim Jong Un's unannounced trip to Dandong in October.

Four weapons tests in a week, a third shipyard, a new stealth drone, and diplomatic outreach to Beijing: Pyongyang is signaling on multiple fronts at once.

4:24Lee nominates defense procurement chief Lee Yong-cheol as justice…

Third, justice minister nomination.

President Lee Jae-myung has nominated Lee Yong-cheol, the current head of the Defense Acquisition Program Administration, as justice minister. Lee Yong-cheol is a former lawyer who served as legal affairs secretary under President Roh Moo-hyun. He also worked at the same law firm as President Lee and served on his Seongnam mayoral transition committee. The nominee pledged to complete prosecution reform and establish the new Serious Crimes Investigation Agency.

Separately, President Lee approved the submission of a confirmation hearing request to the National Assembly for Lee Tae-hyun, the nominee for the next president of KBS, the public broadcaster.

Also in personnel news: Choi In-ho, currently serving as president of the Korea Housing Finance Corporation and a former Democratic Party lawmaker, has been tapped as the new Blue House political affairs secretary. He replaces Hong Ik-pyo, who resigned.

5:22Pyongyang

Now to Pyongyang.

State media confirmed North Korea's new anti-aircraft missile test and cruise missile airport-strike drill, its fourth weapons test in a week, and framed it as a routine exercise. KCNA also condemned the Financial Action Task Force's decision to keep North Korea on its high-risk jurisdiction list, calling it politically motivated. And 38 North separately documented the stealth strategic unmanned aerial vehicle soft reveal from the October exhibition. The bottom line from state media today: North Korea is showcasing weapons abroad while keeping its own people entirely in the dark about what it is testing.

6:02The round-up

The rest of the news, quickly.

South Korea's current account surplus for September hit a monthly record of 61.46 billion dollars, driven by semiconductor exports that surged 260.3 percent year-on-year. That breaks the previous monthly record of 49.73 billion dollars set in June.

Bloomberg separately argues this morning that the semiconductor boom is masking structural weaknesses in South Korea's broader economy. A new CSIS report makes a related point, arguing that South Korea's export-driven growth model faces structural exhaustion from a shrinking workforce, China's push for self-sufficiency, and U.S. trade-rebalancing pressure. The report, authored by Navin Girishankar, Philip Luck, and five co-authors, maps three competing policy options for Seoul.

The Wall Street Journal is also investigating North Korea's 6.3-billion-dollar crypto heist involving the exchange Binance. Reuters separately describes North Korean hackers as a, quote, secret weapon in that exchange's takedown.

South Korea's deputy foreign minister met British lawmakers on Friday for discussions on North Korea policy and the Ukraine conflict. And Deputy Minister Park Yoon-joo met with Belgian Secretary-General Theodora Gentzis to review follow-up from the June summit between President Lee and Belgian leaders. Both sides agreed to revive the Korea-Belgium Policy Consultation mechanism at deputy minister level, last held in 1999.

A Korea-Japan first-posting diplomat exchange program has resumed for its second dispatch. The program was originally agreed under the 1998 Kim Dae-jung and Obuchi joint declaration and had not operated in recent years.

South Korean crypto exchange GOPAX suspended withdrawals on Friday afternoon and moved most assets to cold storage after receiving hacking threats. No asset loss was reported. The Financial Services Commission separately confirmed cybersecurity incidents at 37 financial institutions affecting about 470,000 customer records.

A Gallup Korea poll taken October 27 to 29 put President Lee's approval at 39 percent. The Democratic Party stood at 35 percent, down. The People Power Party was at 30 percent, up. And independents stood at 28 percent, down.

8:27Capitol Hill

On Capitol Hill.

Democrats have taken Congress, and Korean experts are warning Seoul must now engage the new Democratic majority separately from the Republican White House, with implications for South Korea's nuclear submarine program, tariff exposure, and the prospect of a Trump-Kim summit. Meanwhile, House Republicans have requested a closed-door briefing with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang over alleged ties to Chinese intelligence, a development with direct implications for SK Hynix and Samsung. And Senator Jon Ossoff won re-election in Georgia, preserving a Senate voice Chosun Ilbo describes as pro-Korea.

9:07Korea and the World

Beyond the region.

South Korea announced it will extend the Cheonghae Unit's area of operations from the Gulf of Aden to waters south of Oman, adding a maritime patrol aircraft, an explosive ordnance disposal team, and mine-clearance drones. A revised deployment motion goes to the National Assembly next week. President Lee has explicitly ruled out sending troops into the U.S.-Iran conflict zone. And U.N. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres expressed concern on November 5 over Korean Peninsula tensions, including the risk of escalation across the Demilitarized Zone.

9:44Markets

Finally, the markets.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed at about 5,060, down about 6 percent. The won weakened to about 1,357 per dollar. Brent crude was at about 99 dollars a barrel, up slightly. The Bank of Korea's benchmark rate stands at 3.00 percent, last changed in October.
