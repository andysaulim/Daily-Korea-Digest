That first story matters because the first-ever U.S.-China-Russia trilateral summit produced a four-point declaration on nuclear risk and the Korean Peninsula was not in it. South Korea also crossed one trillion dollars in annual exports today, at 2:09 this afternoon.

0:29Trump, Xi, Putin issue first trilateral joint declaration, Korean…

First, the Shenzhen summit and what it left out.

Donald Trump, Xi Jinping, and Vladimir Putin met at the Kylin Villa in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the APEC summit. It was the first-ever U.S.-China-Russia trilateral, and it produced a four-point joint declaration. The three leaders pledged dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing proliferation, reforming the United Nations, increasing production and supply of refined petroleum, and respecting state sovereignty.

North Korea was not mentioned. The North Korean nuclear program was not mentioned. Denuclearization of the Korean Peninsula was not mentioned.

South Korean and Chinese analysts flagged the absence. Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that the three powers' divergent nuclear calculations on the Korean Peninsula produced only a gap in the communique.

The CSIS Korea Chair's Capital Cable podcast argues that North Korea's nuclear program was conspicuously absent from the declaration, and examines what the trilateral's framing of nuclear risk reduction, without naming the Korean Peninsula, means for North Korea's strategic calculus. It connects Kim Jong Un's simultaneous east-coast military inspection to the unresolved question of a Trump-Kim meeting.

On Capitol Hill, VOA Korean reported that U.S. lawmakers hold divergent priorities as the summit convened, with congressional attention spanning Iran and North Korea alongside trade concerns.

Also at APEC: President Lee Jae-myung and Putin held a 10-minute meeting at the welcome banquet, the first Lee-Putin encounter. A senior Blue House official said both leaders agreed on the need to prevent further escalation on the Korean Peninsula and agreed to pursue bilateral cooperation in areas free of friction.

The bottom line from Shenzhen: the first great-power trilateral of this era addressed nuclear risk in the abstract, and left the Korean Peninsula's specific nuclear problem unaddressed.

2:32IAEA confirms Yongbyon enrichment annex externally complete, reactor…

Second, the International Atomic Energy Agency's confirmation on Yongbyon.

The International Atomic Energy Agency Director General Rafael Grossi told the Board of Governors in Vienna that North Korea has completed external construction of a new annex to the original Yongbyon uranium enrichment facility. Construction finished since mid-September, and the structure appeared externally complete by late October.

N.K. News reports the annex is similar in size to one built on the north side of the same Yongbyon building five years ago. State media in January 2025 appeared to show new centrifuge cascades filling a comparable annex interior.

Grossi also confirmed that the 5 megawatt electric reactor is continuing its seventh irradiation cycle, and that the light water reactor has been operating since late April.

That is three simultaneous lines of nuclear activity at Yongbyon: a newly completed enrichment annex, an operating research reactor in its seventh cycle, and a light water reactor running since the spring.

The same Board of Governors meeting in Vienna also took up a separate International Atomic Energy Agency report on South Korea's nuclear-powered submarine program. The agency expressed satisfaction with Seoul's cooperation on safeguards and affirmed that all nuclear materials in South Korea have been used for peaceful purposes since 2007.

Watch what Grossi says publicly after the Board session closes, and whether any member state calls for a formal response to the Yongbyon expansion.

4:06Democratic Party moves to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice after…

Third, South Korea's Supreme Court ruling and the political fallout.

The Supreme Court partially overturned ex-Prime Minister Han Duck-soo's 15-year prison sentence. Han had been convicted for his role in former President Yoon Suk Yeol's December 2024 martial law bid. The court found that Han's proposal to convene a Cabinet meeting did not meet the threshold for insurrection intent. His case has been remanded to the Seoul High Court.

The Democratic Party's response was immediate. Party leader Kim Min-seok announced the party would pursue impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, calling the ruling a, quote, "historical act of treason."

The People Power Party leader Jang Dong-hyuk welcomed the ruling. Jang called it, quote, "the judiciary's rebuke of the Lee administration's insurrection narrative," and said, quote, "another fabricated indictment has revealed its true nature."

The People Power Party had also welcomed a November 18 acquittal of former Daegu Mayor Choo Kyung-ho on charges related to the December 2024 emergency vote.

In the National Assembly, the Democratic Party brought the Democratic Merit Act, which concerns recognition and benefits for participants in pro-democracy movements, to the floor. The People Power Party launched a filibuster in opposition.

The confirmation hearing for Justice Minister nominee Lee Yong-cheol has been set for November 24. The civic group Peoples' Solidarity for Participatory Democracy submitted 26 policy questions to the nominee on judicial reform direction.

A Gallup Korea poll taken November 3 to 5 put presidential approval at 41 percent, with the Democratic Party at 36 percent support and the People Power Party at 28 percent.

5:54Pyongyang

Now to Pyongyang.

State media led today with two stories. First, KCNA reported that Russia will erect a monument this year honoring North Korean troops who fought in the Kursk region. This is the first time North Korean state media has acknowledged North Korean military participation in the conflict in Ukraine.

Second, KCNA announced North Korea will convene a meeting of cell-level and primary party organization leaders of the Korean Workers' Party this month, a routine mobilization mechanism that often precedes major political or economic campaigns.

Kim Jong Un himself inspected the Kim Jong Suk Naval University and construction sites in Riwon and Ragwon counties of South Hamgyong Province. At the university, Kim said, quote, "The university's primary duty is to train professional naval commanders." He criticized what he called the naval activities of wicked enemies. State media made no mention of U.S. talks.

The bottom line: Pyongyang's first acknowledgment of its troops fighting in Ukraine comes as Kim's foreign affairs secretary, Jo Yong Won, returns from Moscow, where he met President Putin. Daily N.K. reports that North Korean authorities issued a gag order on domestic discussion of that visit.

7:12The round-up

The rest of the news, quickly.

South Korea crossed one trillion dollars in annual exports at 2:09 this afternoon, the fourth country globally to do so, after Germany, the United States, and China, with AI-related semiconductors driving the surge. President Lee, posting from Shenzhen as he departed the APEC summit, noted that South Korea's exports stood at 10 billion dollars fifty years ago.

The Korea-China free trade agreement services and investment follow-on talks concluded after 8 years and 8 months. Presidents Lee and Xi declared substantive conclusion at their APEC bilateral in Shenzhen. The two leaders agreed to accelerate market access, expand cultural exchanges, and send a pair of pandas to South Korea ahead of the 35th anniversary of diplomatic ties next year. Korean entertainment, gaming, and travel stocks surged on the news, with drama producer Studio Dragon up nearly 30 percent.

The U.S. abstained from the U.N. Third Committee resolution on North Korean human rights for the first time in 21 years. Washington called for a vote, departing from the established consensus practice, then abstained, citing objections to a reference to the International Criminal Court. The resolution passed 95 to 14, with 58 abstentions, co-sponsored by 58 countries including South Korea. It goes to the General Assembly plenary in December.

The European Union created a dedicated EU Sanctions Office to coordinate restrictions against Russia and North Korea, combining sanctions expertise from policy design through enforcement and anti-circumvention.

38 North reports North Korea showcased a domestic artificial intelligence chatbot at a recent national I.T. exhibition, the first public debut of a North Korea-developed AI tool.

Also in analysis worth your attention: Lee Byong-chul, writing in 38 North, argues that Seoul should pursue a formal two-state framework with Pyongyang, a managed separation that institutionalizes division as sovereign equals rather than maintaining unification frameworks neither side can operationalize. And The Diplomat profiles Newtype, a South Korean defense startup whose CEO says cooperation with Ukraine has continued, noting that a Russian strike on its system suggested Moscow had identified and targeted the technology.

Russian military aircraft entered South Korea's air defense zone for a second consecutive day. The South Korean Air Force scrambled fighters in response.

A Seoul National University student with a perfect score on the law school entrance exam was found dead amid an artificial intelligence cheating probe. A note cited exhaustion, and police are investigating.

South Korea joined the United States, Australia, and other countries in releasing a statement of principles for a Southeast and East Asia-Pacific scam response contact group. Seoul plans a Korea-ASEAN scam response cooperation program starting in early 2027.

10:14Capitol Hill

On Capitol Hill.

Incoming House member Dan Koh addressed North Korean citizens directly, saying, quote, "I am thinking of you," and pledged to work to keep the U.S.-Korea alliance strong. VOA Korean also reported that U.S. lawmakers hold divergent views on priorities as the trilateral summit convened, with congressional attention on both Iran and North Korea alongside trade.

10:40Korea and the World

Beyond the region.

South Korea joined Canada and other NATO partners as funders of the new NATO STARLIFT initiative to boost alliance launch capabilities in space.

India and South Korea announced 25 specific cooperation outcomes and elevated their bilateral relationship to a, quote, "futuristic partnership," covering defense, the digital economy, and supply chains, following a bilateral summit on the sidelines of the ASEAN summit in Manila.

South Korea and the International Energy Agency jointly launched the Rise Asia clean energy cooperation initiative at the 31st Conference of the Parties in Antalya, Turkey, focusing on clean electrification and energy risk information sharing.

11:25Markets

Finally, the markets.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed at about 5,138, up about 1.4 percent. The won strengthened slightly, trading at about 1,328 to the dollar. Brent crude fell about 3 percent, to around 89 dollars a barrel. The Bank of Korea's policy rate stands at 3 percent, last set in October.
