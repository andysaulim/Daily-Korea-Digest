0:00Today's headlines

This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Friday, October 23.

Three things today: Seoul proposes military talks with Pyongyang over DMZ mines, U.S. and South Korean officials say a Trump-Kim summit is unlikely, and a Seoul appeals court acquits Mayor Oh Se-hoon. The mine story is the one to watch: South Korea is reaching out for dialogue even as North Korea threatens to turn on propaganda loudspeakers within days. The National Assembly audit is ongoing today, with committees examining defense, the Future Response Fund, and household loans.

0:44Seoul proposes inter-Korean military talks on MDL as mine estimates…

First, the mines and the proposal for military talks.

South Korea's Defense Ministry Deputy Minister for National Defense Policy, Kim Hong-Cheol, issued a formal statement Friday. He proposed military talks with North Korea to verify the exact location of the Military Demarcation Line and restore military communication lines. The trigger: land mine explosions last month that injured three South Korean soldiers.

At the same time, lawmakers at the National Assembly audit cited intelligence estimates that Pyongyang has planted 960,000 mines in the DMZ since 2024, at a rate of roughly 1,000 per day.

The proposal for talks lands in a charged moment. Kim Yo Jong, after inspecting the inter-Korean border, has threatened to restart propaganda loudspeaker broadcasts toward the South within days. She also warned of what she called new counter means, including new firepower deployments along the line.

Seoul separately sent a letter to the U.N. Security Council urging North Korea to take responsibility for the mine blasts. That is the first formal U.N. Security Council communication on the incident.

So Seoul is doing two things at once: reaching out for military dialogue while also going to the U.N. And that tension sits against a backdrop of an estimated 960,000 mines now in the ground.

2:09US and ROK officials see little chance of Trump-Kim summit in 2026

Second, the Trump-Kim summit picture and questions about Kim Jong Un.

Reuters reported Friday that U.S. and South Korean officials see little chance of a meeting between President Donald Trump and North Korean leader Kim Jong Un this year. The reason officials cited: Pyongyang's demands that Washington abandon its denuclearization approach.

That assessment tracks with what two outside experts said on a podcast hosted by CSIS Korea Chair Victor Cha. Jenny Town of the Stimson Center and Ankit Panda of Carnegie both said North Korea has made clear it rejects any denuclearization framework. The real question, they said, is whether Washington is willing to shift its policy.

Separately, social media has been circulating rumors about Kim Jong Un's health or death. South Korea's National Intelligence Service briefed the National Assembly Intelligence Committee on Friday and dismissed those rumors as false. Kim was last confirmed publicly on October 20.

One item worth connecting here: Daily N.K. reports that North Korea's domestic schedule has shifted, with the ninth party congress and Supreme People's Assembly events reportedly rescheduled, which some read as Kim preparing for a possible Trump meeting. But the weight of official assessment, from both Seoul and Washington, points the other way for now.

The core fact remains: U.S. and South Korean officials say a Trump-Kim summit is unlikely this year.

3:40Seoul appeals court acquits Oh Se-hoon of political funds violation

Third, the acquittal of Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon.

A Seoul appeals court on Friday overturned a lower court ruling and acquitted Mayor Oh Se-hoon of charges related to a political funds law violation. The case stemmed from what is known as the Myung Tae-kyun opinion poll payment allegation.

The ruling drew sharply different reactions from the two main parties. The ruling Democratic Party called the decision contrary to public common sense. The People Power Party called it inevitable justice and demanded the special prosecutor take responsibility for the prosecution.

The acquittal of Oh Se-hoon, mayor of the capital, is a politically charged outcome at a moment when both parties are watching their poll numbers closely.

4:24Pyongyang

Now to Pyongyang.

The dominant story in state media Friday was Kim Yo Jong's border inspection and her warning that loudspeaker broadcasts will be ready within days. That is a story the leads already covered, but note how Pyongyang framed it: as a response to southern threats, with language about resolute action from border troops.

State media also reported the opening of a national garment and footwear exhibition in Pyongyang, consistent with the regime's consumer goods promotion drive. And Daily N.K. reports that North Korean traders are sourcing concrete mixer trucks and pump trucks from China, reflecting demand from the regime's rural development program.

The bottom line from Pyongyang today: Kim Yo Jong's loudspeaker threat dominated state media output, and Kim Jong Un made no public appearance. A Chinese veterans affairs delegation arrived in Pyongyang ahead of the anniversary of China's entry into the Korean War.

5:20The round-up

The rest of the news, quickly.

On the economy, Bank of Korea Governor Shin Hyun-song told the National Assembly audit he sees no return to ultra-low interest rates. He said the Bank of Korea will operate in what he called a high-growth and high-rate environment, and he projected South Korea's growth rate could reach the 3 percent range next year.

President Lee Jae-myung chaired the second meeting of the Regulatory Rationalization Committee Friday, directing officials to ease extended work-hour approvals for semiconductor research and development staff rather than create new legislation. He set additional overtime compensation as a precondition. The government also announced fast-track permitting for semiconductor industrial clusters, covering licensing, compensation, and design approvals.

At the National Assembly audit, the ruling Democratic Party and the opposition People Power Party sparred over the Future Response Fund. Democrats called it fiscally sound; the People Power Party called it a patch fund accumulating future liabilities. A separate committee heard a lawmaker question why public institutions spent 7.5 billion won on compression software licenses.

South Korean police are investigating a suspected North Korean I.T. worker who allegedly obtained employment at a domestic organization under a false identity. The case was independently identified by the National Security Investigation Bureau last year. Separately, a U.S. network monitoring firm assessed that a recent internet outage affecting North Korean state websites was caused by a distributed denial-of-service attack, with sites offline for a second consecutive day as of October 21.

The New York Times published a report Friday on an accelerating arms buildup on the Korean Peninsula, framing North Korea's military assertiveness in the context of its deepened partnership with Russia. The Korea Institute for National Unification separately published an analysis of how expanding North Korea-Russia rail, road, and port infrastructure is reshaping industrial cities on the North Korean border.

On the Korea-Japan front, President Lee received a delegation from the Japan-Korea Business Council, led by Tsutsui Yoshinobu. Both sides agreed the current trade environment requires joint response and that summit shuttle diplomacy should continue.

South Korea and the U.S. remain at odds over the transfer of wartime operational control, according to Nikkei, with Seoul pushing to accelerate the transition away from U.S. command of combined forces.

South Korea donated eight refurbished 500MD helicopters to Kenya at a ceremony in Nairobi, fulfilling a pledge made at the 2021 Seoul U.N. Peacekeeping Ministerial. The U.S. provided maintenance and transport support.

A Gallup Korea poll taken October 13 to 15 put President Lee's approval at 39 percent, up from the previous survey. The Democratic Party stood at 36 percent, also up. The People Power Party was at 27 percent, up as well, while independent support fell to 29 percent.

8:27Korea and the World

Beyond the region.

An EU envoy told Korea JoongAng Daily that North Korea and China are, in the envoy's words, "direct enablers" of Russia's war in Ukraine, framing that trilateral alignment as a direct challenge to European security. South Korea separately recalled a Korea International Cooperation Agency employee from Kyiv, citing a security threat. That move reflects Seoul's sensitivity about its posture on the Ukraine conflict.

8:57Markets

Finally, the markets. Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed up about 0.3 percent on Friday, at about 5,550. The won traded at about 1,325 to the dollar, nearly flat. Brent crude fell about 2.7 percent to about 110 dollars a barrel.
