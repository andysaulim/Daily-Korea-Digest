Transcript

0:00Today's headlines

This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Friday, November 13.

Three things today: South Korea's foreign minister meets China's Wang Yi in Beijing, the U.S. Justice Department moves to seize over 1.2 billion dollars in North Korea-linked crypto, and North Korea redesigns its national flag for the first time since 1948. That diplomatic meeting in Beijing matters because it comes just days before President Lee Jae-myung and Xi Jinping are expected to meet face to face at APEC. President Lee departs for the ASEAN Summit in Manila on Sunday, November 16, before flying to Shenzhen for APEC.

0:47FM Cho, Wang Yi meet in Beijing ahead of Lee-Xi APEC summit

First, the Beijing talks. South Korea's Foreign Minister Cho Hyun met China's Foreign Minister Wang Yi today at the Diaoyutai State Guesthouse in Beijing. Cho described the Korean Peninsula issue as, in his words, "the vital interest of the Republic of Korea, and the peace and stability of the Korean Peninsula is the common interest of both countries." Wang, for his part, assessed that the bilateral relationship has "returned to the normal track of improvement and development." The two sides pointed to a projected one-thousand-fold increase in people-to-people exchanges and more than 40 percent trade growth expected in 2026 as signs of momentum. The talks are explicitly framed as preparation for a Lee-Xi meeting on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen. That summit begins November 18 and would mark the first Lee-Xi bilateral in ten months. Before departing for Beijing, Cho told reporters at Gimpo Airport that the chance of Kim Jong Un appearing at APEC in Shenzhen is, in his words, "not very high." Watch for what Lee and Xi say about the Korean Peninsula when they meet in Shenzhen. The shape of that conversation will tell you a great deal about where Beijing stands. The vital interest framing Cho used today signals Seoul wants that message heard clearly.

2:06DOJ obtains court authorization to seize $1.2B in DPRK-linked Binance…

Second, the crypto seizure. The U.S. Department of Justice announced today that it has obtained court authorization to seize over 1.2 billion dollars stolen from the Binance exchange and linked to North Korean state-sponsored hackers. The Justice Department attributed the theft to two operations: a social engineering campaign called FaceStealer, and a separate exchange hack. Bloomberg separately reported that a broader freeze tied to the same North Korean Binance hacking operation amounts to 2.7 billion dollars. The FBI, meanwhile, attributed more than 400 million dollars in additional thefts over the past 18 months to North Korean state-sponsored actors using video and image-based social engineering. A Seoul court this week also sentenced three people to prison for helping North Korean cyber actors create gambling websites, with one defendant receiving four years and the other two receiving three years each. Taken together, today's actions represent one of the largest single enforcement moves against North Korea's cryptocurrency operations, targeting the funding streams that analysts say help bankroll Pyongyang's weapons programs.

3:19North Korea changes flag aspect ratio for first time since 1948

Third, the flag. North Korea has changed its national flag for the first time since the country's founding in 1948. The official website Naenara began displaying the flag with a new aspect ratio of 1 to 1.65, replacing the longstanding 1 to 2 ratio. The two thin white stripes also appear thicker than before. The change is embedded in a revised constitution. N.K. News and Yonhap both report this as the latest in a series of moves by Kim Jong Un to build a distinct national identity, separate from the founding era of Kim Il Sung. State media also confirmed that Kim inspected a second major east-coast naval base under construction on November 12, ordering it completed on schedule and to the quality standard set by the party central. And N.K. News separately reports that North Korea's November 4 cluster warhead test was faked, with state media releasing doctored photographs from 2022 rather than images of a live test. The flag change and the constitution revision that contains it mark a deliberate break from Pyongyang's founding iconography, reinforcing the Kim Jong Un era as one defined on its own terms.

4:32Pyongyang

Now to Pyongyang. State media's lead today was Kim Jong Un's inspection of an east-coast naval base construction site on November 12. Kim was quoted in the KCNA report saying: "Modern naval base construction is a major core project directly linked to our state's safety and defense of maritime sovereignty, and must unconditionally be completed within the period decided by the party central and at the quality level demanded by the party central." The flag redesign, embedded in the revised constitution, was also prominent in state media coverage. N.K. News reports separately that the November 4 cluster warhead test shown by KCNA was faked using 2022 imagery. And North Korea's Foreign Minister Choe Son Hui issued a statement calling the G7's denuclearization demand "vicious criticism against Pyongyang's constitutional rights," adding that North Korea's nuclear status "can never be a subject of Western countries' debate." The bottom line: state media is projecting construction momentum and constitutional continuity under Kim while pushing back hard on external pressure.

5:41The round-up

The rest of the news, quickly.

South Korea confirmed today that Nvidia has reaffirmed its commitment to supply 260,000 graphics processing units to South Korea for artificial intelligence factory buildouts, despite the resignation of CEO Jensen Huang following affair allegations. The science ministry held a meeting with Nvidia this morning and confirmed that cooperation plans with Samsung Electronics, SK Group, Hyundai Motor Group, and Naver Cloud remain on track. Plans for NVLink integration with domestic AI chips, AI computing infrastructure, joint research, talent development, and holding the GTC conference in Korea will also continue as company-level commitments.

President Lee Jae-myung held a capital markets policy roundtable at the Korea Exchange today and said: "The most important thing is restoring market trust. When the capital market is trusted, our companies get properly valued and citizens and investors can invest with confidence." He pledged governance reform, stronger minority shareholder protections, and steps to close South Korea's valuation discount. The ruling Democratic Party and the government separately proposed requiring CEO appointments at bank holding companies to go through shareholder general meetings, with approval thresholds of two-thirds for initial appointments and three-quarters for reappointments.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, has shed 23 percent in the past month, ranking it as the worst-performing major market globally, with foreign investors withdrawing funds for nine consecutive months. The Financial Services Commission chairman said the market stabilization fund should be deployed only in "extremely urgent and exceptional circumstances." On trade, South Korea's export price index rose 0.7 percent in October, driven by semiconductor price gains, while import prices fell 1.8 percent.

President Lee also attended a groundbreaking for a Chungcheong mega-project today alongside Samsung chairman Lee Jae-yong, their first meeting in about three months. The Samsung chair called the projects "a historic challenge to reshape the future of South Korea."

South Korea's Trade and Industry Ministry has activated emergency management for the Czech nuclear project after domestic contractor negotiations collapsed. The minister pledged fair and transparent contractor selection and said the government will directly engage Czech counterparts on pending issues.

Korea's presidential climate envoy, Special Envoy Kang Geum-sil, delivered a speech at the Antalya Leaders Summit on November 12, announcing Korea's 2035 Nationally Determined Contribution and its K-Green Transformation strategy.

South Korea held its third consecutive separate memorial for the Sado mine today. Ambassador Lee Hyuk called the site one of government-arranged forced labor under Japanese colonial rule. Japan held a parallel separate event, maintaining its own framing.

On personnel, the government announced that Oh Hyun-ju, a former third deputy chief of the National Security Office, will be appointed as the 15th president of the Korea International Cooperation Agency on November 16.

A Gallup Korea poll taken November 3 to 5 put President Lee's approval at 41 percent, up from the prior reading. The Democratic Party stood at 36 percent, also up, while the People Power Party fell to 28 percent.

Russia and North Korea have filed applications to operate a 14-round-trip-daily bus service between Vladivostok and the North Korean port of Rason over a new bridge, covering a route of about 203 miles. Analysts note the buses will likely transport North Korean workers more than Russian tourists.

The Asan Institute published an analysis arguing that Democratic gains in the 2026 U.S. midterm elections create new congressional headwinds for wartime operational control transition and South Korea's aspirations for a nuclear-powered submarine.

9:43Capitol Hill

On Capitol Hill. The House passed legislation requiring small businesses to adopt mandatory cyber defense guidelines to counter threats from North Korea and other hostile state actors, including through illicit information technology worker infiltration. The bill now moves to the Senate.

10:02Korea and the World

Beyond the region. South Korea is maintaining its 100 million dollar aid commitment to Ukraine despite an unresolved diplomatic dispute over North Korean prisoners of war captured on the Ukrainian front. Seoul's presidential office said it expects "progress on issues with Ukraine after useful talks in Paris." Separately, Korea Hydro and Nuclear Power held a progress review with its Czech client on the nuclear project, with Seoul's industry ministry pledging strict accountability for any party obstructing the deal.

10:35Markets

Finally, the markets. The KOSPI closed down about 1 percent, at around 5,291. The dollar bought about 1,336 won. Brent crude was just under 96 dollars a barrel, up slightly on the day. The Bank of Korea's policy rate stands at 3.00 percent, last set in October.
