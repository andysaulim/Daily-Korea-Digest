0:00Today's headlines

This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Saturday, November 28.

Three things today: South Korea's intelligence service watches for a Kim Jong Un visit to China, North Korea and Russia hold their latest economic cooperation meeting, and President Lee breaks ground on a massive AI data center. The China story is the one to watch: Seoul's spy agency is tracking it in real time, even as the presidential office pushes back on what exactly was said at last week's summit. Tomorrow is North Korea's Aviation Day, and a renovated air base just came back online.

0:42NIS monitors signs of imminent Kim Jong Un visit to China

First, South Korea's National Intelligence Service says it is monitoring what it calls "various developments" around speculation that Kim Jong Un may be preparing another visit to China.

The speculation traces back to last week's APEC summit in Shenzhen, where Chinese President Xi Jinping briefed President Lee Jae-myung on North Korea-China relations. Lee's presidential office pushed back carefully: it said there was "no mention to the effect reported by the media," but confirmed it is closely monitoring the relationship.

This isn't the first time Kim has moved quietly toward China. On October 24, he made an unannounced one-day trip to Dandong, where he met Wang Huning, China's top political adviser.

What makes this worth watching for Korea policy is the timing. North Korea-Russia economic relationship is deepening fast, and a Kim visit to Beijing, if it happens, would signal where Pyongyang is positioning itself between its two major partners. The intelligence service hasn't characterized the likelihood, only that it's watching.

State media in Pyongyang, for its part, has said nothing. KCNA maintained complete silence on the speculation today, which is itself a data point.

1:55North Korea and Russia hold 13th economic cooperation committee…

Second, North Korea and Russia convened the 13th Meeting of their Intergovernmental Committee for Cooperation in Trade, Economy, Science and Technology in Pyongyang, agreeing to activate bilateral cooperation projects.

The meeting follows Russia's November 24 agreement to pay North Korea in fuel after a currency dispute, the latest transaction in a bilateral economic relationship that has been tracked since late 2022.

KCNA reported the meeting in the same news cycle as a congratulatory message Kim sent to the president of Mauritania, listing it separately rather than leading with it. That framing tells you something about how Pyongyang manages the optics of its Russia relationship, even as the substance deepens.

This is the 13th such committee meeting, and the agreement to "activate" joint projects suggests both sides want to move beyond individual transactions toward sustained cooperation. For policymakers, the fuel-for-cooperation model is now well established: Pyongyang is converting its military and political support for Moscow into durable economic arrangements.

Watch whether the economic committee outcomes translate into additional bilateral deals in the weeks ahead.

3:09Lee breaks ground on GS Group's 2.4 GW AI data center campus in…

Third, President Lee Jae-myung attended the groundbreaking of GS Group's AI data center campus in Donghae, Gangwon Province on Saturday, the first of the government's three declared megaprojects to begin construction.

GS Group Chairman Huh Tae-soo and Science and ICT Minister Bae Kyung-hoon were both on site. The facility is projected at up to 120 trillion won in total value, including GPU costs. The initial phase is 1.2 gigawatts, targeting completion and operation in 2029.

Lee framed the moment in security terms. He described the global AI technology race as "a silent war at the national level" and pledged to "mobilize all capabilities" to make South Korea a standard-setting country.

On social media, Lee noted that GS Group's decision to build in a region outside the capital, with support from Gangwon Province and Donghae City, allowed the project to move quickly. The Science and ICT Minister added a practical argument: power generated on the east coast can be used close to source, reducing long-distance transmission burdens.

GS Group Chairman Huh said the facility aims to make South Korea a "global hub of the AI era." The first 100 megawatt portion targets completion in the second quarter of 2028.

4:28Pyongyang

Now to Pyongyang.

KCNA led today with two Kim Jong Un stories. The first: Kim attended a performance by a Russian art troupe in Pyongyang, with state media framing the visit as a reaffirmation of North Korea-Russia friendship. That came alongside this week's intergovernmental economic meeting, which a lead story covered in full.

The second: Kim met participants of a Workers' Party cell leaders' conference and urged grassroots officials to faithfully transmit central directives, reinforcing ideological discipline from the top down.

The bottom line from state media today: Pyongyang is showcasing its Russia relationship on two tracks at once, cultural and economic, while saying absolutely nothing about speculation over a China visit.

5:15The round-up

The rest of the news, quickly.

On North Korea's military posture: satellite imagery from Planet Labs and Maxar shows MiG-23 fighters operating at Pukchang Air Base after a three-year renovation that extended the runway to 2,800 meters and added 16 canopy shelters. Tomorrow, November 29, is North Korea's Aviation Day, an occasion Kim has used in recent years for air force events. Separately, Daily N.K. reports that North Korea's IV Corps has completely overhauled its mission from coastal defense of the five islands to a broader offensive orientation, incorporating drones and new weapons systems.

On South Korea's economy: the Bank of Korea raised its 2027 GDP growth forecast from 2.9 percent to 4.1 percent on November 26, a 1.2 percentage-point upward revision described as the largest since December 2008. The 2026 forecast was also raised, from 3.3 percent to 3.9 percent. On the household side, though, three Bank of Korea rate hikes have pushed average fixed-rate mortgage rates to 5.13 percent, rising for 13 consecutive months. Some forecasts project mortgage rates reaching 8 percent in 2027.

In a Gallup Korea poll taken November 27, 7 in 10 stock investors in South Korea said they lost money in 2026. Presidential approval, in the most recent Gallup poll taken November 3 to 5, stood at 41 percent.

On the National Intelligence Service director nomination: Kang Kum-sil, the nominee to lead the National Intelligence Service, responded Saturday to opposition allegations of pro-China bias. She said her advisory roles in Chinese provincial governments in 2013 and 2014 were unpaid and ceremonial, taken during the Park Geun-hye government's period of warm South Korea-China relations.

The Korea Coast Guard has imposed 12.7 billion won in security deposits on 13 Chinese fishing vessels caught illegally fishing since an amended exclusive economic zone law took effect on May 12. None of the vessels apprehended this year have been able to pay the higher amounts.

In tech, Nvidia is working with Kioxia on AI server memory solutions that could serve as an alternative to South Korean memory chips from Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix. And shares of Rainbow Robotics rose 30 percent in a single week on continued investor sentiment around Samsung's robotics investments.

The Sejong Institute published a brief arguing that the domestic political constraints of Prime Minister Takaichi Sanae's administration will shape the near-term trajectory of South Korea-Japan relations in ways Seoul must anticipate. And CSIS released a report from its CSIS-Defense Acquisition Program Administration 2026 conference arguing that the U.S.-South Korea defense industrial partnership requires structural transformation to meet evolving security requirements.

8:20Capitol Hill

On Capitol Hill.

The White House and Congress are pressing Seoul over digital market regulations, with U.S. pressure described as a "diplomatic factor" in the Korea Fair Trade Commission's enforcement actions against major technology companies. Both chambers are involved, and the pressure is being tracked as a live diplomatic issue between Washington and Seoul.

8:42Korea and the World

Beyond the region.

Iran has warned South Korea against expanding its naval role in the Gulf as Seoul readies a larger military presence in the region. At the same time, South Korea secured a final legal victory in a 574 million dollar investment dispute with Iran. And Korea Hydro and Nuclear Power is intensifying efforts to advance its nuclear power plant project in the Czech Republic.

At the International Atomic Energy Agency, the United States backed the International Atomic Energy Agency's authority to negotiate nuclear safeguards for South Korea's planned nuclear-powered submarine program, after China raised objections. The dispute reflects Beijing's resistance to South Korea's naval nuclear ambitions.

9:28Markets

Finally, the markets.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed at about 4,916 on November 27, up about 4.3 percent. The won traded at about 1,334 to the dollar on November 28, up about 0.7 percent. Brent crude was at about 95 dollars a barrel, up less than 1 percent.
