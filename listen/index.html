0:00Today's headlines

This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Saturday, October 24.

Three things today: Kim Jong Un makes a surprise trip to China, he calls for a military revolution at home, and Seoul proposes border talks with Pyongyang. Tomorrow is the anniversary of China's entry into the Korean War, and that date appears to be at the center of all three stories.

0:30Kim Jong Un makes unannounced visit to Dandong via new Yalu bridge…

First, Kim Jong Un crossed into China today, and he did it without any announcement from either side.

Kim crossed into the Chinese city of Dandong on October 24, using the newly opened New Yalu River Bridge. South Korea's Unification Ministry confirmed the visit. The Presidential Office says it is monitoring the situation, quote, with all possibilities in mind, and noted that neither Beijing nor Pyongyang has made any official statement.

Democratic Party National Assembly Intelligence Committee member Yun Geon-young told SBS that Kim likely met with Chinese Premier Li Qiang and returned to North Korea the same day. Yun said the National Intelligence Service briefed the committee on the visit and confirmed that rumors about Kim's health circulating on social media are false.

The timing is deliberate. October 25 is the anniversary of the Chinese People's Volunteer Army's entry into the Korean War, and Seoul's Unification Ministry cited that date as the likely occasion for the visit. A Chinese veterans affairs delegation had already arrived in Pyongyang ahead of the anniversary, underscoring the political choreography around the day.

Looking further ahead, Yonhap reports that the November Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen has fueled speculation about a possible North Korea-U.S. leaders meeting. Analysts quoted by Yonhap say Kim's China trip may reinforce North Korea-China alliance rather than signal any substantive diplomatic shift.

Security preparations in Dandong were visible days in advance. Kyodo reports that strict traffic and hotel controls were imposed in the city three days before Kim arrived, with armored vehicles deployed near the Korean War memorial.

Kim is in China, and Beijing is not saying why.

2:19Kim Jong Un calls for 'military revolution' at KPA company commanders…

Second, just before he left for Dandong, Kim Jong Un was rallying his military.

Kim chaired the Third Meeting of Company Leaders of the Korean People's Army in Pyongyang on October 22 and 23. State media reported that he called for perfecting low-level leadership, improving training systems, and building what he called the world's strongest armed forces. Kim framed the drive in a speech as a, quote, military revolution, aimed at unit-level command.

The conference came immediately before his departure for China, combining a domestic military mobilization message with a diplomatic move toward Beijing. The Korea Chair's analysis of state media coverage notes that KCNA reported the commanders conference at the same time external sources confirmed the surprise China visit, which it calls a dual signal.

The Atlantic Council published analysis today arguing that North Korea has accrued disproportionate strategic gains from its partnership with Russia, including battlefield experience for Korean People's Army units and reverse technology transfer, gains the author says compound over time and elevate security risks across the Indo-Pacific.

The commanders conference and the China visit together suggest Kim is managing two tracks at once: sharpening his military at home while signaling to Beijing.

3:41Seoul proposes military talks with Pyongyang to address border…

Third, Seoul is seeking to talk, and Pyongyang is not answering.

South Korea's Ministry of National Defense proposed military-to-military talks with North Korea to resolve disputes over the Military Demarcation Line and ease rising border tensions. The Washington Post reported the proposal. Yonhap says North Korea had not responded by the following day.

The backdrop makes the silence louder. Dong-A Ilbo reports that North Korea has laid 960,000 mines near the Demilitarized Zone since 2024. Kim Yo Jong has been conducting border fortification inspections and issuing loudspeaker warnings. Seoul's proposal lands against that backdrop.

A CSIS analysis published today examines the growing diplomatic rift between South Korea and Ukraine over North Korean soldiers captured on the Russian front. The piece maps Seoul's dilemma: engaging Kyiv risks escalating tensions with Pyongyang, while refusing risks straining the relationship with Ukraine and European allies. That analysis connects directly to reporting in Chosun Ilbo today on the Lee Jae-myung government's prisoner-of-war dilemma.

Seoul proposed talks. Pyongyang laid 960,000 mines and has not replied.

4:59Pyongyang

Now to Pyongyang.

KCNA reported no top articles in today's delta, but Kim was active on both fronts the brief covers. State media reported his KPA commanders conference on October 22 and 23, framing it as Kim's push for a military revolution at the unit level. External confirmation of his China visit came simultaneously, which the Korea Chair's bottom line calls a dual signal combining domestic military mobilization messaging with an unannounced diplomatic move. Kim's last public appearance was today. No silence flag is raised.

5:36The round-up

The rest of the news, quickly.

Prime Minister Han Seong-sook attended an advance memorial at Seoul Plaza for the Itaewon disaster, ahead of the fourth anniversary on October 29. Han bowed in apology on behalf of the government and pledged support for the ongoing special investigation committee and a memorial foundation. The victim toll has risen to 160.

South Korea's Democratic Party advanced proceedings to investigate the chief justice, with impeachment calls emerging. The People Power Party objected, calling the move Democratic Party dictatorship. The dispute reflects ongoing friction in the current national audit session.

The Bank of Korea's governor, Shin Hyun-song, told parliamentary auditors that a return to ultra-low interest rates is difficult, given expectations of a high-growth, high-rate environment. He also warned the government not to expand fiscal spending premised on semiconductor tax revenue alone.

SK Hynix will suspend the U.S. stock market listing of its NAND subsidiary Solidigm after Seoul authorities flagged concerns about a parent-subsidiary dual-listing overhang. SK Hynix shares rose more than 4 percent on the announcement.

South Korea will ease restrictions on diesel exports after domestic diesel sales fell 7.6 percent, reflecting a shift toward electric vehicles and reduced industrial demand.

The Lazarus Group, the North Korean hacking collective, is exploiting a PHP programming language security flaw to stage cyberattacks, per new analysis. Separately, police are investigating North Korean information technology workers who infiltrated South Korean organizations by falsifying their identities.

A Gallup Korea poll taken October 13 to 15 put presidential approval at 39 percent, up. The Democratic Party stood at 36 percent, and the People Power Party at 27 percent, both trending up. Independents fell to 29 percent.

Foreign Minister Cho Hyun, in remarks delivered by Deputy Foreign Minister Kim Jin-a at the 33rd Japan-Korea Business Council dinner, said bilateral relations have reached the most stable development track since the Lee government took office, citing supply chain cooperation and shuttle diplomacy.

38 North published satellite imagery analysis today documenting North Korea's rural construction drive under Kim Jong Un's 20 by 10 regional development plan, showing visible changes across the countryside.

Looking ahead: the Chinese Communist Party plenum opens October 26, running through October 29, overlapping with Kim's China signaling. South Korea's third-quarter GDP data is due for release around October 30.

8:19Capitol Hill

On Capitol Hill.

The text of House bill H.R. 9963, the North Korean FAKER Act, has been published on Congress.gov as part of the 119th Congress. The bill addresses fraudulent activities by North Korean workers or agents. Separately, the Treasury Department has proposed rules curbing American high-technology investments in China, Russia, and North Korea, covering semiconductors, artificial intelligence, and other strategic technologies.

8:50Korea and the World

Beyond the region.

Ukraine is pressing Seoul to accept or facilitate the transfer of North Korean soldiers captured on the Russian front, and the Lee government has not resolved how to respond, according to Chosun Ilbo. A Vietnamese diplomat says bilateral trade with South Korea is on track to surpass 100 billion dollars. And Poland has announced it will integrate Norwegian remote weapon systems onto K2 tanks of South Korean origin in its fleet.

9:20Markets

Finally, the markets. Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed at about 5,550, up about 0.3 percent as of October 23. Brent crude rose about 3 percent to about 117 dollars a barrel. The won traded at about 1,325 to the dollar.
