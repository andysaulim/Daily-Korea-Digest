0:00Today's headlines

This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Tuesday, November 17.

Three things today: Trump floats a Kim Jong Un meeting at APEC, North Korea's Jo Yong Won pledges support to Putin in Moscow, and Lee Jae-myung and Japan's Takaichi hold their fourth summit in Manila. The APEC summit opens tomorrow in Shenzhen, and Kim Jong Un's attendance, which would trigger both a Trump meeting and a Putin meeting, remains unconfirmed tonight. Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin is expected to announce specific anti-cheating measures today, two days before the college entrance exam.

0:43Trump floats Kim Jong Un meeting at APEC

First, the APEC wildcard. Donald Trump told reporters at the White House before leaving for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen that he may meet Kim Jong Un, saying Kim's attendance was, in his words, "up to President Xi." The Kremlin added its own signal, saying Putin would meet Kim "of course" if he attends. That's two of the world's most consequential leaders publicly welcoming a Kim appearance, before Pyongyang has said a word about it.

South Korea's Foreign Ministry responded carefully. A ministry official said Seoul and Washington are in close communication on the possibility, and that Seoul "supports dialogue between the United States and North Korea." But analysts quoted in the Korea Times say there are no signs of the diplomatic groundwork you'd expect before a summit, and they warn that a deal struck without South Korean input could leave Seoul facing new security risks. North Korea has stated in recent weeks that recognition of its nuclear status is a precondition for any engagement.

Meanwhile, North Korea content has vanished from Chinese social media. Posts on Weibo, Rednote, and WeChat have been disappearing since at least late October, with searches for "Kim Jong Un" and "Pyongyang" returning no results. N.K. News attributes that crackdown to heightened bilateral sensitivity and possible preparations for a Kim visit to China. Kim himself spent November 15 and 16 inspecting the new Mount Phothae ski resort near the Chinese border, calling tourism a "major stimulant to regional development." KCNA published over 70 photographs. The border location, the Chinese social media blackout, and the Trump and Putin remarks are all pointing in the same direction, but Pyongyang has confirmed nothing.

2:32Kim Jong Un's top aide Jo Yong Won meets Putin in Moscow, pledges…

Second, Jo Yong Won in Moscow. While Trump was floating a summit, North Korea's number-two party figure was already meeting Russia's president. Jo Yong Won, a Presidium member of the Political Bureau and secretary of the Workers' Party of Korea Central Committee, met Vladimir Putin at the Kremlin on Monday. Jo vowed to support Moscow's struggle against what he called U.S. "neocolonialism and neo-Nazism." He also met the speaker of Russia's State Duma and addressed a general meeting in Russia.

The timing is striking. Those talks took place just days before the planned Trump-Xi-Putin meeting at APEC in Shenzhen. Jo's visit is a public show of alignment, North Korea signaling solidarity with Russia at precisely the moment the major powers are converging in China. The Kremlin's statement that Putin would meet Kim "of course" at APEC came the same day, suggesting the Moscow talks and the APEC signals are part of a coordinated message. The RAND National Defense Research Institute published analysis this week arguing that closer ties among China, Russia, North Korea, and Iran have made the existing U.S.-South Korea combined deterrence posture strategically insufficient. That report calls for both countries to reassign roles and responsibilities in response. The Jo-Putin meeting is exactly the kind of event that analysis had in mind.

3:57Lee and Takaichi hold fourth bilateral summit in Manila, confirm…

Third, Lee and Takaichi in Manila. South Korea and Japan held their fourth bilateral summit since Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi took office. President Lee Jae-myung and Takaichi met on the sidelines of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations summit in Manila. Lee said South Korea-Japan relations have "brightened more than ever," pointing to record-high favorable public sentiment between the two countries in a recent survey. He confirmed he will visit Japan in January as invited.

The two leaders agreed to strengthen energy and economic security cooperation given Middle East developments, and to deepen strategic communications. Lee posted afterward that "continued shuttle diplomacy confirms that trust between our two countries is growing stronger." That is the fourth bilateral summit in roughly a year, a pace that reflects how much the relationship has shifted. The January visit will be one to watch.

4:55Pyongyang

Now to Pyongyang. State media's headline story was Kim Jong Un's inspection of the Mount Phothae ski resort near the Chinese border on November 15 and 16, a domestic welfare and development story, though the location has drawn outside attention given the speculation about a China visit. The other notable item from outside North Korea: Daily N.K. reports that Chinese used vehicles have been entering North Korea through Hyesan customs since mid-October, ending a prolonged freeze on vehicle imports. Jo Yong Won's Moscow visit dominated external coverage. The bottom line from state media: Pyongyang led with domestic tourism and welfare content at home, while projecting strategic alignment with Russia to the outside world, days before APEC.

5:41The round-up

The rest of the news, quickly.

A Russian military aircraft entered South Korea's air defense identification zone without notice early Tuesday. South Korea's Joint Chiefs confirmed the incursion; fighters were deployed and the aircraft exited without entering territorial airspace.

South Korea signed the STARLIFT space launch cooperation memorandum of understanding at NATO headquarters in Brussels on October 29, becoming the only non-NATO founding member of the multinational framework. The Defense Ministry says the arrangement will diversify military space access and create export opportunities for Korean launch and satellite firms.

Deputy Foreign Minister Kim Jina met U.S. Under Secretary of State for Economic Affairs Jacob Helberg in San Francisco on Monday to discuss artificial intelligence cooperation and implementation of the 350 billion dollar strategic investment pledge.

On the economy, Seoul is approaching a milestone. Industry Minister Kim Jeong-gwan visited Incheon port this week marking the moment South Korea approaches one trillion dollars in annual exports, which would make it only the fourth country to reach that threshold, alongside Germany, the United States, and China. The minister called for identifying new export growth drivers beyond semiconductors.

The minister also convened a critical minerals public-private strategy meeting Tuesday and announced the launch of an interagency Resource Security Council and a new industry resource security division, citing inadequacy of past overseas resource development efforts.

South Korea was elected to the International Telecommunication Union Council for the tenth consecutive time at the ITU Plenipotentiary Conference in Doha. Park Jong-min was separately elected to the ITU Radio Regulations Board.

The National Assembly Budget and Accounts Special Committee began substantive review of the fiscal year 2027 budget. Ten standing committees have collectively proposed a net increase of approximately 25 trillion won, including an 8 trillion won increase from the Welfare Committee.

In a Gallup Korea poll taken November 3 to 5, President Lee Jae-myung's approval rose two points to 41 percent. The Democratic Party edged up to 36 percent, while the People Power Party fell to 28 percent, level with unaffiliated voters.

Japan denied entry to Cho Kuk at Haneda Airport. Japanese authorities cited unspecified grounds under immigration law; Cho's party called it unprecedented for a public figure attending an academic event. Seoul expressed regret and requested an explanation from Tokyo.

Korea joined the Carbon Market Coalition at a high-level climate meeting in Antalya on November 16, becoming the 13th member alongside the EU and China. Climate Minister Kim Sunghwan announced the membership.

Two days before the CSAT exam, Prime Minister Han Seong-sook chaired an interagency meeting and announced an emergency task force on AI-assisted cheating. Han said at a public address: "Smart devices will be completely banned from CSAT exam rooms; glasses and watches will be inspected in full; buttons on clothing will be covered with stickers." President Lee chaired a video meeting on CSAT security from Manila the same evening, ordering a zero-tolerance response.

9:03Korea and the World

Beyond the region. Seoul will provide 2 million dollars through UNICEF to pre-position relief supplies in Peru ahead of forecast El Nino flooding, in line with United Nations anticipatory action funding allocated in July. In Europe, Czech nuclear regulators began their first on-site quality assurance inspection of the Dukovany nuclear plant project, where Korea Hydro and Nuclear Power is the selected contractor. South Korea's National Assembly is separately weighing a hearing on that deal.

9:34Markets

Finally, the markets. Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, fell about 2.6 percent Tuesday to close at about 4,968, breaking below 5,000 for the first time since February. The trigger, according to SBS, was new U.S. restrictions on artificial intelligence semiconductor exports to China, which hit Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix hard. The dollar bought about 1,336 won, up slightly. Brent crude was just under 93 dollars a barrel.
