●Top Stories
DPRK

North Korea tests 'Zam-2.0' AI-equipped torpedo — sixth weapons event in October

KCNA reported Saturday that North Korea conducted an underwater test of the 'Zam-2.0' torpedo on Thursday, describing it as a high-speed precision-guided munition equipped with artificial intelligence and hailing it as a major naval breakthrough — the first DPRK weapon publicly designated with AI targeting capability. The test is the sixth DPRK weapons event in October and follows the Oct 15-16 submarine-launched cruise missile test confirmed earlier this week, extending a sustained testing surge that began in early October. No images were released by KCNA, and no DPRK official, including Kim Jong Un, has been publicly associated with the torpedo test as of Saturday morning.

Japan-Korea

Seoul expresses 'deep disappointment' as Japan PM Takaichi sends Yasukuni offering, forgoing visit

South Korea's government expressed 'deep disappointment and regret' Saturday after Japan PM Takaichi Sanae sent a ritual offering (masakaki) to Yasukuni Shrine, opting against a personal visit. Separately, Takaichi told the Diet's budget committee she will uphold the 1995 Murayama Declaration — a reversal from her earlier on-record criticism of the statement — saying 'the position of past cabinets will remain unchanged' and that 'the Murayama Declaration is of course included.' The dual signal — shrine offering paired with Murayama reaffirmation — tracks the pattern of LDP leaders managing domestic nationalist constituencies while seeking to limit diplomatic fallout with Seoul.

NK-Russia-China

Belarus opens Pyongyang embassy — first permanent mission, nearly three months behind schedule

Belarus's foreign ministry announced late Friday it opened its embassy in North Korea this month, along with a simultaneous mission in Myanmar — framing both openings as part of a deliberate policy to strengthen presence among what Minsk termed countries on 'the axis of distant diplomacy.' The ministry did not disclose the opening date, location, or name of its ambassador to the DPRK; North Korean state media had not reported the opening as of Saturday. The announcement follows Belarus FM Maxim Ryzhenkov's August visit to Pyongyang, where he met Foreign Minister Choe Son Hui and discussed mutual visa-free arrangements, and a September sideline meeting at the UN General Assembly between Ryzhenkov and DPRK Vice FM Kim Son Gyong on the embassy question.
