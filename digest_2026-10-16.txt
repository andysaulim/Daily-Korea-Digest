This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair. It's Friday, October 16.

In today's brief: NK-China bridge opens; SLCM test confirmed; U.S.-South Korea 2+2 finance debut; KOSPI rebounds 2%; and OPCON row deepens.

Our top story: North Korean Missile Administration General Director Jang Chang Ha told state media it is 'hard to believe' South Korea's hypersonic glide vehicle underwent actual hypersonic flight, drawing a swift rebuttal from Seoul's defense ministry, which confirmed the HGV achieved hypersonic speeds using domestically developed heat-resistant materials. The Defense Ministry spokesperson posted that the 'soot-free thermal protection' is itself a design achievement. The exchange follows North Korea's own SLCM test on October 15 and represents Pyongyang's first direct public challenge to a specific South Korea weapons test outcome.

Also making news: North Korea and China opened the New Yalu River Bridge at Sinuiju–Dandong on October 15 in a ceremony confirmed by both sides via KCNA and Chinese state media, with trucks crossing in both directions — more than a decade after construction was completed. Chinese social media users hailed the opening as a 'historic moment,' with footage of convoys circulating widely and users expressing hope for resumed tourism. The bridge is the largest land crossing between the two countries since the 1940s and brings the Sinuiju–Dandong crossing to full operational status alongside the recently opened Tumen River road bridge at Tumangang–Khasan.

And one more top story: South Korea Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-il and U.S. counterparts convened in Bangkok for the first-ever joint 2+2 meeting of finance and monetary authorities from both countries, pledging rapid coordinated response in the event of financial shocks. The format, combining fiscal and central bank officials from both sides, is new to the bilateral relationship. The meeting occurred on the sidelines of the G20, where Lee also called for collective action on global imbalances.

Here's what else moved overnight. U.S. ties with South Korea hit low point over intelligence row. Bloomberg cites deepening friction over intelligence-sharing disputes.

Bipartisan U.S. Senate bill seeks to reaffirm 'ironclad' nuclear deterrence commitment to South Korea. Two senators introduced bipartisan legislation this week.

South Korea reviews expanding military mission in Middle East, report says. Seoul weighing Cheonghae unit scope extension toward Strait of Hormuz.

Special counsel seeks 30-year prison term for ex-president over North Korea drone incursion. Prosecution demands 30-year sentence in ongoing trial.

Ukraine says it busted military equipment theft scheme tied to NK-Russia arms broker. SSU and State Bureau of Investigation detained two individuals.

North Korean GPS jamming targeting South Korea has plummeted in 2026, South Korea data shows. CRMS official confirmed decline but declined to give most recent date.

Now to Pyongyang. State media's top stories today. North Korea says it tested two types of submarine-launched strategic cruise missiles. KCNA confirmed the SLCM test — the fifth North Korea test event in October and the first underwater launch in nearly three years — releasing images of the launch. North Korean Missile Administration General Director Jang Chang Ha questions South Korea's hypersonic glide vehicle test. North Korea says opening ceremony was held for new bridge to China. The bottom line: KCNA's confirmation of two submarine-launched cruise missile types — the first underwater launch in nearly three years — and a named missile chief's public challenge to Seoul's HGV test mark the fifth North Korea test event in October. Watch for Kim Jong Un's first confirmed appearance since October 13 and for any KCNA reaction to Trump's repeated signals about a potential Kim meeting. Kim Jong Un was last seen in public 3 days ago.

The number of the day. 113,000: Jobs added in South Korea in September 2026. The third consecutive month above 100,000 new jobs, sustaining the labor-market recovery that underpins Finance Ministry assessments of solid economic growth driven by exports.

In Seoul, the government side. Kang Shin-chul visited the Marine Corps Command and Northwest Islands Defense Command on October 16, reviewing operational readiness and announcing plans to restructure the Marines as an operational command capable of modern maneuver warfare. He described the Marines as standing at a 'historic turning point' and pledged to advance the quasi-fourth-service reform.

Lee Jae-myung called on the National Assembly to pursue constitutional revision that embeds the spirit of South Korea democratization movements, including the April 19 Revolution and May 18 Gwangju Uprising, in the constitution's preamble or text. The call, made on the anniversary of the Buma Democratic Uprising, follows Lee's social media post invoking multiple democracy milestones.

President Lee Jae-myung appointed a new vice minister of industry and a new deputy head of the financial supervisory authority on Friday; the names of both appointees appear in the Yonhap source article.

At the National Assembly's Legislation and Judiciary Committee: Parties clash over constitutionality of suspending President Lee's ongoing criminal trials under Article 84. At the Seoul High Court audit, Democratic Party members argued that Article 84's bar on 'prosecution' of a sitting president covers both indictment and ongoing trial proceedings, while People Power Party members called the trial suspension an illegal workaround. Seoul High Court Chief Kim Dae-woong stated the suspension was based on the constitutional provision and was not judicial dereliction.

At the National Assembly's Education Committee: National audit questions Seoul National University on law school blind evaluation, regional bias, and 'Xi Jinping reading room'. SNU President Yoo Hong-lim faced bipartisan criticism over law school admissions: DP members cited 94 of 152 final admits coming from SNU itself, and PPP members raised concerns about a campus reading room stocked with Xi Jinping materials. Former PPP lawmaker Yoo Seong-min's daughter's alleged hiring preference at Incheon National University was also scrutinized, with four individuals including the university president referred to prosecutors.

On the economy. The KOSPI closed up more than 2 percent on Friday, recovering from a 7.57% plunge the prior session, as bargain hunters returned to chip stocks and the won strengthened to a near two-year high against the dollar on Fed outlook signals and foreign inflows. KRX activated a buy-side sidecar on KOSDAQ during the session as the index's sharp increase triggered the circuit mechanism.

South Korea added 113,000 jobs in September, marking the third consecutive month of net job creation above 100,000; the finance ministry cited ongoing export-driven recovery as the basis for solid labor market performance.

South Korea's economy maintains solid recovery on exports, finance ministry says. The Finance Ministry's October 16 assessment confirmed export-led growth continuing through the third quarter of 2026, with import prices rebounding in September for the first time in four months on higher oil — a potential inflation risk heading into the Bank of Korea's next rate-setting cycle.

Elsewhere in the region. Nikkei's policy analysis argues North Korea-Russia strategic relationship has been institutionalized — through arms transfers, infrastructure, road and rail border links, and military-technical cooperation — in ways designed to persist regardless of the Ukraine conflict's outcome. The assessment aligns with the CSIS NK-Russia Axis research framework tracking 268+ verified cooperation events since 2022.

How is the public reading all this? In the latest Gallup Korea poll, from week of-10-16, President Lee's approval is 39 percent, up from the poll before. By party: the Democratic Party at 35 percent, the People Power Party at 26 percent, and independents at 31 percent.

In their own words. Lee Jae-myung said: "The transfer of operational control — what changes and what doesn't — we need to have an honest conversation about that with our ally."

U.S. Ambassador to South Korea said: "Pleased to join the U.S.-Korea Business Council and the Federation of Korean Industries for the U.S.-Korea Joint Plenary. Korean investment continues to strengthen the U.S. economy, support American jobs, and deepen commercial ties between our two countries."

A few more things worth knowing. 38 North analysis argues that following the October 5 South Korean military finding that North Korean forces deliberately laid landmines near the military demarcation line, the absence of a functioning inter-Korean verification mechanism leaves both sides unable to confirm or dispute actions in the DMZ — a structural problem for any future arms-control or tension-reduction process.

A school for North Korean defectors found a new home. Then the backlash came. Seoul's only accredited school for defector children, Yeomyung School, secured a permanent campus via a deal with the government and Hyundai, but faced community opposition; the case illustrates persistent social friction around defector integration in South Korea.

Top Korea tech fund favors non-memory stocks in AI value chain. A leading Korean technology fund is rotating toward non-memory semiconductor and AI-infrastructure names, reflecting expectations that the AI buildout benefits system-level chip designers and software firms as much as memory makers.
