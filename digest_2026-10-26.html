Newsis, DPRK

North Korean 122mm howitzers aimed south spotted at western border for second day

ROK Defense Minister Kang Shin-chul told the National Assembly defense committee that North Korea's deployment of 122mm self-propelled howitzers at the western frontline, with the barrels directed toward South Korea, carried 'various intentions' and he was 'watching closely,' while noting no unusual movements had been observed.

Cameras captured the howitzers at the Kaepung County coastal area of Hwanghae Province on a second consecutive day, with propaganda slogans visible on the equipment. Kim Yo Jong had warned on October 22 that new response systems along the full border line would be placed on mission-standby.

NK News, DPRK

North Korea sends UN Security Council letter claiming DMZ mine blast was Seoul fabrication

North Korea sent a letter to the UN Security Council president accusing South Korea of staging the DMZ land mine explosion that injured ROK troops, after Seoul had sent its own letter blaming Pyongyang for the blast and heightened border tensions.

The Korean Central News Agency released a statement on the letter, which came as both governments traded accusations before the UN body. Daily NK separately reported that Pyongyang is using the blast, which it claims was fabricated, to justify intensified military drills for high school students in North Pyongan Province.

Korea Times, US-Korea

Korea-US Strategic Investment Corporation receives Aa2 and AA sovereign-linked credit ratings

The Korea-US Strategic Investment Corporation (KUIC) received its first credit ratings since launching earlier this year: Moody's assigned Aa2 with a stable outlook and S&P Global Ratings assigned AA, both in line with South Korea's sovereign ratings.

Moody's cited the ROK government's 'very high willingness and ability to provide timely support, given the strong credit linkages between KUIC and the Government of Korea.' KUIC is the state-run vehicle created by the National Assembly's Special Investment Act of March 12, 2026 to implement Seoul's $350 billion investment pledge to the United States.

Yonhap, ROK Economy

Lee says CPTPP membership unavoidable but rules out joining during his term

President Lee Jae-myung told a meeting with farmers and fishermen that joining the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP) was 'unavoidable' for South Korea but said the country would not apply this year, pledging to communicate with agricultural stakeholders before setting a direction.

Lee said South Korea would 'not unilaterally impose sacrifice on people with weak power and small voices' in pursuit of export market expansion. The Korea.net government account separately posted CPTPP fact-checks directed at the farming community following Lee's remarks.
