●Top Stories
DPRK

North Korea fires multiple ballistic missiles — fourth test in a month — after Workers' Party anniversary

Kim Jong Un's military fired multiple ballistic missiles from the Pyongyang area toward the East Sea at approximately 9:20 a.m. KST on October 12, with ROK JCS confirming at least some flew over 900 km; precise specifications remain under joint US-ROK analysis. The launch came one day after the subdued 81st Workers' Party founding anniversary concert and nine days after the previous IRBM firing drill. The ROK Presidential National Security Office convened an emergency meeting and demanded North Korea immediately halt what it called illegal launches; China's Foreign Ministry spokeswoman said Beijing had "no new comment" on the firing.

NK-Russia-China

Russian EMERCOM Minister Kurenkov arrives in Pyongyang for first-ever emergency management visit

Alexander Kurenkov, head of Russia's Emergencies Ministry (EMERCOM), landed at Pyongyang's Sunan International Airport around 10 p.m. KST Sunday, according to Flightradar24 data and DPRK state media, marking the ministry's first-ever visit to North Korea. The trip follows a Moscow-Pyongyang agreement to train North Korean rescuers in Russia; Yonhap reported the delegation will discuss cooperation on disaster management projects. The visit extends the pattern of Russian ministerial engagement with Pyongyang across civilian technical domains alongside ongoing military-technical cooperation.

Business

KOSPI falls over 3% in tech selloff; BOK governor defends back-to-back rate hikes

Seoul shares plunged more than 3 percent Monday, extending losses driven by technology sector weakness amid Middle East tensions and inflation concerns, with the KOSPI closing at 6,384.24 (-3.65%). Bank of Korea Governor Rhee Chang-yong said back-to-back rate hikes had bought the central bank time to respond to financial risks, defending the recent tightening cycle even as equity markets fell sharply. The won simultaneously hit a near two-year high against the dollar, driven by lower oil prices and dollar sales, creating a split-screen of currency strength and equity weakness.
