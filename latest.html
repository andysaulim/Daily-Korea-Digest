SBS / NK News, DPRK

North Korea fires two ballistic missiles from Muncheon; Naenara publishes revised constitution text

North Korea launched two ballistic missiles from Muncheon into the East Sea on Nov 11, with a flight distance of approximately 350 km, according to JTBC and Yonhap reporting.

Separately, North Korea's official web portal Naenara published the full text of the constitution amended by the Supreme People's Assembly on March 23, 2026, formalizing Kim Jong Un's expanded authority and the country's redefinition of its territory to exclude the South, the first time the constitution section had been restored to the portal since North Korea pledged to rewrite the document in early 2024, per NK News. The launch is the latest in a series of weapons tests over the past week, all of which have been kept off the domestic news agenda.

Foreign Ministry, US-Korea, Primary

FM Cho Hyun meets SecState Rubio on G7 sidelines; agrees to advance Lee-Trump summit agreements

ROK Foreign Minister Cho Hyun held a bilateral meeting with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio on Nov 10 on the sidelines of the G7 Foreign Ministers' Meeting in Paris, exchanging views on overall bilateral relations, high-level exchanges, and North Korea, according to MOFA. Rubio separately noted at a press conference that South Korea was among the partners invited to the G7 meeting.

How Lax Enforcement in Laos Undermines Security on the Korean Peninsula. Young argues that North Korean IT workers operating from Laos, which is far more susceptible to US and ROK diplomatic pressure than Beijing or Moscow, represent a high-priority target for disrupting the DPRK's estimated $250-600 million annual IT-worker revenue scheme. The March 2026 Treasury sanctioning of Yun Song Guk, a North Korean IT worker unit leader based in Boten, Laos, is cited as a template for action. The piece connects to broader concerns about North Korean cyber capacity following the $6.36 billion Binance hack. (Benjamin R. Young, KEIA)
Trump Is Facing a Very Different North Korea. The Foreign Affairs piece argues that the DPRK Kim Jong Un now leads, with an expanded nuclear program, the March 2026 constitutional revision formalizing the two-state framework, and deep Russia entanglement, presents far more constrained diplomatic space for a potential Trump-Kim engagement than existed during 2018-2019. Trump's August 2026 Truth Social post announcing scaled-back US-ROK exercises, citing his 'very good relationship with Kim Jong Un,' is examined as a signal of the president's intent. The piece is directly relevant to today's NK News reporting on how Democratic control of Congress could limit any deal's scope. (Foreign Affairs)
One Year After Busan: Has China Kept Its Rare Earth Promises?. The CSIS analysis finds that Chinese rare earth exports to the United States remain well below levels recorded before Beijing imposed sweeping export restrictions in April 2025, despite the Geneva, London, and Busan Agreements intended to restore supplies. While China's overall controlled rare earth shipments have remained relatively stable, the selective reduction to the US and Japan suggests continued strategic discretion. With the broader US-China trade truce expiring January 10 2027, the report asks whether Washington can convert diplomatic agreements into reliable access or whether Beijing will continue weaponizing critical mineral dominance.
