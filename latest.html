Rubio: Kim seeks nukes for survival, not expansion, but North Korea still on APEC agenda

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Kim Jong Un's nuclear ambitions differ from Iran's because Kim seeks regime survival rather than territorial expansion, telling a Washington convention that Kim 'just wants to survive in his regime' and 'is not expansionist.'

The remarks came as the first-ever Trump-Xi-Putin trilateral summit, set for November 19 in Shenzhen on the sidelines of APEC, raised Seoul's fears that North Korea's nuclear program could be discussed without Korean input. South Korean FM Cho Hyun separately warned Beijing the summit must not become 'a second Yalta Conference,' conveying Seoul's position directly to Chinese FM Wang Yi in a November 14 meeting in Beijing.

CNN / Korea Herald / FT / Reuters, ROK Politics

South Korea mandates glasses inspection at CSAT after AI cheating confession

South Korea will inspect all glasses worn by approximately 550,000 students sitting the College Scholastic Ability Test on Thursday after the top scorer in the national tax accountant certification exam confessed Sunday to cheating using a miniature camera, a smartphone and AI.

Prime Minister Han Seong-sook convened an emergency meeting Monday, directing officials to ensure all smart devices are surrendered before the test and asking the Ministry of Science and ICT to examine whether public Wi-Fi could be disabled at all 1,312 test sites. Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin told parliament smart glasses have been added to the prohibited items list and that cheating with smart devices could carry criminal punishment.

NK News / Hankyoreh, China-Korea

Seoul asks Beijing to help return North Korea to talks before APEC summit

FM Cho Hyun asked Chinese FM Wang Yi during a November 14 meeting in Beijing to help bring North Korea back to the negotiation table, stressing that peninsula peace serves the common interests of both countries, according to South Korea's Foreign Ministry.

Wang told Cho that China's policy on the peninsula remains unchanged and that Beijing would continue to play a constructive role. A senior ROK Foreign Ministry official said a North Korea-US summit on the sidelines of this week's APEC in Shenzhen 'cannot be ruled out,' but assessed it as unlikely given the variables on both the US and North Korean sides.
