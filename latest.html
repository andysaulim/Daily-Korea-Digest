People's Daily Online / Yonhap / NK News, China-Korea

Lee-Xi summit in Shenzhen clinches FTA phase 2, backs US-North Korea dialogue

Lee Jae-myung and Xi Jinping met for approximately 35 minutes on the APEC sidelines in Shenzhen on November 18, declaring the second phase of Korea-China Free Trade Agreement services and investment negotiations substantially concluded.

Xi called for strengthening macroeconomic policy coordination and building a collaborative ecosystem for emerging technology industries including artificial intelligence. National Security Adviser Wi Sung-lac said Lee asked China to 'play a constructive role on issues concerning the peninsula' and Xi voiced support for US-North Korea dialogue and Seoul's efforts to ease tensions.

NK News / Reuters, DPRK

Rubio says Trump 'serious' about Kim meeting; Kremlin says Putin would join if Kim attends APEC

Marco Rubio, speaking in Manila, said President Trump is 'serious' about meeting Kim Jong Un because 'the only way we're ever going to solve it is if we engage with them,' but explicitly declined to confirm any scheduled meeting.

The Kremlin said Putin would 'of course' meet Kim if the North Korean leader attends the Shenzhen APEC summit. Kim's inspection of the Samjiyon tourism zone near the Chinese border and reports of Chinese social media scrubbing North Korea-related content have fueled analyst speculation about an imminent Kim visit to China.

Yonhap News Agency, US-Korea

ROK-US Combined Forces Command holds senior meeting on AI-driven all-domain operations

ROK Joint Chiefs Chairman Gen. Jin Yong-sung co-led a Combined Forces Command strategy meeting at Camp Humphreys with USFK Commander Gen. Xavier Brunson, focusing on combined joint all-domain operations (CJADO), a concept synchronizing land, air, sea, space, and cyber capabilities into a networked force.

Jin called for AI-powered command-and-control systems to ensure faster decision-making than adversaries through real-time, cross-domain intelligence sharing. The meeting at Camp Humphreys was the latest in a regular series of high-level strategy and coordination sessions between the two allies.

Commentary
NEW BOOK: Rethinking US-Korea Policy for Stability and Coexistence on the Korean Peninsula. Eight contributors from US, South Korean, and regional institutions argue the Korean Peninsula has changed fundamentally since the 2019 collapse of Trump-Kim diplomacy: North Korea has refused denuclearization demands, deepened security ties with Moscow including supporting Russia in Ukraine, and inter-Korean dialogue is at a standstill. The collection calls for new conversations about alternatives, including accepting nuclear-armed coexistence and recalibrating alliance posture, that better reflect the current strategic environment. The book is divided into three sections covering living with a nuclear North Korea, US-ROK alliance recalibration, and regional stabilization. (James Park (ed.), Contributors from Carnegie, Stimson, KINU, KIDA, and universities in Pittsburgh, Nanjing, Seoul, Wellington, Quincy Institute)
To Keep Its Second Place in the Global Battery Race, Seoul Needs More Teammates. South Korea's three major battery firms rank among the global top ten and trail only China in market share, but this position rests on structural vulnerabilities including import reliance for key raw materials and exposure to regulatory and geopolitical shocks. The analysis argues that close government-chaebol coordination and targeted innovation investment drove past success but now entrench risks. Expanding the partnership base to the EU and India would reduce single-partner dependence and diversify supply chain resilience. (Carnegie Endowment for International Peace, Carnegie Endowment for International Peace)
The Model Middle Power. The piece examines how South Korea has built a distinctive foreign policy identity, managing simultaneous alliance depth with Washington and economic ties with Beijing, that offers a replicable template for other middle powers in a fragmenting international order. It situates the Lee government's diplomatic activism, including the China FTA phase 2 and trilateral cooperation, within this framework. The analysis argues Seoul's approach prioritizes outcome-based engagement over ideological alignment. (Foreign Affairs, Foreign Affairs)
