●Top Stories
DPRK

North Korea's missile chief publicly challenges Seoul's hypersonic glide vehicle test as Seoul fires back

DPRK Missile Administration General Director Jang Chang Ha told state media it is 'hard to believe' South Korea's hypersonic glide vehicle underwent actual hypersonic flight, drawing a swift rebuttal from Seoul's defense ministry, which confirmed the HGV achieved hypersonic speeds using domestically developed heat-resistant materials. The MND spokesperson posted that the 'soot-free thermal protection' is itself a design achievement. The exchange follows North Korea's own SLCM test on Oct 15 and represents Pyongyang's first direct public challenge to a specific ROK weapons test outcome.

China-Korea

New Yalu River Bridge opens after 12-year delay, reconnecting North Korea and China by road

North Korea and China opened the New Yalu River Bridge at Sinuiju–Dandong on Oct 15 in a ceremony confirmed by both sides via KCNA and Chinese state media, with trucks crossing in both directions — more than a decade after construction was completed. Chinese social media users hailed the opening as a 'historic moment,' with footage of convoys circulating widely and users expressing hope for resumed tourism. The bridge is the largest land crossing between the two countries since the 1940s and brings the Sinuiju–Dandong crossing to full operational status alongside the recently opened Tumen River road bridge at Tumangang–Khasan.

US-Korea

South Korea and United States hold inaugural 2+2 finance and monetary policymakers meeting in Bangkok

ROK Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-il and US counterparts convened in Bangkok for the first-ever joint 2+2 meeting of finance and monetary authorities from both countries, pledging rapid coordinated response in the event of financial shocks. The format, combining fiscal and central bank officials from both sides, is new to the bilateral relationship. The meeting occurred on the sidelines of the G20, where Lee also called for collective action on global imbalances.

October 16, 2002   During US Assistant Secretary of State James Kelly's visit to Pyongyang (Oct 3-5), North Korean officials allegedly acknowledged operating a clandestine highly enriched uranium program in violation of international agreements — a disclosure that collapsed the Agreed Framework.
Twenty-four years later, North Korea's nuclear program has expanded from a clandestine HEU effort to an IAEA-confirmed enrichment facility at Yongbyon capable of producing material for up to four additional warheads annually, with reactors running continuously per satellite imagery.
