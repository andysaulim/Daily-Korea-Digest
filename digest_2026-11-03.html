Yonhap, DPRK

Kim Yo Jong calls FBI's $6.3B Binance attribution a 'grave political provocation'

Kim Yo Jong, sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, issued a rare personal statement via KCNA flatly rejecting the FBI's finding that Pyongyang was responsible for the theft of more than $6 billion in cryptocurrency from Binance, calling it a 'sinister' attempt to damage North Korea's international image. The FBI linked the theft to the North Korean-affiliated hacking group it calls TraderTraitor.

NK News, DPRK

North Korea tests cruise missiles with cluster dispersal warheads, days after ballistic missile cluster bomb test

North Korea tested strategic cruise missiles fitted with a 'middle-sized dispersal warhead' capable of destroying a target area of 5 to 6 hectares, according to KCNA, with photos showing what appears to be a Hwasal or Pulhwasal series long-range cruise missile releasing dozens of submunitions.

The ROK Joint Chiefs of Staff said separately that South Korea had secured superior counter-cluster munition technology approximately 20 years ago and called the delayed announcement of the October 29 launch a judgment based on operational security.

Yonhap, ROK Politics

Lee and Erdogan agree to expand nuclear plant and defense cooperation at Seoul summit

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung and Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan agreed during a summit in Seoul to deepen cooperation in nuclear power plants, defense manufacturing and infrastructure, with Lee citing 'smooth' progress on Korea Electric Power Corp.'s (KEPCO) potential participation in Turkey's Sinop nuclear power plant project. Erdogan's visit is his first to South Korea since 2018 and follows their November 2025 talks in Ankara. The two countries also elevated the Korea-Turkey Free Trade Agreement Joint Committee to ministerial level, per Korean-language sources.

Hankyoreh, ROK Politics

Passport data of President Lee and 167 delegation members leaked from presidential aircraft manifest

Passport numbers, dates of birth, and other personal details of 168 people, including President Lee Jae-myung, First Lady Kim Hea-kyung, Foreign Minister Cho Hyun, National Security Office Director Wi Sung-lac, and classified bodyguards, were posted online in an unencrypted file titled 'Passenger List for Code One,' uploaded to a foreign malware-scanning platform in June ahead of Lee's European tour.

The presidential office said it was already aware and had taken measures; the Foreign Ministry said it viewed the case as a leak rather than a hack and is working with police to determine the source. Lee subsequently called for creation of a centralized government-wide cybersecurity body.
