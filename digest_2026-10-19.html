DPRK

Kim Yo Jong threatens loudspeaker restart, calls southern border 'very unstable'

Kim Yo Jong issued a statement through KCNA Monday accusing ROK forces of firing at DPRK troops and transmitting warnings via loudspeakers along the border, calling the situation 'very unstable' and saying Pyongyang is 'considering whether to resume' loudspeaker broadcasts toward South Korea. The statement coincides with the start of South Korea's Hoguk exercise, which this year incorporates US joint drills that had been cut from summer training. A fourth North Korean land mine washed ashore on Boreum Island, Ganghwa County the same day, the fourth such incident this month.

Lee demands early OPCON return, calls it 'repatriation' not 'transfer'

President Lee Jae-myung, speaking at a press luncheon marking his 500th day in office, said wartime operational control (OPCON) must be 'returned', using the term 환수 (repatriation) rather than 전환 (transfer), and that Seoul cannot 'continue to leave in the hands of another country' the most core component of national sovereignty. Lee said US-Korea consultations are 'basically going in the direction of getting it back,' while acknowledging 'the process will not be easy.' The statement follows Gen. Xavier Brunson's disputed remarks on the extent of South Korean territory, which the US has since expressed 'understanding' of Seoul's concerns about.

NK-Russia-China

North Korea-China trade hits $299M in September, second-highest this year

North Korea's trade with China reached $299 million in September, up from $238 million in August, according to China's General Administration of Customs, the second-highest monthly total of 2026, coming as both sides prepared to open their largest cross-border bridge. Cumulative January-September trade stood at approximately $2.27 billion, up 18.6 percent year-on-year per SBS reporting. The surge tracks with the opening of the new Yalu River bridge between Sinuiju and Dandong, which China's state media framed as the biggest gateway to the Korean Peninsula in 80 years.
