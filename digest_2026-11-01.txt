This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Sunday, November 1.

Three things today: North Korea tests a cluster warhead, the FBI pins a six-point-three-billion-dollar crypto heist on Pyongyang, and South Korea's October exports hit a record high.

North Korea's new warhead is designed to hit exactly the kinds of targets that matter most to South Korean military planners, and Pyongyang says four types will be in the field within a year. Tomorrow, President Lee Jae-myung delivers his budget address to the National Assembly.

First, North Korea's Missile Administration conducted a live-fire test on October 31 of what it calls the Standard Type 2 cluster-bomb warhead, built for tactical ballistic missiles.

KCNA published imagery showing the warhead dispersing submunitions over mock-ups of South Korean military facilities, including arched-roof structures that resemble aircraft hangars and ammunition depots. Pyongyang claims a single detonation can devastate 12 to 13 hectares, roughly the area of 18 soccer fields.

The Missile Administration's director stated that all four categories of cluster warheads will be fielded by military units before the end of 2026. South Korea's Joint Chiefs confirmed a ballistic missile launch from North Pyongan Province toward the Yellow Sea on October 31. They identified the likely projectile as the KN-24.

For Korea policy watchers, the combination of imagery targeting South Korean military infrastructure and a firm deployment timeline is the detail to track. This is not just a test announcement. It is a public commitment to a specific in-service date.

General Kim Jeong-sik, a Korean Workers' Party Central Military Commission member, supervised the test on behalf of the Missile Administration, according to KCNA.

That cluster warhead test is the headline from Pyongyang on October 31.

Second, the FBI has formally attributed the theft of approximately 6.3 billion dollars from Binance to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-linked hacking unit.

Blockchain intelligence firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis made the link first, and the FBI has now formally concurred, according to Reuters.

The figure makes this the largest cryptocurrency theft on record. Speaking to reporters on October 31, President Donald Trump said, quote: "We don't know if North Korea is responsible. If it is confirmed to be North Korea, we will talk to them about it." That is the first time Trump has publicly connected a North Korean cyber operation to a potential diplomatic opening in his second term.

Separately, the British government warned that North Korean information technology workers posing as legitimate employees have targeted thousands of British banks and companies.

For anyone watching the diplomatic track, Trump's comment is the signal. A cyber-attribution has now become, at least rhetorically, a potential channel to Pyongyang.

The six-point-three-billion-dollar Binance hack, now formally pinned on North Korea's TraderTraitor unit.

Third, South Korea's October exports reached approximately 115.3 billion dollars, nearly doubling year-on-year, according to Korea Customs Service data reported by Reuters, Bloomberg, and the Wall Street Journal.

That is the highest monthly export figure on record. The surge was led by semiconductors, with the Wall Street Journal reporting that chip-led exports are accelerating as global investment in artificial intelligence infrastructure drives demand for high-bandwidth memory and advanced chips.

With two months left in the year, the gap to the annual one-trillion-dollar export target has narrowed to approximately 70 billion dollars.

The record export figure lands the same week that South Korea's economy grew faster than expected in the third quarter, according to the Wall Street Journal. That faster growth is adding pressure to a debate inside the Bank of Korea, where arguments for a November rate hike have been gaining ground.

South Korea's monthly exports: 115.3 billion dollars, the highest on record.

Now to Pyongyang.

State media's lead today was the Missile Administration's confirmation of the October 31 cluster warhead test, which the leads have covered. KCNA also carried a Foreign Ministry statement asserting that international sanctions have not worked to deny Pyongyang's status as a nuclear weapons state, and that the attribution matters going into any prospective U.S. diplomatic approach.

Kim Jong Un has not made a public appearance in the past two days. His most recent quoted statement came on October 30, at a drone exhibition. KCNA reported him saying, quote: "The aim to seize the absolute superiority in the drone technology is an indispensable option in the light of the ever-changing patterns of modern warfare. Strength precisely means peace and peace is guaranteed only by strength."

The bottom line from state media: the cluster warhead test is front and center, the nuclear-weapons-state position is being restated, and North Korean trucks remain absent from the New Yalu River Bridge two weeks after its October 15 opening.

The rest of the news, quickly.

Foreign Minister Cho Hyun attended the G20 foreign ministers' meeting in Atlanta on October 30 and 31, chaired by U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio. Cho briefed counterparts on Seoul's position on the September 21 DMZ mine blast and called deepening North Korea-Russia military cooperation a serious threat to regional security. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband, Indian External Affairs Minister Subrahmanyam Jaishankar, and Mexican Foreign Minister Roberto Velasco. On the sidelines, the foreign ministers of South Korea, the U.S., and Japan held a trilateral meeting and reaffirmed the goal of North Korea's denuclearization.

Cho also met Jaishankar separately to review implementation of agreements from President Lee's April state visit to India, the first South Korean presidential visit to India in eight years.

On the alliance, National Assembly Defense Committee chair Jin Seong-jun departed for the United States on November 1 after the U.S. government issued his official visa only one day before departure, following an earlier refusal that he described as inconsistent with alliance norms. People Power Party figure Oh Se-hoon, the Seoul mayor, said separately that the U.S.-Korea alliance is dangerously strained and blamed the current government.

On North Korea's weapons programs, 38 North published analysis on October 30 documenting a public display of a vessel North Korea has designated a Tactical Nuclear Attack submarine, alongside its latest intercontinental ballistic missile. The submarine-launched ballistic missiles shown are previously seen systems presented as new.

A U.N. rights official said North Korea is more isolated than at any point in its history, even as factories, smartphones, and new laws coexist with what the official called the deepest external cutoff on record.

The Washington Post published analysis finding no verifiable denuclearization progress since Trump's second term began, and concluding that North Korea's nuclear arsenal has continued to grow.

Deputy Prime Minister Lee Hyeong-il chaired a Sunday economic consultative meeting covering housing supply expansion and the national jobs project. The Democratic Party held a closed-door leadership session to discuss the 2027 budget framework ahead of President Lee's budget address to the National Assembly on November 2.

Samsung Electronics chairman Lee Jae-yong visited the company's advanced semiconductor packaging research facility in Yokohama, Japan, on November 1 to review packaging technology and explore materials cooperation with Japanese partners.

In Gallup Korea polling taken October 13 to 15, presidential approval stood at 39 percent, up from prior polling. The Democratic Party's support was at 36 percent and the People Power Party's at 27 percent, both trending up. A separate Gallup poll reported October 30 found 34 percent of respondents said the acquittal verdict for Oh Se-hoon was the right decision, while 37 percent said it was wrong.

On Capitol Hill.

The Binance attribution featured in statements from blockchain intelligence firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis, with the FBI formally concurring. President Trump told reporters on October 31 he would seek talks with Pyongyang if North Korea's involvement is confirmed. The story is drawing attention on the Hill given the scale of the theft and the diplomatic signal Trump has now attached to it.

Beyond the region.

President Lee Jae-myung will host Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan on November 4 and East Timorese President Jose Ramos-Horta on November 7. The Turkey summit is Erdogan's first Seoul visit since 2018 and comes ahead of the bilateral 70th diplomatic anniversary in 2027. South Korea will also rebid the construction contract for the Czech Dukovany nuclear power plant after talks collapsed, with the Trade and Industry minister saying the government's commitment to completing the project is unchanged. The New York Times reports Korean language study has picked up sharply in Japan, with enrollment rising across schools and private institutions.

Finally, the markets.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed at about 5,731 as of October 30, down about 3.5 percent. The won stood at about 1,335 to the dollar as of October 31. Brent crude was at about 113 dollars as of October 30, down about 1.3 percent. The Bank of Korea's policy rate stands at 3.00 percent as of October 22.
