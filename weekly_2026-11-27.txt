This is the Korea Week in Review from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It covers November 21 to 27, 2026.

The week in brief.

The defining development of the week was North Korea's decision to reject Trump's summit overture at APEC and then, within days, publicly inspect nuclear warhead facilities and order expanded tactical nuclear deployment, closing off near-term diplomatic space. The expulsion of Switzerland's Pyongyang mission adds a new coercive diplomatic instrument to Pyongyang's toolkit. Next week, the Democratic Party's December 3 impeachment motion against Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae and the Supreme Court verdict in the Kim Keon Hee bribery case will dominate the domestic political calendar.

The top 10 stories of the week.

First: Kim Jong Un rejects Trump summit offer, then inspects nuclear warheads and orders tactical nuke expansion. Kim Jong Un declined to meet President Trump during Trump's Asia trip, with the Trump administration having actively pursued a summit and explored various inducements including a Korean War end declaration; North Korea set conditions the Trump team found unrealistically excessive. On November 25, Kim inspected three nuclear sites, a weapons-grade nuclear materials production base, a nuclear-powered equipment production base, and the Nuclear Weapons Institute, and ordered wider tactical nuclear deployment, asserting North Korea's nuclear status 'will never change.' N.K. Pro satellite imagery also indicated a North Korean intercontinental ballistic missile engine test occurred the same week Trump sought the Kim meeting at APEC.

Second: Yongbyon uranium-enrichment plant expansion confirmed by satellite imagery. New commercial satellite photographs published by the Wall Street Journal and analyzed by Jeffrey Lewis at ArmsControlWonk showed a concrete addition to North Korea's original uranium enrichment plant at Yongbyon. The International Atomic Energy Agency separately confirmed North Korea had expanded the facility. The expansion was described as small but concrete, with the broader Yongbyon complex already assessed as having a new centrifuge hall operational.

Third: North Korea expels Swiss diplomat, closes Pyongyang mission within 48 hours. Kim Yo Jong issued a KCNA statement threatening to force closure of Switzerland's newly opened Pyongyang mission after Bern sanctioned the Songdowon youth camp for its role in Ukraine child transfers. North Korean Foreign Ministry European Department director general Pak Hyon Su then declared Swiss mission head Sylvain Astier persona non grata and ordered him to leave within 48 hours. Switzerland said it would maintain the sanctions despite the expulsion, and analysis noted North Korea selectively targeted Switzerland and the UK while sparing EU member states.

Fourth: KOSPI suffers extreme volatility: drops 5.2 percent, rebounds 6.32 percent, then falls 6.16 percent in three sessions. The KOSPI closed November 20 down 5.21 percent at 4,869.96, breaching the 5,000 level after a global AI-sector selloff triggered by an Nvidia stock rout, with circuit breakers activated and the government convening an emergency 'F4' market review. The index surged 6.32 percent to 5,006.03 on November 24 as Jensen Huang returned to Nvidia, before falling again 6.16 percent to 4,697.51 on November 25 as the Wall Street tech selloff resumed. Deputy Prime Minister Lee Hyoung Il announced phased market stabilization measures, and the government prepared a market stabilization fund.

Fifth: Russian warplanes, including Tu-160 strategic bombers, repeatedly enter Korean Air Defense Identification Zone. About five Russian warplanes entered the KADIZ over the East Sea early on November 24 and approached Ulleung Island, prompting the Joint Chiefs of Staff to issue a pre-emptive air raid alert and scramble fighters. A Russian Tu-160 strategic bomber entered the KADIZ near Ulleungdo again, with the South Korean Defense Ministry summoning Russia's defense attaché and financial news reporting a Su-57 presence was suspected. Russia's FSB and North Korea's State Intelligence Bureau were also reported to have signed a classified information pact in March.

Sixth: Bank of Korea raises benchmark rate to 3.25 percent, third hike of 2026. The Bank of Korea raised its benchmark interest rate by 25 basis points to 3.25 percent on November 26, the third such increase in 2026. The Bank of Korea governor cited AI-sector expansion and inflationary pressures as grounds for the decision.

Seventh: Lee reshuffles security team, nominates first female National Intelligence Service director in South Korea's history. President Lee Jae-myung nominated former Justice Minister Kang Kum-sil as National Intelligence Service director, which would make her the first woman to head South Korea's spy agency. The People Power Party demanded Lee withdraw the nomination, citing Kang's alleged China advisory roles. Lee also approved confirmation hearing requests for four ministers and the National Intelligence Service director nominee.

Eighth: Democratic Party targets December 3 for Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae impeachment motion. The Democratic Party formally adopted the impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae as party policy on November 20, naming Representative Yang Bu-nam to lead a task force and setting December 3, the second anniversary of the martial law declaration, as the target date to introduce the motion in the National Assembly. The Democratic Party formally proposed a five-party coalition to carry the impeachment. A national judges' representative conference and a national court presidents' conference were both scheduled for the following week in connection with the push.

Ninth: Trump's new Section 301 tariff proposal targets 14 economies, preserves South Korea's 15 percent ceiling. The Trump administration proposed new tariffs under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 against 14 trading partners over industrial overcapacity, including South Korea, while a senior U.S. official confirmed South Korea's tariff ceiling would be preserved at 15 percent. Separately, 22 House Republicans wrote to the U.S. Trade Representative urging scrutiny of South Korea's platform regulation legislation.

Tenth: U.S. The Justice Department seeks civil forfeiture of 1.17 billion dollars in cryptocurrency stolen by North Korean hackers from Binance. The U.S. Department of Justice filed a civil forfeiture complaint in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia seeking to recover 1.17 billion dollars in USD Coin frozen after North Korean hackers stole it from Binance. The filing was reported on November 24.

Now to Pyongyang.

Kim Jong Un made a public appearance on 4 of 6 days, most recently on Thursday November 26.

North Korea's official posture this week moved from diplomatic snub to active nuclear demonstration: Kim rejected Trump's summit offer at APEC, held an unprecedented direct ambassador credentials ceremony citing East Asia security threats, and then publicly inspected nuclear warhead and enrichment facilities while ordering expanded tactical nuclear deployment. KCNA maintained complete silence on the failed Trump outreach and the N.K. Pro-reported intercontinental ballistic missile engine test throughout. The expulsion of Switzerland's Pyongyang mission head and the threat to Kim Yo Jong represent the most coercive diplomatic move Pyongyang has made against a European neutral in recent memory.

Kim Jong Un, in their words: The security situation in North Korea and the political and military environment in East Asia urgently demand that we provide for ourselves the power and capability.

Kim Jong Un, in their words: The nuclear forces of North Korea have been built up as a powerful treasured sword to protect the sovereignty and dignity of the state and people, and their status will never change.
