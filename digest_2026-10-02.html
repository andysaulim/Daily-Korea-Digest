●Top Stories
US-Korea

Seoul-Kyiv POW row escalates: Lee warns Ukraine of 'measures,' demands apology over undisclosed transfer

Lee Jae-myung warned Friday that South Korea will pursue 'additional measures' if Ukraine refuses to apologize for disclosing — without prior Seoul coordination — the transfer of two North Korean POWs to South Korea. ROK FM Cho Hyun and his Ukrainian counterpart separately discussed sending a 'proper message,' while a Ukrainian minister told media he hoped to heal the 'misunderstanding.' The dispute raises broader questions about the legal and diplomatic status of the two North Koreans now in Seoul, whose transfer has thrust them into the international spotlight.

CNBC / Reuters / NK News, Oct 2
US-Korea

Seoul contests White House's $50 billion Alaska investment claim, says Trump 'jumped the gun'

South Korea formally disputed the White House announcement that Seoul had committed to a new $50 billion investment in Alaska energy infrastructure, with CNN reporting Seoul said Trump made the announcement prematurely. The WSJ and NYT both confirmed the discrepancy, with the NYT framing it as Trump 'dangling' the Alaska gas project as part of a broader Korean investment package. Seoul had separately pushed back on the Alaska LNG project even before the announcement, per earlier CNBC reporting.

WSJ / CNN / NYT, Oct 2
US-Korea

South Korea abolishes 78-year prosecution service, launches new criminal justice agencies

South Korea launched a scaled-down Public Prosecution Office (공소청) and a new Serious Crime Investigation Agency (중대범죄수사청) on Friday, formally abolishing the prosecution service that had existed since 1948. The FT reported the reform as President Lee 'bringing powerful prosecution to heel' — the same service that indicted him before he won the presidency. The PPP warned that with the new Serious Crime Investigation Agency still lacking a director and full staffing, victim protection gaps could emerge immediately.

Yonhap EN / FT, Oct 2
DPRK

Kim Yo Jong doubles down on DMZ mine denial, says DPRK goal is severing all ties with Seoul

Kim Yo Jong issued a second consecutive statement Friday accusing South Korea of 'fanning tensions' by attributing the Sep 21 DMZ blast to North Korean mines, and stating Pyongyang's goal is to close its border so tightly that 'even wild animals cannot cross.' She mocked Lee Jae-myung's call to ease military tensions. Unification Minister Chung Dong-young simultaneously proposed formal border-line talks, creating a direct public contradiction between Seoul's offer of dialogue and Pyongyang's explicit rejection.
