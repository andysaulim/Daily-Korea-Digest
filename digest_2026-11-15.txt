This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Sunday, November 15.

Three things today: Kim Jong Un's closest aide arrives in Moscow days before a landmark U.S.-China-Russia summit; an AI exam cheating scandal triggers an emergency response from Seoul's presidential office; and South Korea wraps up a senior officials' meeting at APEC in Shenzhen. That first story carries real weight because the visit puts a senior North Korean figure in Moscow just as Washington, Beijing, and Moscow prepare to sit down together for the first time. And mark your calendars: that trilateral summit, along with South Korea's nationwide college entrance exam, is scheduled for November 19.

First, the Moscow visit.

Jo Yong-won, secretary of the Central Committee of the ruling Workers' Party and Kim Jong Un's closest aide, arrived in Moscow by private plane on Saturday. He's leading a party and government delegation, and the invitation came from Dmitry Medvedev, chairman of Russia's United Russia party.

The timing is what makes this significant. The first-ever U.S.-China-Russia trilateral summit is scheduled for November 19, on the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen. Trump, Xi, and Putin are expected to meet, with nuclear arms reduction and Iran policy reported as agenda items.

Pyongyang is sending its most senior diplomat to the Russian capital in the days just before that meeting. That's a signal worth watching closely.

There's more context from the overnight wires. China's Foreign Minister Wang Yi briefed South Korea's Foreign Minister Cho Hyun on Kim Jong Un's visit to Dandong and on the prospects for a Kim-Trump meeting. The Chinese side told Seoul that a Kim-Trump meeting was unlikely.

So the picture taking shape is this: North Korea is deepening its alignment with Moscow at the precise moment Russia is engaging in high-stakes diplomacy with Washington and Beijing, and Pyongyang appears to be positioning itself to influence, or at least observe, whatever emerges from that room.

Jo Yong-won is now in Moscow. Watch what comes out of November 19.

Second, the AI cheating scandal.

South Korea's presidential policy chief of staff, Ha Joon-kyung, convened an emergency meeting and ordered a full review of all suspected AI-cheating cases across every national certification exam. The urgency is clear: the nationwide college entrance exam, the Suneung, is four days away, on November 19.

The government's response is substantial. Thermal-sensing cameras will be deployed at every test site. Dedicated personnel will screen for AI devices. And a police special team will target organized cheating operations.

This isn't just an education story. Presidential policy staff convening emergency sessions over an exam integrity issue reflects how seriously Seoul is treating the threat that AI tools pose to high-stakes national systems. The Suneung is one of the most consequential single days in a Korean student's life, and the government is treating a potential breach of that process as a matter requiring a government-wide response.

Prime Minister Han Seong-sook's office separately announced a cross-ministry vice-ministerial meeting for November 16 to coordinate AI-cheating countermeasures further.

The emergency measures are in place. The exam is November 19.

Third, APEC in Shenzhen.

South Korea's senior official Park Jong-han wrapped up the APEC Concluding Senior Officials' Meeting in Shenzhen. Park pushed for AI cooperation and demographic change response to become standing items on APEC's agenda, and stressed energy supply chain resilience for the region.

The meeting also reviewed progress on the Internet and Digital Economy Roadmap and produced final assessments from the Committee on Trade and Investment and from the Economic Committee.

The diplomatic backdrop matters here. China and South Korea are in the middle of a bilateral reset, framed around economic cooperation, with Presidents Xi Jinping and Lee Jae-myung expected to interact on the margins of the APEC and ASEAN summits.

President Lee posted on X on Saturday, saying, in his words, "Public officials who have been delegated power by the people must use that power not to gain popularity but to do what is necessary for the state and the people." His office described it as a statement on reform priorities, days before he represents South Korea at APEC.

South Korea's senior officials have concluded their preparatory work. The leaders' meeting is next.

Now to Pyongyang.

State media had no articles in today's update, but the KCNA picture is not quiet. The senior official activity logged today is the Jo Yong-won delegation's departure for Moscow, which state media framed as an expression of deepening North Korea-Russia alliance relations. Domestic content covered welfare and technology topics. Notably, all recent weapons tests remain absent from internal KCNA output. Kim Jong Un has not appeared publicly today; his last appearance was one day ago. The bottom line from KCNA: Pyongyang is amplifying the Russia relationship while keeping its weapons program off the domestic record.

The rest of the news, quickly.

On the economy, Deputy Prime Minister Lee Hyong-il chaired a meeting on inflation management, with processed food and dining costs showing expanding price increases. Energy utility fee pressure is also building, tied to elevated global oil prices.

The Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy extended the petroleum price cap for another four weeks, the ninth consecutive month it has been in place. Minister Kim Jeong-gwan cited continued Hormuz Strait tensions and high international oil prices. South Korea also expanded its naval unit's operations toward the Hormuz Strait, a move the Korea JoongAng Daily reports places Seoul between its commitment to freedom of navigation and the risk of provoking Iran, which has previously warned South Korea over its naval presence there.

On the Czech nuclear deal, Minister Kim held a late-night video call with Czech industry minister Havlicek. Both sides agreed to hold monthly ministerial meetings in addition to existing weekly director-general talks. The minister said the decision to suspend construction-contract negotiations was, in his words, "an unavoidable judgment to complete the project successfully," and reaffirmed that construction will be completed on the agreed schedule. South Korea's National Assembly is separately weighing a hearing on the Dukovany project.

Korea Aerospace Industries is expanding its KF-21 fighter production cooperation with Indonesia to include parts, simulators, and civil aircraft. And Malaysia's defense minister confirmed that all 18 FA-50M light combat aircraft ordered from Korea Aerospace Industries will be delivered by March 2028.

HD Hyundai launched a 600-ton autonomous surface vessel co-developed with Anduril Industries. Sea trials are underway off Ulsan, with U.S. testing planned early next year.

In the National Assembly, People Power Party lawmaker Park Chung-kwon filed a bill to restrict contact with North Korean residents, citing the need to block North Korean influence operations. Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok referred senior committee member Choi Min-hee to the party's central ethics tribunal over remarks at a closed-door meeting.

A Gallup Korea poll, taken November 3 through 5, put presidential approval at 41 percent, up from prior readings. The Democratic Party stood at 36 percent support, also up. The People Power Party was at 28 percent, down. Independents also at 28 percent, stable.

CSIS analysis finds that North Korea's TraderTraitor hacking group's theft from the Binance exchange was preventable. The theft, carried out on October 29, was preceded by a month of social engineering via LinkedIn. The amount taken: 6 billion dollars.

Three North Korean information technology workers were recalled from China in late October, after less than a year abroad, on suspicion of plotting to defect. The Ministry of State Security ordered them home.

South Korea's October rent inflation rose 1.4 percent year-on-year, the fastest pace in nearly four years. Ulsan posted the sharpest regional gain.

The Bank of Korea's final rate-setting meeting of the year is approaching, and bond markets are watching. The current rate stands at 3.00 percent, set in October, with some analysts anticipating a possible hike given continued inflation pressure.

Beyond the region.

Malaysia confirmed it will receive all 18 FA-50M light combat aircraft from Korea Aerospace Industries by March 2028. That's the full order, complete. Separately, the Czech nuclear project is drawing scrutiny on two fronts: South Korea's National Assembly is weighing a hearing on the Dukovany contract, while the Ministry of Trade has assured Prague that timelines will be met.

Finally, the markets.

Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed at about 5,291 on November 13, down about 1 percent. The dollar bought about 1,336 won as of today, up slightly. Brent crude stood at about 96 dollars a barrel on November 13.
