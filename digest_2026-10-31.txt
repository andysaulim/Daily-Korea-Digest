This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair, read by an AI voice. It's Saturday, October 31.

Three things today: the U.S., South Korea, and Japan reaffirm denuclearization at the G20 in Atlanta; North Korea puts drones on display that target mock South Korean and American weapons; and President Trump says the U.S. is investigating whether Pyongyang stole 6.3 billion dollars from the crypto exchange Binance. The drone exhibition is the sharpest signal yet of how Pyongyang wants you to picture its military reach. Tomorrow, the APEC summit opens, and there is speculation about a possible Trump-Kim encounter on the sidelines.

First, the foreign ministers of the United States, South Korea, and Japan met on October 30 on the sidelines of the G20 in Atlanta. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, South Korean Foreign Minister Cho Hyun, and Japanese Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi signed a Memorandum of Cooperation for a Trilateral Arctic Pacific Partnership, covering maritime cooperation and economic partnership in the Pacific Arctic. The three also reaffirmed what they called their resolute commitment to the complete denuclearization of North Korea and called for full implementation of U.N. Security Council resolutions.

South Korea's Foreign Ministry noted this was the fourth such trilateral in four months, coming roughly one month after a similar meeting on the sidelines of the U.N. General Assembly. Foreign Minister Cho described Seoul's role as pacemaker for Korean Peninsula peace.

One notable wrinkle: according to South Korean broadcaster SBS, the U.S. side raised China's coercive behavior in the Yellow Sea during the meeting, but neither the South Korean nor the Japanese readouts included that item.

Also on the margins of the G20, Cho met with Indonesia's newly appointed Foreign Minister, Arrmanatha Nasir. They agreed to expand cooperation across diplomacy, defense, trade, energy, and advanced technology, building on the Special Comprehensive Strategic Partnership the two countries elevated in April.

The bottom line: four trilaterals in four months is a sustained pace. The denuclearization language is unchanged, even as a new Stimson Center report argues that the conventional framework requiring complete denuclearization before any engagement has failed and that Washington and its allies need new approaches.

Second, North Korea put its drone arsenal on public display, and the targets it chose were specific. Kim Jong Un attended the October 29 exhibition called Unmanned Aviation Technology Development-2026. North Korea showed at least 15 drone types across strategic, operational, and tactical categories and ran live demonstrations.

One loitering munition resembling the Harop destroyed a mock South Korean border guard post on an island. An X-shaped drone struck mock-ups of a South Korean K9 howitzer and a U.S. M1 Abrams tank. A Bulsae-4 air-to-ground missile, fired from a Satbyeol-9 unmanned attack aircraft, struck a mock counter-battery radar.

The exhibition came the day after Pyongyang rejected Seoul's proposal for military talks, and on the same day North Korea launched approximately 10 short-range ballistic missiles toward the sea.

At the exhibition, Kim said: "The aim to seize the absolute superiority in the drone technology is an indispensable option in the light of the ever-changing patterns of modern warfare. Strength precisely means peace and peace is guaranteed only by strength."

Separately, an analysis by Vann Van Diepen published by 38 North documents state media coverage of a North Korean defense event earlier this month, noting that displayed submarine-launched ballistic missiles are not new systems despite how state media framed them.

The key fact to carry: at least 15 drone types, demonstrated against mock South Korean and American targets, on the same day as a ballistic missile salvo.

Third, President Donald Trump said from the Oval Office that the United States is investigating whether North Korea is responsible for the largest cryptocurrency theft on record. The target was Binance, and the amount stolen was 6.3 billion dollars. Two blockchain intelligence firms, TRM Labs and Chainalysis, both attributed the theft to a North Korean hacking unit called TraderTraitor, which has a history of targeting digital asset platforms. The Wall Street Journal also reported that clues point to North Korea, noting the hack was executed in minutes. A Financial Times analysis argues that sanctions-driven investment in cyber warfare now funds significant North Korean state revenues.

Trump's public confirmation that the U.S. is investigating this marks a significant step. The TraderTraitor attribution is coming from private intelligence firms, not yet the U.S. government. Watch for whether a formal government attribution follows.

Now to Pyongyang.

State media led today with the Swiss foreign minister's visit to Pyongyang. North Korea's state news agency confirmed that Swiss Foreign Minister Ignazio Cassis met Foreign Minister Choe Son Hui and Workers' Party Secretary Cho Yong Won, the first Western ministerial engagement of this kind in years, following the reopening of Switzerland's diplomatic office in North Korea. That visit also drew attention in Seoul: South Korea's Foreign Ministry official Jeong Yeon-du held a dinner consultation with Cassis on October 30 and said Seoul hopes the Swiss diplomatic office reopening will positively contribute to expanding communication and engagement with North Korea.

State media's second major item was a Foreign Ministry press statement rejecting sanctions and refusing the designation of North Korea as a non-nuclear-weapons state. That statement was timed to coincide with the Atlanta trilateral. Notably, state media reported on the October 29 drone exhibition and a 600-millimeter Sniper rocket live-fire demonstration without acknowledging the simultaneous ballistic missile salvo.

The rest of the news, quickly.

On wartime operational control: Wi Sung-lac, director of national security at South Korea's Presidential Office, said the allies completed a Full Operational Capability assessment during this year's Ulchi Freedom Shield exercise. Seoul hopes to set a target year for the transfer, aiming to finalize a timeline at a defense ministers' meeting in December, followed by a Security Consultative Meeting early next year. Wi also said Seoul has demanded an apology and concrete accountability from North Korea over the September DMZ land mine explosion.

On a defense contract: Hanwha Defense USA has won a 269 million dollar U.S. Navy contract to build a medium landing ship at Philly Shipyard. South Korea's Yonhap News Agency called it the first U.S. Navy shipbuilding contract for a Korean-owned yard.

On Capitol Hill, U.S. Congress is advancing a munitions transfer bill that would ease the transfer of American-purchased arms to Ukraine through South Korea and allied countries. The measure is designed to address Ukraine's ammunition shortfall while preserving allied stockpile management.

On Seoul and Kyiv: Ukraine's Foreign Ministry said it is working to de-escalate tensions with South Korea over Seoul's refusal to supply arms. South Korea's Foreign Ministry confirmed it has no plans to sever diplomatic relations with Ukraine and said it is seeking a diplomatic resolution.

South Korea's intelligence community, according to UPI reporting, assesses that Kim Jong Un conveyed Pyongyang's position on potential Trump talks during a recent visit to Dandong in China. That is driving Seoul to prepare for a facilitation role ahead of any U.S.-North Korea engagement.

On the economy: Goldman Sachs strategist Timothy Moe told CNBC that South Korea is delivering 350 percent earnings growth this year, driven by the semiconductor cycle. The Bank of Korea rate-hike speculation is growing after a third-quarter GDP beat and persistent inflation data, according to analysts cited by Newsis.

A Gallup Korea poll taken October 13 to 15 put presidential approval at 39 percent, up. The Democratic Party stood at 36 percent, also up. The People Power Party was at 27 percent, up. Independents fell to 29 percent.

The U.N. Command's General Xavier Brunson attended a burial ceremony in Busan on October 31 for Serge Archambeau, a French Korean War veteran whose final wish was to rest beside his comrades in Korea.

On Capitol Hill.

U.S. Congress is advancing a bill that would facilitate the transfer of American-purchased munitions to Ukraine through South Korea and allied countries. The measure is designed to address Ukraine's ammunition shortfall. No hearing is listed for today.

Beyond the region.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, visiting Seoul on October 29, said North Korea's weapons cooperation with Russia threatens freedom across Europe. Merz and President Lee Jae-myung announced an enhanced strategic partnership covering technology, defense, and supply chain cooperation. Ukraine said it is working to ease tensions with South Korea over the arms supply dispute. Seoul denied any plan to sever diplomatic ties.

Finally, the markets.

The dollar was buying about 1,335 won on October 31, down slightly. Seoul's main stock index, the KOSPI, closed at about 5,731 on October 30, down about 3 and a half percent. Brent crude was up about 2 and a half percent to about 118 dollars a barrel. The Bank of Korea's policy rate stands at 3 percent.
