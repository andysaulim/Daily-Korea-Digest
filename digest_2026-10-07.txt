This is the Korea Daily Brief from the CSIS Korea Chair. It's Wednesday, October 7.

In today's brief: North Korea warns Seoul over DMZ demining; Kim letter to Putin; Nuri-5 launches 15 satellites; and South Korea 747 billion dollar energy transition.

Our top story: North Korea condemned Seoul's final investigation into the Sept. 21 DMZ mine blast that injured three South Korean soldiers and warned its forces would respond to any South Korean demining crossing into North Korean territory. The North Korean statement came via KCNA as the South Korean Defense Ministry simultaneously announced plans to seek a formal armistice violation citation through UN Command. South Korean Prime Minister Han Seong-sook separately said Seoul must keep demanding a North Korean apology, while Unification Minister Chung Dong-young described the situation as underscoring the need for 'fundamental peace.'

Also making news: South Korea's homegrown Nuri rocket lifted off from Naro Space Center on Oct. 7 and successfully deployed 15 satellites into orbit at 570 km, including five constellation satellites released sequentially — the country's first microsatellite constellation. President Lee called the achievement proof of South Korea's expanded launch capacity and urged further advances in satellite technology. Private sector participation in the mission was highlighted as a milestone toward a 'New Space' civilian-led era.

And finally: Kim Jong Un sent a letter to Vladimir Putin vowing to deepen the 'strategic' partnership and calling Russia's war in Ukraine 'sacred,' per KCNA via Reuters and the Kyiv Post. Simultaneously, North Korea issued a statement criticizing U.S. support for Taiwan and pledging backing for Beijing's 'One China' position, framing the U.S. as responsible for regional instability. The dual-track diplomatic messaging — to Moscow on its war and to Beijing on Taiwan — arrived ahead of the Workers' Party founding day on Oct. 10.

Here's what else happened overnight. South Korea unveils $747 billion energy transition plan through 2035. 1,000 trillion won package targets carbon neutrality and green industrial upgrade.

NK Pro satellite imagery: North Korea upgraded Sangum-ri coastal missile site before Kim-attended IRBM launches. New launch support infrastructure built at Anbyon County beach ahead of Oct 3 to 5 drills.

Ukraine says South Korean exports eased Russia's drone-strike fuel crisis; Seoul pushes back. Blue House calls Kyiv's framing a 'distortion' of South Korea sanctions compliance.

Seoul appeals court acquits former President Yoon in 'free opinion polls' political funds case. Appellate ruling overturns lower court conviction; Yoon's separate insurrection trial continues.

AI-linked cyberattacks sweep Japan and South Korea businesses; banks, churches, firms hit. Multiple IPs used to obscure origin; South Korea megachurches and financial firms among targets.

U.S. Brig. Gen. Robert Brown assumes command of 2nd Infantry Division in South Korea.

Turning to Pyongyang. Kim Jong Un's letter to Putin calling Russia's Ukraine war 'sacred,' combined with North Korea's coordinated Taiwan/Russia diplomatic statements and continued DMZ escalation threats, signals maximum rhetorical alignment with Moscow and Beijing in the three days before the Workers' Party founding anniversary (Oct 10) — the highest-risk provocation window this quarter. Kim Jong Un was last seen in public 2 days ago.

On U.S.–Korea trade. Steel and aluminium remain at 50% and autos at 15%, both under Section 232. The Section 122 surcharge expired July 24, leaving no baseline reciprocal tariff. Section 301 is now the primary prospective legal vehicle for any new measures against South Korea. Selected so far under the 350 billion dollar investment pledge: Encinal gas-fired power plant, Texas, 22.3 billion dollars.

In business news. Major global investment banks upgraded South Korea's growth forecasts, citing robust chip export performance. The revisions reflect accelerating AI-driven demand for memory semiconductors produced by Samsung and SK Hynix.

LG Electronics estimated its third-quarter operating profit rose 13.5 percent year-on-year, driven by home appliance segment performance. The result comes despite a broader KOSPI decline tied to rising global bond yields.

Excess funds held by South Korean businesses reached an all-time high in Q2 2026, fueled by semiconductor export revenues. The accumulation raises questions about reinvestment pace domestically versus overseas capital deployment.

Around the region. North Korean and Taliban military officials participated in discussions at Russian war games, per NK News reporting from KCNA. The three-way military contact — North Korea, Taliban, Russia — at a Russian-hosted event is a rare documented instance of this trilateral military contact.

Foreign Minister Cho Hyun and Canadian Foreign Minister Anita Anand agreed to expand energy supply chain resilience through increased Canadian crude and LNG imports, and to explore cooperation in defense, critical minerals, and AI. The call signals Seoul's continued effort to diversify supply chains and strengthen ties with like-minded partners.

In the markets. The KOSPI is at 6,803.90, down 1.98 percent. The dollar is at 1,338.38 won, down 0.4 percent. Brent crude is at 101.92 dollars a barrel, up 1.33 percent. The Bank of Korea's base rate is 2.50 percent.

Looking ahead. October 10: North Korean Workers' Party founding day — historically accompanied by military displays, missile tests, or KCNA propaganda output. October 14: KBO postseason begins in South Korea. October (next month): South Korean government plans to ship medical equipment to a North Korean hospital.
