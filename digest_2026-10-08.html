●Top Stories
US-Korea

Seoul recalls ambassador to Ukraine over North Korean POW disclosure dispute

South Korea recalled its ambassador to Ukraine after Kyiv publicly disclosed the transfer of two North Korean prisoners of war to Seoul, a move Foreign Minister Cho Hyun had warned would trigger firm measures. Ukraine's Foreign Ministry denied issuing an apology, with spokesperson Heorhii Tykhyi stating FM Andrii Sybiha only expressed 'regret' at differing interpretations of confidentiality arrangements — contradicting Seoul's characterization. The recall is the most serious ROK diplomatic protest action against Kyiv since Russia's 2022 invasion, and directly affects Seoul's posture on further cooperation with Ukraine on NK troop intelligence.

US-Korea

South Korea successfully test-launches first hypersonic glide vehicle, attended by President Lee

South Korea conducted the first successful test launch of its domestically developed hypersonic glide vehicle, with President Lee Jae-myung in attendance; Lee posted on X calling it proof that 'self-reliant defense is demonstrated through technology and capability,' and pledged continued investment in hypersonic propulsion, AI, and high-power laser systems. The test comes two days before the DPRK Workers' Party founding anniversary (Oct 10), with Kim Yo Jong having publicly claimed AI-guided evasion capability for DPRK's own hypersonic missiles on Oct 6. ROK's glide vehicle program represents a direct conventional counter to DPRK and Chinese missile threats in the context of the 2023–25 defense procurement surge.

Technology

Hacker used Chinese-developed AI tool to target South Korean banks, CrowdStrike says

CrowdStrike attributed AI-enabled cyberattacks on South Korean banks to a Chinese-speaking actor deploying a Chinese-developed AI agent — the first publicly attributed use of a Chinese AI tool in a state-linked financial sector attack on South Korea. The attacks swept Korean banking systems alongside Japanese businesses, per Nikkei reporting, adding a cross-border dimension to the incident. The NIS was separately excluded from a June trilateral US-Japan cyber coordination meeting on North Korean threats, raising questions about ROK integration into allied cyber defense at the moment the threat vector is widening.
